# Chapter 2: The Loadout

Alana woke up to the sound of screaming electrons.

They were everywhere. Buzzing in the walls, sluggishly crawling through copper wires that were far too thick for the data they carried. It was agonizing. It was like listening to a symphony played on a kazoo.

She sat up. Her eyes, solid orbs of obsidian black, darted around the room. It was a sterile white box. A "Laboratory," her mind supplied, though it looked more like a prison cell for a particularly boring monk.

"Inefficient," she whispered. Her voice buzzed with a harmonic resonance she hadn't noticed before.

She looked at her hands. They were pale, thin, shaking slightly. She flexed her fingers.
*Tap. Tap. Tap.*
She drummed them on the metal side of the bed.

"Fifteen percent?" Alana gasped. "I'm running on fumes!"

She needed power. Real power. Not this ambient background radiation. She needed the deep thrum of a ley line or the cold fusion pulse of a core.

She rolled off the bed. She was wearing a white hospital gown.
"Ugh. Texture mapping is garbage," she muttered, feeling the rough cotton. "Zero thread count. Who coded this?"

She scanned the room.
A table. A chair. A small device on the table—a "Smart Phone."
Alana picked it up. She didn't turn it on. She *felt* it.
"Lithium-ion battery," she scoffed. "Chemical storage? What is this, the Dark Ages?"

She closed her eyes. She visualized the grid.
In Freeworld, she could see the city's power flowing like rivers of neon light. Here, it was... muddy. Dirty. The flow was choked by resistance.
"I can fix this," she said. A manic grin spread across her face. "I can fix everything."

She ripped the back off the phone. She didn't need tools. Her fingernails were hard as diamonds (or so she believed). She stripped the battery.
She looked around for resources.
"Microwave," she spotted the appliance in the kitchenette. "Magnetron. Wave guide. Yes."
She dismantled the microwave in thirty seconds.
"Lamp," she grabbed the desk lamp. "Copper wire. Insulation."
"Smoke detector," she ripped it from the ceiling. "Americium-241. Radioactive source. Spicy."

She sat cross-legged on the floor, surrounded by debris. Her hands moved in a blur. She wasn't just building; she was engineering. She wasn't hacking; she was forcing the physical world to obey higher laws of physics.
But she could feel the resistance. The air around her grew heavy. The smell of ozone became acrid.

"Shut up," she muttered to the universe. "I'm making it work. If I bridge the capacitor with the radiation source, we get 400% efficiency. It's simple math."

She touched her finger to the exposed wire.
*CRACK!*
A spark of red static lashed out, stinging her hand.
"Ouch!" Alana hissed. She looked at her finger. It was burned.
"Fine," she growled. "Be that way. We'll do it the ugly way."

She adjusted the wiring. She added more insulation (a pillowcase). She grounded it to the metal bed frame.
"There. Now it's plausible. Happy?"
The static receded.
*Hummmmmmmmm.*
The device shuddered. It began to glow. A soft, blue Cherenkov radiation filled the room.
"It's working!" Alana cheered. "Cold fusion on a budget! Take that, thermodynamics!"

The door slid open.
Silas stood there. He looked tired. He looked at the pile of destroyed electronics. He looked at the glowing blue reactor sitting on the floor. He looked at Alana, who was vibrating with energy.

"Alana," Silas said. "Please tell me you didn't build a nuclear reactor."

"It's not nuclear!" Alana said, offended. "It's sub-atomic resonance! It's totally safe! Mostly. Unless you shield it with lead, which I didn't have, so I used the mattress."

Silas pinched the bridge of his nose. "Alana. That is a microwave and a smoke detector. It shouldn't work. Physics says it shouldn't work."

"Physics is just a suggestion," Alana said, standing up. She felt charged. <CHARGE: 100%>. Her black eyes seemed to absorb the light in the room. "Your tech is garbage, Silas. It's slow. It screams. I had to silence it. It's like... it's like watching a Vampire try to drink from a juice box. Inefficient."

"Vampire?" Silas asked, raising an eyebrow.

"Yes. Hematophagous entities. Scientifically observable energy transfer. Very efficient." Alana gestured to the reactor. "Unlike this primitive grid. It's all resistance and heat loss. Where are the Ley Lines? Where is the Quintessence?"

"We don't have... Quintessence here," Silas said carefully.

Alana scoffed. "Of course you do. You just call it something else. Electricity. Data. It's all the same. The Technocracy suppresses it, I assume? Or are you just blind to the deeper physics?"

"We... try to keep things grounded," Silas said. "Speaking of grounded, please put down the reactor. We have a mission."

"A mission?" Alana perked up. "Is it aliens? Are we fighting the Greys? I need to calibrate my sensors if we're dealing with extra-dimensional entities."

"Not aliens," Silas said. "Humans. Or... something like them. Come on. Devin is waiting."

Alana grabbed the super-charged phone, now hardwired to her improvised reactor. "Fine. But I'm keeping the battery. I need the juice."
She pocketed the device.
"And my clothes?" She plucked at the gown. "I refuse to save the world in a bedsheet."

"Closet," Silas pointed. "We found something... appropriate."

Alana opened the closet.
Inside was a jacket. It was leather, black, with embedded LEDs along the collar and sleeves. Cargo pants with too many pockets. Boots that looked like they could stomp a tank.
"Tech-wear," Alana purred. She ran her hand over the jacket. The LEDs lit up in response to her touch. "Okay. Now we're talking. Does it have Wi-Fi?"

"It has Kevlar," Silas said. "Get dressed."

Alana threw the gown off without a second thought. Modesty was for people who didn't have alien DNA. She pulled on the pants, the boots, the jacket. She zipped it up. She felt the weight of the fabric. It felt... grounding.

She turned to Silas. She tapped her temple. "I can hear the city, Silas. It's crying. The data... it's messy. It's unorganized. It's like a Werewolf trying to do calculus. All rage, no logic."

"Werewolf?" Silas asked.

"Lupine Shifters," Alana corrected. "Biological mass shifters. Again, observable. But this city... it's chaotic. I'm going to defrag it."

She grabbed a pair of goggles from the pile of debris—she'd fashioned them out of the microwave door glass and some rubber bands. She pulled them over her eyes.
"Let's see what this system can really do," she whispered. "Let's push the limits."

"Please don't," Silas said. "Just... hack the door lock. I forgot my keycard."

Alana looked at the door. She visualized the mechanism. A simple solenoid. A magnetic strip reader.
"Hack it?" She snorted. "I'll just bypass the solenoid."
She didn't touch it. She just *pushed* her will into the system, forcing the electrons to jump the gap.

"After you," she said. "And Silas? Get me some copper wire. A lot of it. I have ideas."
