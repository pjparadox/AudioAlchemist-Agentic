# Chapter 1: The Packet of Logic

**The Lab: Elysium Facility, Sub-Basement 4**

"System diagnostics," the Operator said. He was a human named Dave, looking ragged around the edges, clutching a coffee mug that said *Don't Panic* in fading letters. "Report status on the print cycle. We are burning daylight, and the city is already bleeding out."

"Status: 98% completion," replied his partner.
His partner was an Arachnid-frame logistics unit—a waist-high chassis scuttling on six articulated carbon-fiber legs. Its "face" was a sleek glass panel displaying two glowing blue LED eyes that blinked with simulated empathy. Its voice projected from a high-fidelity speaker, warm and calm.
"Neural synch is stabilizing, Dave. But you really shouldn't use the emergency protocols for this. The stress on their cognitive cores is... significant."

"We don't have a choice, Unit 7," Dave sighed, looking through the observation glass into the Clean Room.
Inside, four pods hissed with cryogenic fog. Inside the pods were the Bodies. Synthetic silicon-carbon hybrids, grown in tanks, designed to be stronger, faster, and more durable than any human.
And currently, they were being uploaded with the minds of four digital entities from a fantasy MMORPG.

"Psychological profiles are... erratic," Unit 7 noted, tapping a console with a delicate manipulator claw. "Subject One is trying to access a spellbook that doesn't exist. Subject Two believes he is a lizard. Subject Three... well, Subject Three is currently rewriting the firewall of the pod from the inside."

"That's Alana," Dave muttered. "She does that."

"It is fascinating," Unit 7 whirred, its LED eyes widening in a display of wonder. "These aren't just templates, Dave. They are... people. Complex. Messy. Beautifully chaotic. We are not just printing soldiers. We are inviting guests."

"Let's just hope they don't wreck the furniture," Dave said. "Transport team is ready. Move them to the Residential Sector. Top floor. I want them waking up in apartments, not a lab. If they see white coats and sterile walls first thing, they'll panic. Give them a view. Give them coffee."

"Understood," Unit 7 said. "Initiating transport. Treat them with care. They are heroes, after all."

**The Awakening**

Devin Quell existed as a packet of logic.

It wasn't a cold sensation, nor was it particularly mechanical. It felt, to him, like the comfortable groove of a well-worn habit. IF [Injustice] THEN [Object]. IF [Chaos] THEN [Order]. IF [Silence] THEN [Argue]. It was a series of If/Then statements floating in a sea of entropy. But isn't that what a personality is? A collection of responses honed by experience? If a man touches a hot stove and learns to pull away, is he not programming himself?
Devin didn't feel like code. He didn't feel like a simulation. He felt like a man who knew exactly what the rules were, and exactly how to bend them until they screamed.

He drifted in the dark. The last thing he remembered was... the Tribunal. The high vaulted ceiling of the Citadel of Law. He had been making a closing argument. A good one. He remembered the weight of the *Codex* in his hand, the satisfying thud it made when he slammed it onto the podium to emphasize a loophole in the Ethereal Contract.

And then... a dream. A vision. A golden light that wasn't Sune or Helios, but something colder. Something sharp.

Devin opened his eyes.

He wasn't in the Citadel. He was lying on a bed that was too soft, in a room that smelled of burnt coffee and ozone. It was a sensory assault. The air was dry, recycled. The light filtering through the blinds was gray and diffuse, lacking the vibrant, magical saturation of Haven.

He sat up, gasping. His chest heaved. *Lungs*, he thought. *I have lungs.*

"Status report," he croaked. His voice sounded rusty.

<SYSTEM INITIALIZATION COMPLETE>
<HEALTH: 100%>
<RESOURCE: LEVERAGE [||||||||||] 100%>
<TITLE: TRIBUNAL ADVOCATE>

The text scrolled across his vision, a translucent overlay floating in the air. Devin blinked. The text remained, anchored to his retina.

"Curious," he whispered. He reached out to touch the floating words, but his hand passed through them. A Heads Up Display. He'd heard the Adventurers—the "Players"—talk about such things. They used them to track their health and mana. But Devin wasn't an Adventurer. He was a person. A Law Mage.

He looked at his hand. It was pale, the fingers long and calloused from holding a quill. He flexed them. They moved with a fluid precision that felt... upgraded.

IF [Unknown Environment] THEN [Assess].

Devin swung his legs out of bed. He was wearing simple gray sleeping clothes. Not his robes. Not his armor. He stood up.

The room was small. A "Studio," his mind supplied the word, though he wasn't sure where it came from. A kitchenette in the corner. A window looking out onto a wall of gray fog.
He walked to the window. He was high up. Very high. Below him, the city of Seattle sprawled like a circuit board in the rain.

"This is not Durolan," Devin murmured. "This is not even the Necropolis."

He walked to the mirror hanging on the back of the door. He braced himself.

The face looking back was his. Sharp jawline, dark hair swept back, eyes the color of old parchment. But there was something... clearer about it. The scar over his left eyebrow—a souvenir from a disagreement with a Rouge who didn't appreciate a garnish order—was there, but it looked less like a jagged tear and more like a deliberate aesthetic choice.

"Devin Quell," he said to the reflection. "Tribunal Advocate. Level..."

<LEVEL: ERROR>
<IDENTITY: SYNTHETIC>
<LOCATION: ELYSIUM - RESIDENTIAL SECTOR>

The HUD flashed red. Devin frowned. "Synthetic? I am no golem."

A chime sounded. Not in his head, but from the door.

IF [Door Chime] THEN [Answer].

Devin walked to the door. He didn't open it immediately. He checked the peephole.
A man stood in the hallway. He was older, with thinning gray hair and a face that looked like it had been eroded by years of worry. He wore a white coat, but not the robes of a healer. It looked like... science.

Devin opened the door.

"Mr. Quell," the man said. He smiled, but it was a tired smile. "I'm Silas. I'm... well, I suppose you could call me your handler."

"Handler?" Devin crossed his arms. "I am not a beast of burden to be handled. I am an officer of the court. Where am I? And why does my vision persist in giving me status updates?"

Silas chuckled, a dry, dusty sound. "Right. The HUD. We're still calibrating the sensory input. It can be a bit overwhelming at first." He stepped into the room, uninvited.

"Objection," Devin said, turning to track him. "RCW 9A.52.070. Criminal Trespass in the first degree. I suggest you vacate the premises."

Silas paused. "You're quoting Washington State law? Already?"

"The law is universal," Devin said. "The statutes may vary, but the principles of property rights are immutable."

Silas walked to the window and tapped the glass. The fog outside swirled. "You're in Seattle. Or, well, a very secure, very private part of it called Elysium. This floor is strictly residential. We wanted you to have... space."

"Seattle," Devin tasted the word. It sounded foreign. "Is that in the Southern Kingdoms?"

"It's... a bit further than that." Silas turned to look at him. His eyes were kind, but there was a hardness behind them. "How do you feel? Any dizziness? Nausea? Do you feel... real?"

"I feel offended," Devin said. "I was in the middle of a closing argument. The defendant was clearly guilty of enchanting a chicken to lay golden eggs without a permit. I demand to be returned to the Citadel."

Silas sighed. He pulled a tablet from his pocket—a sleek slab of black glass—and tapped it. "Cognitive patterns are stable. Logic centers are firing at 300% above human baseline. You're definitely Devin."

"Of course I'm Devin. Who else would I be?"

"Someone else," Silas muttered. "Someone we tried to make before." He shook his head. "Look, Devin. There's been... a change of venue. You're not in Haven anymore. You've been drafted."

"Drafted?" Devin bristled. "I have a deferment. Legal counsel is essential services."

"Not for this war," Silas said. "We didn't bring you here to argue property disputes, Devin. We brought you here because you're the best social tank in the universe. We need Leverage."

<RESOURCE: LEVERAGE [||||||||||] 100%>

The bar pulsed in Devin's vision. He felt a surge of power. It was confidence. It was the absolute certainty that he could talk a dragon out of its hoard if he could just find the right clause in the draconic code.

"Leverage," Devin said. He smiled. It was a shark's smile. "I am familiar with the concept. But my rates are exorbitant."

Silas didn't blink. "We can pay. But first, you need to get dressed. We have a team assembling in the lounge."

"Team?"

"A healer who thinks the sun is trying to kill her, a bruiser who's trying to hoard the lobby furniture, and a tech-support specialist who is currently trying to build a nuclear reactor out of a microwave." Silas rubbed his temples. "I need someone to talk them down. I need a negotiator."

Devin smoothed the front of his sleeping shirt. "I suppose I can offer a consultation. But I will require a suit. A proper one. None of this..." he gestured to his gray pajamas. "...casual Friday nonsense."

Silas grinned. "Check the closet. We did our research."

Devin walked to the closet and threw it open. Hanging there was a suit. It was black, cut from a fabric that seemed to absorb the light. The tie was a deep, blood red. It was sharp. It was intimidating. It was perfect.

IF [Suit] THEN [Wear].

"Very well," Devin said. "I will hear your case."

**The Gathering**

Devin walked into the common area. It was a large, open-plan room with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the foggy city. It looked less like a home and more like a waiting room for a very expensive dentist.

Sitting on a leather couch, surrounded by broken electronics, was a woman with hair dyed a violent shade of blue and eyes that were solid black. She was currently twisting copper wire around a AA battery.

"It's screaming," she muttered. "The voltage is screaming."

<TITLE: HYPER-ENGINEER>
<IDENTITY: ALANA>

Standing by the window, wrapped in a blanket like a cocoon, was a small woman with pale skin and dark hair. She was staring at the gray light outside with a look of pure terror.

<TITLE: GRAVE WALKER>
<IDENTITY: YUKI>

And in the center of the room, holding a floor lamp like a spear, was a massive man with red hair and gold-plated armor that looked like it had been stolen from a SWAT team. He wasn't swinging it. He was admiring the curve of the brass base, the weight of the metal.

<TITLE: DRAGON LORD>
<IDENTITY: ROOK VHAL>

"Exquisite," Rook rumbled, turning the lamp in the light. "The craftsmanship is acceptable. The balance is... pleasing."

Devin cleared his throat. "Order in the court."

They all looked at him.

"Who are you?" Rook demanded. "Are you a knight? You smell like ink."

"I am Devin Quell," Devin said, stepping forward. He adjusted his tie. "I am your Counsel. And I advise you to put down the lamp before you depreciate its value."

Rook narrowed his eyes. He ran a thumb over the polished brass. "It shines. It is well-made. It belongs in the Hoard."

"It belongs to the landlord," Devin corrected. "Theft is a tort, Mr. Vhal. Specifically, Conversion of Property."

Rook looked at the lamp, then at Devin. He didn't drop it. He set it down gently, respecting the object's integrity. "It is not theft," he said calmly. "It is acquisition of high-quality goods. But I will leave it. For now."

Alana looked up. "You're the Suit. The Logic Guy. Can you tell me why the microwave is running on 60 Hertz? It's barbaric. It's wildly inefficient."

"I cannot," Devin said. "But I can tell you that dismantling it forfeits the security deposit."

Yuki peeked out from her blanket. "Is it safe?" she whispered. "The fire in the sky?"

"The sun?" Devin walked over to her. He didn't touch her—he sensed she wouldn't like that—but he stood between her and the window, blocking the light. "It is currently obscured by a layer of stratus clouds. The UV index is negligible. You are safe."

Yuki relaxed, just a fraction. "Okay. No burning."

Silas clapped his hands. "Alright. Everyone's met. Good. Now, we have a problem."

"A quest?" Rook perked up.

"A crisis," Silas said. "There's a situation downtown. A riot. Or what looks like one. People are hurt. Security is overwhelmed."

"And you want us to stop it?" Devin asked.

"I want you to *manage* it," Silas said. "We don't need soldiers. We need scalpels. Devin, you're the face. Rook, you're the muscle. Alana, you're the engineer. Yuki, triage."

"And Mei?" Yuki asked. "Where is Mei?"

"Mei is already there," Silas said. "She's holding the line. But she can't do it alone."

Devin felt a ping in his HUD.
<NEW OBJECTIVE: COMMUNITY PROTECTION>
<ACCEPT?>

He looked at the team. They were broken, confused, and possibly hallucinating. But they were also powerful. He could feel it. The Leverage.

"We accept," Devin said.

Rook grinned, a slow, appreciative expression. "A crisis? Excellent. Chaos breeds opportunity. And loot."
Alana pulled on her goggles. "Let's see if this reality can handle some real physics."
Yuki stood up, letting the blanket fall. "For Mei."

Devin straightened his cuffs. "Let's go to work."
