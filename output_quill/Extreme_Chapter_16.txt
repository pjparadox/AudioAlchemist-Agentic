**[INTERSTITIAL DOCUMENT: DEFENSE EXHIBIT 14]**

**DOCUMENT:** Prescription Pad (Dr. Mueller, General Practitioner / Dr. Vogel, Neurologist).
**DATES:** October 1976 - January 1977.

The document was projected onto the screen in the courtroom. It was a mosaic of slips of paper, taped together, creating a timeline of chemistry.
Erin Bruner stood next to the screen. She held a laser pointer. The red dot was steady.
"This," she said to the jury, "is a map."
She pointed to the first slip.
**DATE:** Oct 14, 1976.
**RX:** *Gambrolic (Carbamazepine).*
**DOSE:** *200mg b.i.d. (Twice daily).*
**NOTES:** *Initial diagnosis: TLE. Patient reports anxiety.*

"This is where it started," Erin said. "A standard dose. Therapeutic. Safe."
She moved the dot down.
**DATE:** Nov 01, 1976.
**RX:** *Gambrolic.*
**DOSE:** *400mg b.i.d.*
**NOTES:** *Patient reports breakthrough seizures. Visual hallucinations persisting. Increase dosage.*

"Two weeks later," Erin said. "Doubled the dose. Why? Because the 'cure' wasn't working. The 'sickness' was fighting back."
She moved the dot again.
**DATE:** Nov 15, 1976.
**RX:** *Gambrolic.*
**DOSE:** *600mg b.i.d.*
**ADDITIONAL RX:** *Diazepam (Valium) 10mg PRN (As needed for panic).*
**NOTES:** *Patient agitated. Combativeness. Claims drugs are 'poison'.*

"Now we have a cocktail," Erin said. "We have an anticonvulsant to stop the brain from firing, and a sedative to stop the patient from caring."
She looked at Juror 11 (The Nurse). The nurse was frowning. She knew what that much Gambrolic did to a liver. She knew what it did to a mind.
Erin moved the dot to the final slip.
**DATE:** Dec 20, 1976.
**RX:** *Haldol (Haloperidol).*
**DOSE:** *5mg.*
**NOTES:** * psychotic break. Patient claims to be 'Legion'. Sedation required.*

"Haldol," Erin whispered. "An antipsychotic. A chemical sledgehammer."
She turned to the jury.
"Look at the timeline. October to December. Two months."
She pointed to the margin of the exhibit.
There were handwritten notes. Dr. Vogel's handwriting. Small, cramped.
*Patient resistance increasing.*
*Family interference?*
*Priest involved.*

Erin highlighted the last note.
*Priest involved.*
"Dr. Vogel saw Father Moore as a variable," Erin said. "A complication. An interference."
She turned off the laser pointer.
"But look at the dosages. 200. 400. 600. Haldol."
She walked to the defense table.
"They weren't treating a disease. They were trying to drown a fire. And every time they poured more water on it... the fire got hotter."
She picked up the photo of Emily—the one from the hospital, where she looked grey and lost.
"Dr. Vogel calls this 'titration'. He calls it 'standard of care'."
She looked at Father Moore.
"Father Moore called it what it was."
She dropped the photo on the table.
"Suffocation."
