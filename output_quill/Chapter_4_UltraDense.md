# CHAPTER 4: THE AVENGER PITCH (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: EARTH-616 (THE SACRED TIMELINE)**
**DATE: MARCH 14, 2018**
**LOCATION: STARK TOWER**

The elevator ride was a lesson in physics and anxiety.

I could feel the G-force pressing down on me, a gentle hand pushing me toward the floor. But the anxiety? The anxiety was a boot on my neck.

It was quiet. Not the good kind of quiet. Not the "we just saved the world and are enjoying the silence before the shawarma" quiet. It was the "I am about to ask the coolest kid in school to prom and I am wearing a diaper made of bees" kind of quiet.

I checked my watch.

It was a Hello Kitty watch. Pink strap. Rhinestones. It had been a gift from a blind woman who thought it was a Rolex.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

The sound was deafening in the brushed-steel box. It sounded like a bomb timer.

*Okay, Wade. Focus. You are Wade Wilson. You are a special forces operative. You have killed dictators with a Zamboni. You have regenerated from a pile of ash. You can do this.*

I adjusted my tie.

I wasn't wearing the suit. I was wearing *the* suit.

No, not the red one. The red one smells like blood and chimichangas. This was a business suit. Charcoal grey. Sharp lapels. A silk tie that cost more than my first car. I looked like I was about to defend a mob boss in court, or perhaps sell a very aggressive timeshare.

The doors opened.

*Ding.*

Stark Tower. Or Avengers Tower. Or whatever they call it this week. The branding changes every time the toys stop selling.

The lobby hit me with a wall of sensory information.

**Smell:** Money. It smelled of ozone, polished marble, and vibranium. It smelled like Elon Musk's cologne, if Elon Musk actually showered and didn't smell like musk (ironic, really).
**Sight:** Glass. Chrome. Light. Everything was reflective. I saw a hundred distorted Wades staring back at me from the walls, the floor, the ceiling.
**Sound:** A low, hum. The building itself was alive. Powered by an Arc Reactor. It buzzed with the energy of a thousand suns contained in a donut.

I walked to the reception desk.

The receptionist was a hologram? No. Just very shiny. She looked at me with eyes that had seen gods and aliens and was unimpressed by a guy in a charcoal suit.

"Wade Wilson," I said. My voice cracked. I cleared my throat. "Wade Wilson. Here to see Happy Hogan."

She didn't blink. "Do you have an appointment?"

"I have a destiny," I said. "And a referral. From Dopinder. He drives a taxi. He knows people. He drove Tony Stark once. Or maybe it was Tony Danza. Dopinder gets confused."

She sighed. It was a long, weary sigh. "Floor 42."

I got back in the elevator.

*Floor 42. The answer to life, the universe, and everything. That's a good sign. Unless it's a Douglas Adams reference, in which case the planet is about to be demolished to make way for a hyperspace bypass.*

The doors closed. The numbers climbed.

*38... 39... 40...*

My heart was hammering against my ribs. *Thump-thump-thump.* It felt like a prisoner trying to escape.

*Why am I nervous? I'm Deadpool. I don't get nervous. I get manic.*

*Because this matters,* the voice whispered. *Because if you get this, you're not just a mercenary anymore. You're a hero. You're an Avenger. You matter.*

*42.*

The doors opened.

And there he was.

Happy Hogan. Iron Man's best friend. The guy who drove the car in the movies. The guy who looked like he was constantly suffering from a low-grade migraine.

He was sitting behind a desk that was too big for the room. He was moving papers around. I suspect they were blank. Just props to make him look busy.

"Mr. Hogan!" I marched in. I put on the 'Confidence' mask. "Happy! Can I call you Happy? I feel like we're vibing. The energy in here? Electric."

Happy looked up.

He looked... annoyed. He looked like a man who had dealt with too many Skrulls before lunch.

"Mr. Wilson," Happy said. "You're late."

"I was saving a cat," I lied. "From a tree. It was a very tall tree. And a very fat cat. It had diabetes. I had to administer insulin. It was a whole thing."

"Sit down."

I sat.

The chair was leather. Expensive leather. It groaned under my weight. It was softer than any furniture I had ever owned.

"So," Happy said, looking at my resume. It was written in crayon. I had drawn a picture of me fighting a T-Rex on the back. "You want to join the Avengers."

"I don't just *want* to join," I said, leaning forward. "I *need* to join. I need to matter, Happy."

I looked him in the eye.

"I've done the solo thing. I've done the X-Men trainee thing—which, by the way, pays in exposure, which doesn't cover rent or ammo. I'm ready for the big leagues. I'm ready to step up."

Happy stared at me.

He didn't see the suit. He didn't see the tie. He saw the scars underneath. He saw the killer.

"You're a mercenary," Happy said. "You kill people for money."

"I *un-alive* bad guys for *justice*," I corrected. "And money. But mostly justice. Look, I have skills. I heal. I'm great with swords. I have a very high pain tolerance. And I'm funny. Every team need a funny guy. You have Tony, but he's... sarcastic. I'm *wacky*. I'm chaotic good."

"We don't need wacky," Happy said.

"What do you need?" I asked. Desperation crept into my voice. It tasted like bile. "I can be serious. Look at this face."

I took off my sunglasses.

Happy winced.

"Okay," I said. "Bad example. My face looks like a topographic map of Utah. But inside? Inside, I am Captain America. I am moral. I am good. I salute the flag. I recycle. I help old ladies cross the street, even when they don't want to go."

*Please,* I thought. *Please say yes. I need this. If I'm an Avenger, I matter. If I matter, I'm worthy of Vanessa. I'm worthy of a life.*

Happy sighed. He closed the folder.

"No," Happy said.

The word hung in the air.

It was a small word. Two letters. But it hit me with the force of a train.

"No?" I repeated.

"No," Happy said. "You're not Avengers material, Wade. You're... volatile. You're a liability. We save lives. You take them. We build things. You break them."

"I can change!" I stood up. "I can be non-lethal! I'll use beanbag rounds! I'll use stern language! I'll use interpretive dance! I'll be the mascot!"

"Go home, Wade," Happy said.

He looked at his computer. He was dismissing me. He was dismissing my entire existence.

"Go back to your life. Be a hero to the people who love you. That's enough."

*That's enough.*

The words echoed in the office.

*It's not enough. Being Wade isn't enough. Being a mercenary isn't enough.*

I stood there for a second.

I visualized jumping across the desk. I visualized hugging him. I visualized crying.

But I didn't do any of those things.

I put my sunglasses back on.

"Right," I said. "Home. Good advice. Thanks, Happy."

I walked out.

I walked back to the elevator.

I pressed the button for the lobby.

The doors closed.

I was alone again in the steel box.

I didn't cry. I didn't rage. I just stood there, watching the numbers go down.

*42... 41... 40...*

*Going down,* the voice in my head whispered. *All the way to the bottom.*

The reflection in the metal doors stared back at me. A man in a suit that didn't fit, leaving a job he didn't get, going back to a life that wasn't enough.

*Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

The Hello Kitty watch marched on. Counting down the seconds of my mediocrity.
