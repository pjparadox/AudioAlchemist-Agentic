# CHAPTER 15: THE SILENCE (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE GREY PLAINS**

The next day was a lesson in geology and boredom.

We drove across an endless grey plain. The landscape was flat, featureless, and infinite. It was like driving across the surface of a parking lot that spanned the entire universe.

The buggy—battered, scorched, and smelling of Sabretooth's blood—rattled over the hard-packed earth.

*Rattle-thump. Rattle-thump.*

The silence between us had changed.

Before, in the Honda Odyssey, the silence had been sharp. It was filled with knives. It was the silence of two animals waiting for an excuse to kill each other.

Now?

Now it was... heavy.

It was the silence of two soldiers who had survived a trench. We had killed together. We had bled together. We had eaten stale chimichangas together. That changes the frequency of the quiet.

I looked at Logan.

He was driving. His hands—knuckles scarred, fingernails black with oil—gripped the wheel with a relaxed competence. He wasn't fighting the vehicle anymore. He was piloting it.

"You know," I said, breaking the quiet because I am allergic to it. My voice sounded loud in the open air. "That decapitation last night? Solid 9 out of 10. The arc. The separation. The way the head rolled. It was cinema."

Logan didn't growl. He didn't tell me to shut up.

He just adjusted his grip on the wheel.

"You lost a point for the landing," I added. "You fell to your knees. A little melodramatic. Next time, stick the landing. Hands on hips. Chest out. Be the hero."

"Don't push it, Wade," he said.

His voice was rough, but the edge was gone. The growl had been replaced by a weary rumble.

"I'm just saying," I continued, leaning back in the seat and putting my feet on the dashboard. "We make a good team. Red and Yellow. Ketchup and Mustard. The Murder Twins. We could franchise this. *Deadpool & Wolverine: The Road Trip*. *Deadpool & Wolverine: Miami Nights*. *Deadpool & Wolverine: Tax Audit*."

"Stop," Logan said.

"Okay. Stopping."

I looked out the window.

The Void rolled by.

*Grey sand. Grey rock. Grey sky.*

It was sensory deprivation. It was a world without color. Without hope.

*We're never getting out of here,* the voice in my head whispered. It sounded like Stephen King reading a eulogy. *This is it. Purgatory. We drive until we run out of gas, and then we walk until we run out of legs, and then we sit until we turn into dust.*

I looked at the horizon.

I saw a shape. A ruin.

A giant, stone head. Half-buried in the sand.

It was Thor.

Not the Chris Hemsworth one. A comic-accurate one. Winged helmet. Square jaw. The stone eyes stared up at the purple sky with an expression of eternal shock.

"Look at that," I whispered. "Even the gods end up in the trash."

Logan glanced at it.

"Everything ends," he said.

"That's your catchphrase, isn't it?" I asked. "You're a real ray of sunshine, peanut. 'Everything ends.' 'I'm the best there is.' 'Go screw yourself.'"

He looked at me.

His eyes were yellow. Wolf eyes. But they weren't feral anymore. They were just... old.

"It's not a catchphrase," he said. "It's the truth."

He turned back to the road.

"We make a good team," I said softly.

I wasn't joking this time. I wasn't performing for the audience. I was just saying it.

Logan didn't answer immediately. He let the silence stretch. He let the engine roar fill the space.

"We're not a team," he said finally. "We're two idiots in a buggy."

"Two idiots who killed Sabretooth," I corrected. "Two idiots who are going to save the world."

He glanced at me.

For a second, the corner of his mouth twitched. A microscopic movement. A tectonic shift in his facial structure.

"Maybe," he said.

It wasn't a yes. But it wasn't a no.

And in the Void, "maybe" is as good as a promise.

We drove on. The sun (that pale, sickly eye) began to dip lower in the sky. The shadows lengthened, stretching out across the grey sand like long, black fingers grasping for purchase.

I closed my eyes.

I could still smell the burning flesh. I could still taste the copper of my own blood.

But I felt... calm.

For the first time in six years, I wasn't alone.
