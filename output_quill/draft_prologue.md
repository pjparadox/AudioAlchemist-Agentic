# PROLOGUE: BYE BYE BYE

The ground was frozen. Not just cold—*frozen*. Like the heart of a Disney executive when you ask for an R-rating.

I slammed the shovel into the North Dakota permafrost. It made a sound like a gunshot wrapped in a wet towel. *Thwack-crunch.* My shoulders burned. My back screamed. My healing factor was working overtime, knitting torn muscle fibers back together just so I could tear them again with the next swing.

*It’s been a while since I did any manual labor that didn't involve disposing of bodies. Actually, this involves a body. Just... reverse disposing. Exhuming? Is that the word? Sounds fancy. Like a perfume that smells like old people.*

I wiped sweat from my brow, smearing dirt across the mask. It smelled of pine needles, squirrel shit, and the looming specter of copyright infringement.

"Come on, Wolvie." I stabbed the earth again. "Don't be shy."

The grave of James Howlett. Logan. The Wolverine. The man who died saving a bunch of kids because he was just that kind of grumpy, self-sacrificing prick. I needed him. I needed the Anchor Being. I needed the guy who could help me save my world from getting pruned faster than a bonsai tree in a bored gardener's hands.

*Clang.*

Metal on metal. The sound vibrated up the shovel handle and rattled my teeth.

"Jackpot."

I tossed the shovel aside and fell to my knees, clawing at the dirt with gloved hands. I brushed away the soil until I saw it. The Adamantium coffin. Or, well, the box. Whatever.

"Okay, peanut. Time to wake up. We got work to do. We got a universe to save. We got—"

I pried the lid open. It shrieked, rusted hinges protesting the disturbance.

I looked inside.

And screamed.

*Oh, come on. Really? You have got to be kidding me.*

It wasn't a sleeping Wolverine. It wasn't a coma-napping Logan ready to pop his claws and say "Bub."

It was bones. Just... bones.

Shiny, metallic, indestructible bones. Ribs like the grill of a '58 Plymouth. A skull that seemed to be grinning at me, mocking my desperate, timeline-hopping stupidity.

"You died?" I asked the skeleton. "You actually died? In *Logan*? I thought that was just... artistic license! Oscar bait! You're Wolverine! You regenerate! You don't just—" I poked the skull. "*Decompose.*"

I sat back on my heels, the cold seeping into my suit. *Well, Wade. You promised Vanessa. You promised the TVA guy—well, you headbutted him, but the sentiment was there. And now? Now you're sitting in a hole in the ground with a pile of very expensive scrap metal.*

"Freeze!"

The voice came from above. Mechanical. Amplified.

I looked up.

TVA Minutemen. A dozen of them. Standing on the rim of the grave, those little glow-sticks aimed right at my face. Pruning batons. The "Delete Key" of the multiverse.

"Wade Wilson." The lead agent raised his baton, the tip glowing with erasure energy. "Step away from the deceased Variant."

I looked at the agents. I looked at the skeleton.

*Okay. Plan B. Or maybe Plan C. I've lost track.*

I stood up, brushing dirt from my knees. "You guys. You picked a really bad time. I'm grieving here. This is my emotional support skeleton."

"Prune him," the agent ordered.

*Music cue. Something poppy. Something that says 'I'm about to murder all of you with a dead guy's limbs'.*

I reached down and grabbed the Adamantium femur. It was heavy. Dense. Balanced perfectly for blunt force trauma.

"Hit it," I whispered to the non-existent DJ in my head.

*BYE BYE BYE START*

I launched myself out of the grave.

The first agent didn't even see it coming. I swung the leg bone like a baseball bat. *CRACK.* His helmet shattered. The sound was wet—like dropping a melon on concrete. He folded, his body shutting down instantly.

*Ooh. That felt good. That felt... resonant.*

Two more rushed me. I ducked a glowing baton that sizzled the air where my ear used to be (before the cancer took it, anyway). I spun, driving the sharp end of the femur into the second guy's gut. The Adamantium didn't give. His armor did. He gasped, falling to his knees, wheezing like a pug with asthma.

*Left swing. Right swing. Pirouette.*

I was dancing. I was a beautiful, red-and-black ballerina of death, and my partner was a piece of the X-Men's most popular merchandise.

I jammed the skull onto a guy's head like a helmet, then punched it. *BONG.* He went down.

"I! Want! To! Be! With! You!" I sang, synchronizing the punches to the beat that only I could hear.

Another agent lunged. I grabbed Logan's claws—still attached to the hand bones—and *SNIKT*. I didn't have the muscles to pop them, but I could manually deploy the stabby bits.

I raked them across the agent's chest. Sparks flew. Armor parted like tissue paper. Blood sprayed, hot and copper-smelling in the freezing air. It misted on my mask. I tasted it through the fabric—salty, metallic.

*This is it. This is the stuff. The physics of violence. It's not clean. It's messy. It's exhausting.*

My breath was coming in ragged gasps. The suit was tight in the crotch. My heart hammered against my ribs like a prisoner demanding parole.

"I'm doing this for you, peanut!" I yelled at the scattered bones. "Don't think I don't appreciate the loan!"

I backflipped off a tree stump, landing on another agent's shoulders. I drove the leg bone down. *Crunch.* Collarbone. Goodbye.

The snow around the grave was turning red. A slushy, bloody mess. Steam rose from the fresh kills.

*God, I love this job.*

"Is that all of you?" I panted, standing amidst the carnage, holding the Adamantium claws in a dual-wield stance that looked cool as hell but was practically useless.

A portal opened. Orange. Rectangular.

More agents poured out.

*Oh, good. The sequel.*

I cracked my neck.

"Maximum effort." And then, because I couldn't help myself, I added, "And somebody check on the box office tracking. I think we just secured the opening weekend."
