# CHAPTER 2: A VERY WILSONS BIRTHDAY

The apartment smelled of cheap tequila, taco seasoning, and that specific, humid musk of too many bodies crammed into a space designed for two. It was a happy smell, theoretically. It was the smell of friendship. Of celebration. Of life continuing, despite everything.

Wade stood in the center of the room, holding a paper plate that was rapidly losing its structural integrity under the weight of a chimichanga.

"Surprise!" they had yelled.

And he had acted surprised. He had done the face—the widening of the eyes, the hand over the chest. He was good at acting. He'd been acting for years.

Blind Al was sitting on the couch, arguing with Peter about the viscosity of the guacamole.

"It's too chunky, Peter!" she shouted, waving a chip like a dagger. "It's like dipping a chip into a salad! I want paste! I want green sludge!"

"It's rustic, Al!" Peter beamed, his mustache quivering with wholesome delight. "It's artisanal!"

Wade looked around the room.

There was Dopinder, chatting up a girl who looked terrified. There was Negasonic, looking bored and fierce, tapping away on her phone like she was ordering a drone strike on the party. There was Yukio, radiating pink energy and genuine joy. There was Colossus, stooping to avoid hitting his head on the ceiling fan, holding a tiny red cup that looked like a thimble in his metallic hand.

And there was Vanessa.

She was by the window, laughing at something Buck said. The light from the streetlamp outside caught her hair, turning it into a halo of dark silk. She looked... radiant. Normal. Happy.

Wade felt a crack in his chest. A fissure running right down the center of his sternum.

*You don't belong here,* the thought whispered. It sounded like Stephen King reading an audiobook of his own suicide note. *Look at them. They're real. They're solid. You? You're a cartoon character drawn by a shaky hand.*

He took a bite of the chimichanga. It was cold in the middle.

"Wade!"

Vanessa saw him. She waved him over.

He walked toward her. The floorboards creaked under his feet—a sound he knew well. *Eeee-errrk.* The sound of home. Or at least, the sound of the place where he kept his stuff.

"Hey." He slid his arm around her waist. It felt natural, yet foreign. Like wearing a glove on the wrong hand. "Great party. Who invited the giant metal Russian? I thought we had a weight limit on the floor joists."

"Be nice." She poked him in the ribs. "He brought vodka. Good vodka. Not the stuff you buy in plastic jugs."

Wade shrugged. "I like the plastic jugs. They bounce when you drop them."

She looked at him. Really looked at him. Her eyes were dark pools, deep enough to drown in. "Are you okay, Wade?"

"Me? I'm fantastic. I'm the birthday boy. I'm... I'm just soaking it in."

"You seem... quiet."

"Quiet is my middle name. Wade Quiet Wilson. It's on my birth certificate. Or it would be, if I hadn't used it to light a cigar in 1998."

She touched his cheek. Her hand was warm. His skin—scarred, lumpy, hideous—burned under her touch. Not with pain, but with shame.

*She sees you,* the voice narrated. *She sees the monster under the wig. She sees the tumor with legs.*

She leaned closer, her breath warm against his ear. "I miss you."

Wade squeezed her waist. "I'm right here."

"Are you?"

The question hung in the air, heavier than Colossus.

Wade opened his mouth to answer with a joke. A deflection. A reference to *The Great British Bake Off*. But the words died in his throat.

Because she was right. He wasn't there. He was somewhere else. He was back in the grave, digging up bones. He was back in the dealership, selling lies. He was floating in the space between panels, waiting for the writer to decide what happened next.

"I'm trying," he said, and for the first time all night, it was the truth. "I'm trying so hard, Ness."

"I know," she said. She leaned in and kissed him. It was soft. Sweet. And it tasted like goodbye.

Someone knocked on the door.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Three sharp raps. Precise. Authoritative. Not the knock of a neighbor complaining about the noise. Not the knock of a pizza delivery guy.

This was the knock of Plot.

The room went quiet.

"I'll get it." Wade pulled away from Vanessa. He felt a sudden chill, a premonition that prickled the hairs on the back of his neck (the few that were left).

He walked to the door. He put his hand on the knob. The metal was cold.

*Don't open it,* the voice said. *If you open it, the genre changes. If you open it, the horror starts.*

Wade opened the door.
