# CHAPTER 2: THE MASK THAT DOESN'T FIT (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: EARTH-10005 (SIX YEARS LATER)**
**LOCATION: WILSON MOTORS**

The wig didn't just itch. It conspired.

It sat on my head like a dead animal that was slowly waking up. The mesh lining was a grid of sandpaper, digging into the keloid scars of my scalp with a persistence that felt personal. It was a torture device sold as a hairpiece, a Toupee of Damocles that threatened to slide off at the first sign of sudden movement or moderate humidity.

I stood in the center of the showroom.

Wilson Motors.

The name itself was a joke. A cosmic pun. Wade Wilson. Wilson Motors. It sounded like a front for a money-laundering operation run by a guy who watched too much *Breaking Bad*. But it wasn't. It was real. It was aggressively, banally real.

The air in the room was stagnant. It was a cocktail of scents that no amount of air conditioning could scrub away:
*   **Top Note:** Industrial floor wax. Lemon-scented chemical warfare.
*   **Middle Note:** Stale coffee. Burnt. Acrid. The kind of coffee that hates you for drinking it.
*   **Base Note:** The musk of desperation. The specific, pheromonal stink of men who work on commission and haven't hit their quota in three months.

"So," the customer said.

He was a man named Gary (not my boss, a different Gary, let's call him Customer-Gary). He was forty-two. He wore a polo shirt tucked into khaki shorts that were aggressively beige. He had the calves of a man who owned a Peloton but never turned it on.

"Does it have the... you know?" Customer-Gary asked.

He made a hand gesture. A vague, circular wave that could have meant anything from "heated seats" to "a button that launches nuclear missiles."

I knew what he meant. They always meant the same thing.

"The sliding doors?" I asked.

My voice sounded wrong.

It was smooth. Polished. It lacked the grit, the gravel, the beautiful chaotic rasp that usually accompanied a katana swing. It was the voice of a man who owned a steam mop. It was the voice of Wade Wilson, Regional Sales Associate (Bronze Tier).

"Yeah," Customer-Gary said. "The kids, you know? They kick the doors."

"Sir," I said. I patted the hood of the white Kia Carnival.

*Thump.*

The sound was hollow. Tinny. It echoed in the cavernous showroom like a drumbeat of mediocrity.

"Let me tell you about the Carnival," I said. "This isn't just a minivan. This is a fortress of solitude on wheels. It has automatic smart-power sliding doors. You approach the vehicle with the key fob in your pocket? Boom. Sesame opens. No hands required."

I leaned in. I lowered my voice to a conspiratorial whisper.

"Because let's be honest, Gary. Your hands are full. Groceries. Screaming toddlers. The crushing weight of the choices you made in your twenties when you thought you were going to be a rock star but ended up in middle management."

Gary laughed.

It was a dry sound. Like stepping on a dead leaf.

"Funny guy," Gary said. "You're a funny guy, Wade."

"I try," I said.

My face hurt.

It was the smile. I had been smiling for six hours. My zygomaticus major muscles were trembling from the exertion. Under the mask of my face—not the red mask, the human mask—my skin felt tight.

*Kill him,* the voice in my head whispered. *Just a little bit. Use the stapler on his desk. It’s right there. Swingline. Red. Heavy.*

I visualized it.

*The Fantasy:*
I grab the stapler. I vault over the hood of the Kia. I drive the stapler into Gary's forehead. *KA-CHUNK.*
"That's for the khaki shorts!" I scream.
I rip off the wig. I throw it into the ceiling fan.
I steal the Kia. I drive it through the plate glass window. *CRASH.*
I am free. I am chaos.

*The Reality:*
"It also has Apple CarPlay," I said. "Wireless."

"Nice," Gary said. "Does it come in blue?"

"It comes in 'Ceramic Silver'," I said. "Which is grey. Everything is grey, Gary. It's the color of the soul."

"I'll take the grey," Gary said.

I processed the paperwork.

It took forty minutes. Forty minutes of typing. Forty minutes of listening to the dot-matrix printer scream as it birthed another contract.

*Reee-errrr-reeee-errrr.*

When Gary left, I walked to the window.

It was raining outside.

Not a dramatic storm. Not the kind of rain you fight a supervillain in. This was a drizzle. A grey, miserable mist that coated the world in a layer of grime. The parking lot was a sea of wet asphalt and unsold inventory. Rows and rows of cars, staring at me with dead headlights.

I looked at my reflection in the glass.

Wade Wilson. Toupee (slightly askew). Beige suit (polyester blend, flammable). Name tag (crooked).

I looked... fine. Normal.

But underneath?

I rubbed my wrist. The bone ached. It was a dull throb, a reminder of the Adamantium handcuffs I wasn't wearing, the katanas I wasn't holding.

My skin was a map of pain. Under the cheap suit, my body was a topographic representation of a cancer ward. Lumpy. Scarred. It looked like an avocado had sex with an older, uglier avocado.

And under the skin?

*Empty.*

There was a hole in me. A void. And I was filling it with Honda Civics and lies.

"Wilson!"

The shout came from the manager's office.

Gary. My boss. (Yes, everyone is named Gary today. It's a glitch in the simulation).

I turned.

"Gary! My man! Looking... adequate today. Very symmetrical."

Gary sat behind his desk. He looked tired. He had bags under his eyes that could carry the weekly shopping.

"The upsells, Wilson," Gary sighed. He rubbed his temples. "You sold the Carnival, but you didn't push the rust protection. Rust is the enemy."

"Rust is nature, Gary," I said. "Entropy. Dust to dust. Rust to rust. Who are we to fight God?"

"We are a dealership, Wilson," Gary said. "We fight God for margin. Just sell the coating."

"Yes, sir."

I walked back to my desk.

I sat down. The chair squeaked. *Eeee-errrk.*

I opened my drawer.

Inside, hidden under a stack of brochures for the 2024 Sorento and a bag of stale pretzels, was a polaroid.

Vanessa.

She was smiling. It wasn't a "customer service" smile. It was real. It reached her eyes.

I touched the photo. My finger shook.

*I promised her,* I thought. *No more chaos. No more masks. Just Wade. Just this.*

I closed the drawer.

I looked around the office.

At the next desk, Peter was on the phone.

Peter. My best friend. My only friend who didn't try to kill me or sleep with me.

"No, ma'am, the floormats are extra," Peter said. He sounded so sincere. "But they are high-quality rubber. They capture... spills."

Peter was happy. He had a locker full of photos of his cats. He had a sugar glider named Muffin. He had a life. He had content.

I had a wig and a lie.

*I am the B-story of my own life,* I realized. *I am the comic relief in a tragedy that nobody is watching.*

I stood up.

"I'm taking a break," I announced.

"You just got here," Gary called out.

"Diarrhea," I said loudly. "Explosive. Unpredictable. Like the third act of a superhero movie. You don't want to be in the splash zone, Gary."

I walked to the bathroom.

I locked the door.

The bathroom smelled of bleach and something that had died in the ventilation system.

I leaned against the sink. I stared at myself in the mirror.

"You're doing great," I told the reflection. "You're nailing it. You're a productive member of society. You have a 401k. You have a dental plan."

The reflection stared back. The eyes were dead. Blue chips of ice in a melted face.

"Liar," the reflection whispered.

I gripped the sink. The porcelain was cold.

I wanted to scream. I wanted to peel the face off. I wanted to reach into the mirror and strangle the man in the beige suit.

But I didn't.

I straightened my tie. I adjusted my wig.

I flushed the toilet (for sound effects).

I unlocked the door and walked back out into the grey.
