# ACT II: THE VOID

# CHAPTER 7: THE TRASH COMPACTOR

**LOCATION: THE VOID**
**TIMELINE: NULL**

*Side Story D: The Pruned Nobody*

**POV: STANLEY (VARIANT 442-B)**

Stanley was not a superhero. He was not a villain. He was a junior accountant from a timeline where humans had evolved to have slightly larger thumbs.

He had been pruned because he filed a tax return 0.004 seconds late, creating a butterfly effect that eventually led to the heat death of his universe.

Now, Stanley lived in a refrigerator.

It was a sturdy refrigerator. A 1950s Smeg. Lead-lined. It was his home, his fortress, and his coffin.

He peeked out through a crack in the door.

The Void stretched out before him. It was a desert of rust and bone. The sky was the color of a bruise that wouldn't heal. And above it all, *It* prowled.

Alioth.

The cloud beast. The smoke monster. The great eraser.

Stanley watched as a new shipment arrived from the sky. A portal opened—a jagged orange tear in the fabric of the grey. Debris rained down. A tank. A billboard for a movie called *Green Lantern 2*. A man in a red suit.

The man hit the sand with a wet *thud*.

"Ouch," the man yelled. He stood up. He brushed dirt off his spandex. "Okay! Who ordered the existential dread with a side of sand?"

Stanley pulled the refrigerator door shut.

*Don't engage,* he told himself. *New arrivals are loud. Loud things get eaten.*

He huddled in the dark, clutching his most prized possession: a stapler that still had staples in it.

*Just survive,* Stanley thought. *Just wait for the end.*

Outside, the man in red started singing.

"I'm a Barbie girl! In a Barbie wooooorld!"

Stanley sighed. *He's going to die so fast.*

**POV: WADE WILSON**

The Void didn't just smell like trash. It smelled like *old* trash. It smelled like the back of a Spencer's Gifts that had been set on fire and then urinated on by a kaiju.

I stood up. My back popped. *Crack-crack-pop.*

"Hello?" I called out. "Is this the afterlife? Because if it is, I have some notes on the decor. Very 'Mad Max' meets 'Hoarders'."

No answer. Just the wind, howling through the wreckage of a giant 20th Century Fox logo.

*I know that logo,* I thought. *I used to work for those guys. Before the merger. Before the Mouse bought the house.*

I looked around.

Sand. Ruins. And... him.

Logan.

He was lying face down in the dirt, a few yards away. He looked like a pile of dirty laundry that someone had thrown out of a moving car.

"Hey!" I jogged over. "Wakey wakey, eggs and bakey! And by bakey, I mean scorching radioactive heat!"

I kicked his boot.

"Get up, peanut. We have a world to save. Or at least, I do. You just have a liver to punish."

Logan groaned. He rolled over.

His face was a mess. Mud caked in his beard. Blood—dark and sticky—matting his hair. He looked at me with eyes that were yellow and bloodshot.

"Where..." he rasped. His voice sounded like gravel in a blender. "Where are we?"

"The Void," I said, sweeping my arm wide. "The end of the line. The cosmic garbage chute. Welcome to the rest of your life. Which, statistically speaking, is probably about fifteen minutes."

Logan sat up. He looked at the sky. He saw Alioth—the purple storm cloud on the horizon.

"What is that?"

"That's the dog," I said. "Don't pet him. He bites. And by bites, I mean he consumes your atomic structure and turns you into purple poop."

Logan stood up. He swayed. He was drunk. Not 'fun party' drunk. 'I want to forget my own name' drunk.

"You shot me," Logan said.

It wasn't a question. It was an accusation. A statement of fact.

"I did," I admitted. "In the head. Point blank. It was very *Of Mice and Men*. 'Tell me about the rabbits, Logan.' *Bang.*"

Logan popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

They came out slow. Reluctant. Even his metal was tired.

"I'm going to kill you," Logan said.

"Get in line," I said. "Take a number. It's a long list. My ex-landlord. My ex-girlfriend's cat. The entire writing staff of *X-Men Origins*."

He lunged.

It was clumsy. I sidestepped. He stumbled past me, slashing the air.

"Woah, easy!" I grabbed his arm. "You're wasted, Wolvie. You're fighting at 10% capacity. You're the Windows Vista of Wolverines."

He shoved me away. "Don't touch me."

He stumbled toward the horizon.

"Where are you going?" I asked.

"Away," he said. "Away from you."

"There is no away!" I shouted. "We're in a void! It's infinite nothingness! That's literally the definition!"

He kept walking.

I watched him go. A lonely, yellow-and-blue figure trudging into the wasteland.

*He's broken,* the voice in my head whispered. *He's not the Anchor. He's a paperweight.*

*But he's all I've got.*

"Wait up!" I yelled, running after him. "I know a great bar! It's in the skull of a dead celestial! Happy hour starts at the end of time!"

# CHAPTER 8: A MAN-SHAPED SCAR

The bar was real.

Well, "bar" was a generous term. It was a hollowed-out fuselage of a crashed Helicarrier, propped up against the ribs of a giant skeleton. Someone had hung a sign that said "THE LAST CALL" in spray paint.

Inside, it smelled of kerosene and unwashed bodies.

Logan sat at a spool table, staring at a bottle of clear liquid that looked like lighter fluid.

"So," I said, sliding onto a crate opposite him. "Let's talk."

Logan didn't look up. "No."

"Come on. We're partners! We're a buddy cop movie! I'm the loose cannon, you're the grump who's 'too old for this shit'."

"I'm not your partner," Logan growled. "I'm your prisoner."

He took a swig of the lighter fluid. He didn't even wince.

"You're not a prisoner," I said. "You're a volunteer. A conscript. A..."

"I failed," Logan said.

The words were quiet. Heavy. They dropped onto the table like lead weights.

I stopped joking.

"What?"

Logan looked at me. His eyes were wet.

"My world," he said. "The X-Men. The humans. They're dead."

"I know," I said. "I saw the Wikipedia page. 'The Westchester Incident'. Sad stuff."

"You don't know," Logan snapped. "You don't know anything."

He leaned forward. The smell coming off him was intense. Stale beer. Old sweat. And under it all... iron. The smell of old blood that never washed off.

"I wasn't there," Logan whispered. "They called me. Scott. Jean. They called for help. And I was in a bar. I was drunk. I ignored the call."

He tapped the bottle.

"When I got back... it was over. The mansion was burning. The bodies..."

He shuddered. A tremor ran through his adamantium frame.

"I buried them," he said. "All of them. And then I walked away. And I kept walking. And I kept drinking. Until I ended up here."

He looked at me.

"So don't tell me I'm a hero," he spat. "I'm not a hero. I'm the reason they're dead. I'm a man-shaped scar."

I stared at him.

*Okay. Heavy. This is... real. This isn't a bit.*

*What do I do? Make a joke? Deflect?*

*No. Truth.*

"I get it," I said softly.

"You don't—"

"I do," I interrupted. "I really do. I wasn't there for Vanessa. I wasn't there when the bullet hit her. I could have stopped it. I didn't."

I touched my mask.

"We carry it," I said. "The guilt. It's part of the suit. It's the lining."

Logan looked at me. For a second, just a second, the hate in his eyes softened.

Then he blinked. The wall came back up.

"Go to hell," he said.

He stood up.

"I'm leaving."

"Leaving where?" I asked. "To the bathroom? Because it's a bucket in the corner. I checked."

"Anywhere," Logan said. "Anywhere you aren't."

He turned to leave.

"No," I said.

I stood up. I blocked his path.

"Get out of my way, Wade."

"No. I need you. My world needs you. And frankly? You need this. You need redemption."

"I don't want redemption," Logan roared. "I want a drink!"

He shoved me.

I flew back, crashing into the bar. Bottles shattered.

"Okay," I said, picking myself up. "Physical comedy. I can do that."

I drew my katanas.

"If you want to leave," I said, "you have to go through me. And I am very annoying to go through."

Logan popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

"Fine," he said.

# CHAPTER 9: THE HOSTAGE DEAL

**POV: WADE**

The fight in the Honda Odyssey (which we found parked outside, don't ask, it's a plot convenience) was cramped.

It’s hard to have an epic superhero duel in a minivan. There's no room for choreography. It's just elbows and stabbing.

*Beat 1: Mechanics.*
Logan lunged from the driver's seat. He drove his claws into my shoulder. *THUNK.* The metal slid between my clavicle and my scapula. It felt like being bitten by a steel shark.
I screamed. I jammed my katana handle into his eye socket.

*Beat 2: Consequence.*
Logan roared. He ripped the claws out—tearing muscle and suit fabric. Blood sprayed the dashboard. It coated the GPS screen, which was currently claiming we were in "Unknown Territory."
I headbutted him. My nose crunched against his forehead.
He bit my ear. Literally bit it off.

*Beat 3: Aftertaste.*
I tasted copper and earwax. The pain was blinding white hot flashes.
"That's my good ear!" I yelled. "The one I use to listen to Taylor Swift!"

We were tangling in the front seat. The van swerved. We hit a rock.

*CRASH.*

The van flipped.

The world spun. Dashboard. Roof. Sky. Dashboard. Roof. Sand.

We rolled.

When the world stopped moving, we were upside down.

I was hanging by my seatbelt. Logan was slumped against the roof (which was now the floor).

"Ow," I whispered.

Blood dripped from my nose into my eye.

"Are we there yet?" I asked.

Logan groaned. He popped his neck. *CRACK.*

"I hate you," he said.

"I know," I said. "It's the sexual tension. It's overwhelming."

I reached for the door handle. It fell off.

"Okay," I said. "New plan. We kick the window out."

We kicked. Together. *Thump. Thump. CRASH.*

Glass shattered. We crawled out into the sand.

We were battered. Bloody. Exhausted.

"Are we done?" I asked, lying on my back, staring at the purple sky.

Logan sat up. He spat out a tooth.

"For now," he said.

"Good," I said. "Because I think we have company."

I pointed.

A group of vehicles was approaching. Mad Max style. Spikes. Skulls. Leather.

"Scavengers," Logan growled.

"Or a fan club," I suggested. "Let's be optimistic."

# CHAPTER 10: THE MIND TOUCH

**POV: CASSANDRA NOVA**

*Side Story F: The Tasting*

Cassandra sat on her throne of bone.

It wasn't a metaphor. It was literally made of the femurs of a Celestial. It was quite comfortable, if you didn't mind the metaphysical hum of dead cosmic energy.

She closed her eyes.

She reached out with her mind.

The Void was loud. So many screaming voices. So much fear. It was a cacophony of terror.

But amidst the noise... a new flavor.

Two new flavors.

One was chaotic. Sour. Like rotting fruit wrapped in candy. A mind that buzzed with static and pop culture references. A mind that knew it was being watched.

*Wade Wilson,* she tasted. *The clown. The cancer.*

The other flavor...

She inhaled sharply.

It was deep. Earthy. Bitter. Like dark chocolate and old blood. It tasted of regret. It tasted of centuries of pain.

*Logan.*

She smiled. She licked her lips.

"Bring them to me," she whispered to her lieutenants. Pyro bowed. Sabretooth grinned.

"I want to taste them," she said. "Especially the heavy one. He has so many secrets. So many delicious traumas."

She opened her eyes. They were blue. Pale. Empty.

"It's dinner time," she said.
