# CHAPTER 17: THE CONFESSION (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE CAMPFIRE**

The fire popped. A single spark, orange and bright, flew up into the oppressive purple dark of the sky. I watched it rise, fade, and die. A tiny life, extinguished in a second.

I looked at him.

James Howlett. Logan. The Wolverine. The man whose face was on a lunchbox I owned in 2009.

He looked small.

He was sitting with his back against a rock, his knees drawn up. The firelight danced across his face, illuminating the deep lines around his eyes, the grey in his beard, the dirt smudged on his cheek.

He wasn't the legend. He wasn't the indestructible killing machine. He was just a man who was tired.

*He bleeds like it matters,* the voice in my head whispered. *He hurts like it's the first time, every time. I bleed like a punchline. I heal before I can learn the lesson.*

"I didn't just ignore the call," Logan said.

His voice was barely a whisper. It was rough, like gravel grinding against steel. It was a voice that hadn't spoken the truth in a very long time.

I froze. I stopped breathing. I stopped being Deadpool.

"I was... I was afraid."

The word hung in the air between us, heavier than the Adamantium in his bones.

*Afraid.*

Wolverine didn't get afraid. He got angry. He got drunk. He got stabbed. But afraid?

"Afraid of what?" I asked. My voice was soft. No quip. No deflection.

He looked at his hands. His knuckles were white, gripping his own knees like he was trying to hold himself together.

"I lose control, Wade. You know that. The Berserker Rage."

He unclenched his right hand. The claws were retracted, but I could see the skin stretched tight over the housings.

"It's not a superpower," he whispered. "It's a disease. It's a fire in my brain. When it starts... I don't see friends. I don't see family. I just see meat."

A tear leaked out of his eye. It cut a clean track through the grime on his face. It caught the firelight, shining like a diamond.

"I was afraid I'd hurt Jeannie. Or the kids. Or Scott."

He shuddered. A physical tremor that started in his shoulders and ran down his spine.

"So I stayed away. I went to the bar. I drank whiskey until I couldn't feel my hands. I told myself I was protecting them. I told myself I was the danger, and if I wasn't there, they'd be safe."

He sobbed.

It was an ugly sound. A racking, wet sound that seemed to tear itself out of his chest.

"But they needed me. The humans... Trask... they came with the new Sentinels. The quiet ones."

He looked up at me. His eyes were red-rimmed pools of agony.

"They slaughtered them, Wade. While I was staring at a bottle of cheap scotch. While I was feeling sorry for myself."

He wiped his nose with the back of his hand.

"I didn't kill them. But I let them die. Is there a difference?"

I stared at him.

The fire crackled. The wind howled. Alioth roared in the distance.

But in that circle of light, there was only the truth.

I wanted to make a joke. My brain was screaming for it. *Make a joke about daddy issues! Make a joke about alcoholism! Deflect! Deflect! The intimacy is burning your skin!*

But I couldn't.

Because he was right. And because I knew exactly how he felt.

I stood up. My knees popped.

I walked around the fire.

I sat down next to him. Not close enough to touch, but close enough to share the warmth. Shoulder to shoulder. Two broken monsters in the dark.

"No," I said. "There isn't a difference."

He flinched. He expected absolution. He expected me to say, *It's not your fault.*

But that would be a lie. And we were done with lies.

"You messed up," I said. "You failed. You let them down."

He looked at the ground.

"But you're here now."

"Does it matter?" he asked. His voice was hollow.

"It matters to me," I said. "And it matters to the kid we saved back at the camp. And it matters to the universe we're going to save."

I reached into my utility pouch. My fingers brushed against the cool, smooth surface of the photo.

I pulled it out.

Vanessa.

She was smiling. It was the photo I took the day we got the apartment. Before the cancer. Before the scars. Before I became a walking tumor with a mouth.

"We messed up, Logan. We both did. We failed the people we loved. We let them down. We weren't there when it mattered."

I held the picture over the fire.

The heat curled the edges. The emulsion bubbled.

"But we can't change the past. We can't uncork the bottle. We can't un-bury the bodies."

I looked at him.

"We can only change the ending."

I dropped the picture.

It fell into the heart of the flame.

Vanessa's smile turned brown, then black. The paper caught. *Whoosh.* It turned to ash.

It hurt. It hurt more than the rebar in my shoulder. It hurt more than the cancer.

But it was a clean hurt. A cauterizing hurt.

"So let's not fail the next ones," I said. "Let's save the world. Not because we're heroes. We suck at being heroes. But because we're the only ones left."

Logan watched the ash float up into the smoke.

He took a deep breath. A shuddering inhale that filled his lungs with the cold air of the Void.

He wiped his face. He sat up straighter.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay?"

"Okay. Let's save your damn world."

He stood up. He offered me a hand.

I looked at it. The hand that had killed thousands. The hand that had dug graves.

I took it.

His grip was strong. Anchor strong. It was a grip that could pull a timeline back from the brink.

"Maximum Effort?" he asked.

I smiled beneath my mask. A real smile. Not the salesman smile.

"Maximum Effort."
