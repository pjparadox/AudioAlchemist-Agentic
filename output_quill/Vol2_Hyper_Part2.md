# CHAPTER 10: THE MIND TOUCH

**LOCATION: THE BORDERLANDS**
**POV: CASSANDRA NOVA**

The mind is not a book. People say "read my mind," but they are wrong.

A mind is a meal.

Cassandra Nova sat on her throne. It was built from the ossified remains of a Celestial's thumb. Smooth, white bone, cool to the touch.

She ran a finger along the armrest.

"Bring them," she whispered.

Her voice didn't need to be loud. It carried on the psychic wind.

She closed her eyes and extended her senses.

The Void was a buffet.

There were the small snacks: the scavengers, the lost variants. Their minds were simple. Hunger. Fear. Greed. They tasted like stale popcorn.

Then there was Alioth. The cloud beast. His mind was a storm. A hurricane of hunger. He tasted of ozone and static. Too spicy.

But the new arrivals...

She licked her lips.

One was... chaotic.

*Wade Wilson.*

His mind was a funhouse mirror maze. Thoughts bounced and refracted. He knew he was in a story. He knew *she* was in a story. It was disorienting. It tasted like Pop Rocks and battery acid. A sour, fizzy explosion on the tongue.

*Deliciously annoying,* she thought.

But the other one.

*Logan.*

She inhaled deeply through her nose.

His mind was a cellar. Deep. Dark. Damp.

She pushed past the surface thoughts—the anger, the pain, the desire for whiskey. She went deeper.

She tasted the Adamantium. It was a cold, metallic tang. It tasted of violation. Of being cut open and filled with something that didn't belong.

She went deeper.

She tasted the memories.

*1845. A sickbed. A scream. Bone claws popping for the first time. The taste of a father's blood.*

*The wars. Civil. World 1. World 2. Vietnam. Mud. Blood. Jungle rot.*

*The Weapon X program. The tank. The wires. The feeling of drowning in green fluid.*

And then... the fresh wound.

*Westchester.*

It tasted of ash. Of burning wood. Of betrayal.

Cassandra shuddered. A wave of pleasure rolled through her.

"Oh," she whispered. "He is exquisitely broken."

She opened her eyes.

Her lieutenants—Pyro, Sabretooth, Toad—were watching her. They looked nervous. They knew that when she was hungry, anyone could be on the menu.

"The Wolverine," Cassandra said. "I want him alive. I want to peel him apart, layer by layer. I want to see what's at the center of all that pain."

She looked at Pyro.

"Burn the red one," she said. "He tastes like cheap plastic. But bring me the animal."

# CHAPTER 11: THE GAUNTLET - PART 1 (THE SETTLEMENT)

**LOCATION: THE RIB CAGE**

We parked the Odyssey behind a dune.

"Stealth mode," I whispered.

Logan looked at the bright gold minivan. "We're in a gold van, Wade. There is no stealth mode."

"It's camouflage," I argued. "We blend in with the... majestic gold sands of the Void."

"The sand is grey."

"Details."

We walked toward the settlement.

It was built inside the ribcage of a Giant-Man. The bones arched over the camp like the vaulted ceiling of a cathedral made of calcium.

The smell hit us first.

It was the smell of unwashed humanity concentrated in a small space. Sweat. Urine. Cooking meat (of dubious origin).

We moved through the crowd.

Variants. Hundreds of them.

I saw a Gambit. He was dealing cards to a Blob.
I saw a Daredevil (the Ben Affleck one). He looked sad.
I saw a Howard the Duck. He was roasting a rat on a stick.

"Keep your head down," Logan muttered.

He pulled his collar up. He was trying to hide, but you can't hide presence. He walked like a predator. People moved out of his way instinctively.

We found the fuel depot. A tanker truck guarded by a Toad variant.

"We need gas," Logan said.

Toad looked up. He spat a sunflower seed shell. "Trade?"

"I have a kidney," I offered. "It grows back. It's a renewable resource."

Toad ignored me. He looked at Logan.

"The jacket," Toad said. "Leather. Real leather."

Logan touched his jacket. It was old. Battered. It was the only thing he owned.

"No," Logan said.

"Then no gas."

I stepped forward. "Look, Kermit. We're on a mission from God. Or at least, a god-complex bureaucrat. We need the go-juice."

Toad smiled. It was wide and wet.

"There is another way," Toad said.

He pointed to a cage.

Inside, a boy. Glowing blue eyes. He was shivering.

"The Boss wants the kid," Toad said. "Cassandra. She collects... power sources. You take the kid to the Borderlands, I give you a full tank."

I looked at the kid. He looked like he was twelve. He looked like he knew exactly what was happening.

I looked at Logan.

His jaw tightened. A muscle jumped in his cheek.

*He's remembering,* I thought. *He's remembering the kids he didn't save.*

"We're not slavers," Logan growled.

"Then walk," Toad said.

We walked away.

"We take the fuel," Logan whispered when we were out of earshot.

"And the kid?"

Logan stopped. He looked at the massive white ribs curving above us.

"We can't save everyone, Wade."

"We can save *him*."

"If we start a fight, the whole camp comes down on us. We die. Your world dies."

"So we just leave him? To Cassandra? You know what she does to minds. You felt it."

Logan looked at his hands.

"I know."

"Then let's do something stupid," I said. "Let's be the X-Men."

He looked at me.

"I'm not an X-Man."

"You're wearing the suit," I pointed out. "Yellow and blue. The colors of 'I make bad decisions for the right reasons'."

He sighed. A long, weary exhale.

"I take the guards," he said. "You get the kid."

"Deal."

That night, we didn't just steal gas. We started a riot.

*The Raid.*

Logan moved like smoke. He took out the perimeter guards silently. *Snap. Crunch. Thud.* No claws. Just efficiency.

I crept to the cage.

"Hey, kid," I whispered. "You like road trips?"

The boy looked up. "Who are you?"

"I'm the fairy godmother," I said, picking the lock. "But with more cancer and guns."

The lock clicked.

*CLANG.*

I dropped the lock.

"Oops."

The camp woke up.

Toad screamed. "Get them!"

And then... chaos.

Mutants everywhere. Pyro blasting fire. A Quill guy shooting spikes.

Logan dropped the stealth. *SNIKT.*

He became a whirlwind. He cut through the mob. He wasn't killing them—mostly—he was just disabling them. A slash to the leg. A punch to the gut.

I grabbed the kid. "Run to the van! The gold one!"

We sprinted.

Fireballs exploded around us. The heat was intense. It singed my suit.

We made it to the van. Logan jumped in the driver's seat.

"Hold on!"

He gunned it. The Odyssey roared (or purred aggressively).

We smashed through the gate. We hit the open sand.

I looked back. The camp was a hornet's nest.

"We did it!" I yelled. "We saved the—"

*THUNK.*

An arrow hit the back window. It exploded.

"Why is it always the windows?" I complained. "Do you know how hard it is to get safety glass in the Void?"

The kid was huddled in the back seat. He was glowing.

"Thank you," he whispered.

Logan looked in the rearview mirror. He met the kid's eyes.

For a second, the hardness in Logan's face melted.

"Don't thank us yet," Logan said. "We're not safe."

# CHAPTER 12: THE GAUNTLET - PART 2 (THE RUINS)

**LOCATION: THE 20th CENTURY FOX LOT**

We dropped the kid off at a resistance outpost a few miles down the road. (Yes, there's a resistance. It's mostly just people who haven't been eaten yet).

We kept driving.

The landscape shifted. The organic rot gave way to concrete skeletons.

We stopped at a massive ruin half-buried in the sand.

I got out. I wiped the dust off a stone block.

*20th CENTURY FOX.*

The monolith. The fanfare structure. The symbol of my corporate origin.

I sat on the giant 'X'.

It was cold. The stone was rough, pitted by the Void winds.

"This is it," I said. "This is where I came from."

Logan stood next to me. He looked at the ruin.

"It's just rocks," he said.

"It's not just rocks," I said. "It's history. It's *X-Men*. It's *Daredevil*. It's *Elektra*! Okay, maybe we don't talk about *Elektra*. But it was home."

I looked at the 'F'. It was cracked.

"They bought it," I said. "Disney. They bought the whole thing. And then... they pruned it. They sent it all here."

I felt a genuine pang of grief. Not for a studio. But for the era. For the messy, weird, inconsistent movies that made me.

"Everything ends," Logan said.

He sat down next to me. He pulled out a cigar stub. He didn't light it. He just chewed on it.

"Does it have to?" I asked. "Why does it have to end? Why can't we just... keep going?"

"Because nothing lasts," Logan said. "Not studios. Not heroes. Not us."

He looked at the horizon. The purple sky was swirling.

"I miss them," I whispered. "Vanessa. Blind Al. Even Weasel. I miss my world. I don't want it to be just... rocks in a desert."

"Then fight for it," Logan said.

"I am," I said. "But I'm tired, Wolvie. I'm so tired. Being the funny guy... it's exhausting. It's heavy."

He looked at me.

He put a hand on my shoulder. It was heavy. Warm. It was the first time he had touched me without trying to kill me.

"Me too, kid," he said. "Me too."

We sat there for a long time. Two relics of a dead universe, sitting on the tombstone of the company that created us.

The wind howled. It sounded like the opening fanfare, played on a broken trumpet.

# CHAPTER 13: THE GAUNTLET - PART 3 (THE AMBUSH)

**LOCATION: THE CANYON**

The ambush happened in the canyon.

It was a text-book kill zone. High walls. Narrow choke point.

*BOOM.*

An explosion rocked the buggy (we had traded the Odyssey for a buggy at the resistance camp. Better suspension. Less legroom).

We flipped.

The world spun. Sky. Ground. Sky. Ground. Pain.

I crawled out of the wreckage. My leg was bent at a 90-degree angle. I snapped it back. *CRUNCH.*

"Motherfluffer!" I screamed.

"Wade!" Logan yelled.

I looked up.

Logan was pinned. The chassis of the buggy was on his chest. He was pushing, veins popping, but it was heavy.

And standing over him...

Sabretooth.

Not the Liev Schreiber one. The Tyler Mane one. The big one. The one who looked like a lion that ate a wrestler.

"Well, well," Sabretooth growled. His voice was deep, a rumble in the earth. "The runt."

"Victor," Logan wheezed.

"I've been waiting for this," Sabretooth said. "A long time."

He extended his claws. They were dirty. Yellow.

I tried to run. I tried to help.

But Pyro was there.

He stood on a ridge. He snapped his fingers.

*FWOOSH.*

A wall of fire cut me off.

"Stay back, freak," Pyro yelled.

I rolled. The heat singed my eyebrows.

I watched, helpless, as Sabretooth raised his hand.

"Time to die, Jimmy," Sabretooth grinned.

*Beat 1: Mechanics.*
Logan didn't try to lift the car. He stopped pushing. He let the weight settle.
Then, he exploded.
He popped his claws *through* the metal door of the buggy, stabbing upward into Sabretooth's foot.
Sabretooth howled. He stepped back, hopping.
Logan used the moment. He bench-pressed the chassis. *HNNNG.* He threw it off.
He stood up. He was bleeding from his chest. His suit was torn.

*Beat 2: Consequence.*
They collided.
It wasn't a martial arts fight. It was two animals trying to eat each other.
Claws met claws. *SPARK.*
Sabretooth bit Logan's shoulder. He tore a chunk of flesh out.
Logan didn't scream. He stabbed Sabretooth in the gut. Three claws deep.
They rolled in the dirt. Fur. Blood. Dust.

*Beat 3: Aftertaste.*
Logan got the upper hand.
He pinned Sabretooth.
He put his claws to Sabretooth's neck.
"Do it!" Sabretooth hissed. "Do it, runt!"
Logan didn't hesitate.
*SNICK-SLICE.*
He took the head off.
The head rolled. It stopped at my feet. Sabretooth's eyes were still open, frozen in a look of surprise.
Logan stood up. He was panting. Blood—his and Victor's—covered him.
He looked wild. He looked like the animal everyone said he was.
He looked at me.
"Logan?" I whispered.
He blinked. The animal receded. The man came back.
He fell to his knees.
"Let's go," he said. "Before I eat him."
