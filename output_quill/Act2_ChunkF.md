# CHAPTER 15: THE SILENCE

We drove for another day. The canyon opened up into a flat, grey plain.

The silence between us had changed. It wasn't the hostile silence of the bar. It wasn't the awkward silence of the Honda Odyssey. It was a companionable silence. The silence of two guys who had seen some shit and killed a giant cat-man.

"You know," I said, breaking the quiet because I am allergic to it. "We make a good team. Red and Yellow. Ketchup and Mustard."

Logan didn't snarl. He just adjusted his grip on the wheel. "Don't push it."

"I'm just saying. We could franchise this. *Deadpool & Wolverine: The Road Trip*. *Deadpool & Wolverine: Miami Nights*."

"Stop," Logan said.

"Okay. Stopping."

I looked out the window. The Void was endless. Just ruins and dust.

*We're never getting out of here,* the voice whispered. *This is it. Purgatory.*

But I looked at Logan. He was solid. He was real. And for the first time in a long time, I didn't feel alone.

# CHAPTER 16: THE CAMPFIRE (SETUP)

We stopped for the night near a cluster of rocks that offered some shelter from the wind.

We built a fire out of driftwood and pieces of a shattered "Stark Industries" sign.

The fire crackled. It was the only light in the universe.

I sat on a rock, sharpening my katanas. *Shhhk. Shhhk.* The sound was rhythmic. Meditative.

Logan was sitting opposite me, cleaning his claws. He used a rag he'd found in the buggy. He wiped the blood away with a tenderness that was surprising.

"Why do you wear the suit?" Logan asked.

I stopped sharpening. "What?"

"The suit," he said. "The yellow one. Why do you wear it? You didn't wear it in your timeline. You wore black leather. Like a biker."

Logan stopped cleaning. He stared at the fire.

"Scott," he said. "Scott wanted me to wear it."

"Cyclops?"

"Yeah. He said... he said the X-Men needed to be symbols. Bright. Hopeful. He said people needed to see us coming."

He laughed. A bitter sound.

"I told him he was an idiot. I told him I wasn't a billboard. I told him I wasn't a hero."

He looked at his claws.

"I never wore it. Not once. Until..."

He trailed off.

"Until the funeral?" I asked softly.

He nodded.

"I put it on," he whispered. "After they died. I put it on to bury them. Because... because I wanted them to know I was one of them. Finally."

He looked at me. The firelight danced in his eyes.

"But it was too late. They were dead. And I was just a clown in a yellow suit."

# CHAPTER 17: THE CONFESSION

*The Bestseller Moment*

The fire popped. A spark flew up into the dark.

I looked at him. The Wolverine. The legend.

He looked small. He looked broken.

*He bleeds like it matters. I bleed like a punchline.*

"I didn't just ignore the call," Logan said. His voice was barely a whisper. "I was... I was afraid."

I froze.

"Afraid?"

"I was afraid that if I went... I'd kill them."

He looked at his hands.

"I lose control, Wade. You know that. The Berserker Rage. It's not a superpower. It's a disease. I was afraid I'd hurt Jeannie. Or the kids."

Tears leaked out of his eyes. He didn't wipe them away.

"So I stayed away. I drank. I told myself I was protecting them by not being there."

He sobbed. A racking, ugly sound.

"But they needed me. The humans... they came with Sentinels. They slaughtered them. And I was safe in a bar, staring at a bottle."

He looked at me. His face was a mask of agony.

"I didn't kill them, Wade. But I let them die. Is there a difference?"

I stared at him.

I wanted to make a joke. I wanted to say something about how that's a classic trope. I wanted to diffuse the tension because the tension was crushing me.

But I couldn't.

Because he was right.

And because I knew exactly how he felt.

I stood up. I walked around the fire.

I sat down next to him.

I didn't hug him. He would have stabbed me.

I just sat there. Shoulder to shoulder.

"No," I said. "There isn't a difference. But you're here now."

"Does it matter?" he asked.

"It matters to me," I said. "And it matters to the kid we saved. And it matters to the universe we're going to save."

I pulled out the picture of Vanessa.

"We messed up, Logan. We both did. We failed the people we loved."

I put the picture in the fire.

It curled. It blackened. It turned to ash.

"So let's not fail the next ones," I said.

Logan watched the picture burn.

He took a deep breath. He wiped his face.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay?"

"Okay. Let's save your damn world."

He stood up. He offered me a hand.

I took it.

His grip was strong. Anchor strong.

"Maximum Effort?" he asked.

I smiled beneath my mask.

"Maximum Effort."
