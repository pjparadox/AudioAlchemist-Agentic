# CHAPTER 14: THE AFTERMATH (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE CAVE**

We didn't go far. The buggy was totaled (RIP Buggy, we hardly knew ye, and your suspension was terrible anyway), and Logan was leaking too much blood to walk miles.

We found a cave. It was a shallow gash in the rock face, sheltered from the wind but exposed to the cold.

It smelled of:
*   **Bat Guano:** Acrid. Ammonia-rich. The scent of a thousand tiny things digesting insects.
*   **Damp Stone:** Cold. Ancient. The smell of a basement that hasn't been opened in a century.
*   **Old Smoke:** Someone had been here before us. Maybe years ago. Maybe yesterday. Time is a flat circle here.

We built a fire. I used pieces of a shattered billboard ("Coca-Cola: Open Happiness") and dry scrub brush. It crackled, casting long, dancing shadows on the walls. The light turned our faces into flickering masks.

Logan sat by the entrance. He was staring out at the dark.

He was cleaning his claws.

He had found a rag in the wreckage. It was grey and greasy, but it was absorbent.

*Scrub. Scrub. Scrub.*

The sound of the cloth on the Adamantium was distinctive. It was a high-pitched *shhhk-shhhk*. It sounded like a knife being sharpened on a whetstone. It was a sound that set my teeth on edge.

I watched him.

His healing factor was working overtime. I could see the steam rising from his shoulder where Sabretooth had taken a bite. The flesh was knitting together, pink and raw, covering the gleaming silver bone underneath. It was a time-lapse of biology.

But his eyes...

His eyes weren't healing. They were fixed on a point in the distance that didn't exist. They were replaying the fight. The snap of the neck. The roll of the head.

"You okay?" I asked.

The question hung in the air, stupid and small. Like asking a burn victim if they wanted a heating pad.

"I killed my brother," he said.

His voice was rough. Gravel in a cement mixer.

"He was trying to kill you," I pointed out. "And me. And probably a lot of other people. He was a bad guy, Logan. A capital-B Bad Guy. He had yellow teeth and bad breath."

"He's still my brother."

He scrubbed a spot on his middle claw that wouldn't come clean. It was a stubborn smear of dried blood. Victor's blood.

"In my timeline," Logan said, "Victor and I... we fought for a century. Back to back. We were monsters together."

He looked at the fire. The flames reflected in his eyes, turning them orange.

"We survived the firing squads. We survived the wars. We survived everything because we had each other. We were the only ones who understood the hunger."

He swallowed.

"I thought... I thought maybe here, in the Void... maybe he'd be different. Maybe we could have..."

He trailed off.

"Redemption?" I offered.

"Peace," he corrected. "Just peace. A moment where we weren't trying to tear each other apart. A moment where we were just... brothers."

I moved closer to the fire. The warmth felt good on my skin, which was currently regenerating from second-degree burns (thanks, Pyro).

"There is no peace for guys like us, Wolvie. We're weapons. We're built for war. Peace is... it's a foreign language. We don't speak it. We try, but we just end up mispronouncing 'happiness' as 'violence'."

"I'm tired of war," he whispered. "I'm tired of the noise. I'm tired of the blood."

"Me too," I said. "But the war isn't over. Not yet. Cassandra is still out there. Paradox is still out there. My world is still dying."

I reached into my pouch.

"I have something," I said.

I pulled out a chimichanga. It was squashed flat. It looked like roadkill wrapped in a tortilla. It was cold.

"Want half?" I offered. "It's been in my pocket for... time is relative, so let's say 'a while'. But the preservatives in the tortilla alone could survive a nuclear winter."

He looked at the sad, flat burrito.

He looked at me.

For a second, I thought he was going to stab me. I braced for the *SNIKT*.

Then, he snorted. A short, sharp exhalation.

"No," he said.

"More for me."

I took a bite. It tasted of pocket lint, stale cumin, and friendship. It was delicious.

We ate in silence.

The fire crackled. Outside, Alioth roared in the distance—a reminder that we were just ants in a jar.

But inside the cave... it was quiet.

It was a grim night. We were battered. We were bloody. We were lost.

But we were alive. And for the first time since I dragged him out of that grave... we were on the same side.
