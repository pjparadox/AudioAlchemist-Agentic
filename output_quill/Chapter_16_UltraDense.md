# CHAPTER 16: THE CAMPFIRE (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE STONE CIRCLE**

We stopped for the night near a cluster of rocks that jutted out of the sand like broken teeth.

It wasn't a good campsite. It offered no real shelter from the wind, which had picked up and was now howling with a voice that sounded like a thousand lost souls screaming into a vacuum.

But we were tired. Even monsters need to sleep.

I gathered wood. Or, what passed for wood in the Void. Driftwood. Pieces of shattered crates. A sign that said "STARK INDUSTRIES" in faded blue letters.

"Iron Man makes good kindling," I muttered, tossing the sign onto the pile.

I struck a match.

The flame flared. Orange. Bright. It caught the dry wood with a hungry *whoosh*.

The fire crackled. It was the only light in the universe. It pushed back the purple dark, creating a small, flickering bubble of existence.

I sat on a rock, sharpening my katanas.

*Shhhk. Shhhk. Shhhk.*

The sound was rhythmic. Meditative. It was the sound of preparation.

Logan sat opposite me. He was sitting on the ground, his back against a stone. He looked... small.

The firelight danced across his face, illuminating the lines of exhaustion, the scars, the dirt. He wasn't the Wolverine. He was just a man who had lived too long.

He was cleaning his claws.

He had found a rag—part of a parachute, maybe—and he was wiping them down.

*Rub. Rub. Pause.*

He was gentle. Careful. He treated the metal like it was holy.

"Why do you wear the suit?" Logan asked suddenly.

His voice was quiet. It didn't have the growl. It was just a question.

I stopped sharpening. I looked up.

He was staring at my chest. At the red spandex.

"Because it hides the blood," I said. "Classic line. True story. Also, it makes my ass look fantastic. It's a dual-purpose garment. Tactical and fashionable."

"No," he said. He shook his head. "Why do *you* wear it? You could wear anything. You could be anyone. You regenerate. You don't need armor."

"It's who I am," I said. "It's the only thing that fits. Wade Wilson is... messy. Wade Wilson is a cancer patient with a skin condition. Deadpool? Deadpool is clean. Deadpool has a script. Deadpool is a brand."

I looked at him.

I looked at the yellow and blue. The bright, garish colors that screamed "Hero" in a world that was entirely grey.

"Why do *you* wear it?" I asked. "You never wore it in the movies. You hated it. You called it 'pyjamas'. You wore black leather. You looked like you were in a *Matrix* cover band."

Logan looked down. He touched the yellow fabric on his thigh. His fingers traced the black X on his belt.

"Scott," he said.

The name hung in the air.

"Cyclops?"

"Yeah. He said... he said the X-Men needed to be symbols. Bright. Hopeful. He said people needed to see us coming and know that help had arrived. He said we couldn't hide in the dark anymore."

He laughed.

It was a bitter, dry sound. Like stepping on dry leaves.

"I told him he was an idiot," Logan whispered. "I told him I wasn't a billboard. I told him I wasn't a hero. I told him to take his yellow spandex and shove it."

He looked at the fire. His eyes were wet.

"I never wore it. Not once. I kept it in a box. I laughed at it."

He swallowed. The fire popped, sending a spark spiraling up into the night.

"Until..."

He trailed off.

"Until the funeral?" I asked softly.

He nodded.

"I put it on," he whispered. "After they died. After I buried them. I put it on to stand over their graves. Because... because I wanted them to know I was one of them. Finally. I wanted them to know I was on the team."

He looked at me.

The pain in his eyes was absolute. It was a physical weight.

"But it was too late," he said. "They were dead. And I was just a clown in a yellow suit standing over a pile of dirt."

I stared at him.

The firelight cast long shadows behind him. He looked like a statue of grief.

*He bleeds like it matters,* I thought. *He hurts like it's the first time.*

I wanted to make a joke. I wanted to say something about how that was a very heavy backstory dump.

But I couldn't.

Because the silence was too loud. And for once, I didn't want to break it.
