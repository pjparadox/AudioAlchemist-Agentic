# ACT III: THE RIPPER

# CHAPTER 18: THE MACHINE

**LOCATION: TVA OUTPOST (VOID SECTOR)**

The outpost didn't belong here.

In the Void, everything was organic in its decay. Rust was a fungus; rot was a smell. But the TVA outpost was a blister of sterility. It was white plastic and orange glass, slammed into the grey bedrock of the wasteland like a syringe into a bruise.

We crouched on a ridge, looking down.

"It's quiet," Logan said.

"It's too quiet," I added. "It's 'we blew the budget on the final fight so we can't afford extras' quiet."

The wind shifted.

It hit me then. The smell.

It wasn't just ozone. It was the smell of *un-happening*.

Have you ever smelled a lightning strike? It tastes like copper and burnt hair. Now imagine that lightning strike is hitting history itself. It smelled like the library of Alexandria burning. It smelled like your first pet dying. It smelled like every mistake you ever made, cooked down into a gas.

"The Time Ripper," I whispered.

"What?"

"That hum. Can you feel it?"

Logan nodded. He touched his chest.

"It feels like..." he struggled for the word. "Like I'm being erased."

"It's vibrating the atoms," I said. "It's unzipping the DNA of the universe."

We moved closer.

The ground around the outpost was glassed. Smooth. Obsidian. The heat from the Ripper's exhaust vents had turned the sand into a mirror.

We walked on our reflections. Two distorted monsters approaching the castle.

"How do we get in?" Logan asked.

"We knock," I said. "And by knock, I mean we drive a Honda Odyssey through the front door."

"We don't have the Odyssey anymore."

"Right. We wrecked it. Okay. Plan B. We use the element of surprise."

"Which is?"

"We walk in the front door and look like we belong."

"You're wearing a red spandex suit covered in blood and dog hair," Logan noted. "You don't belong anywhere."

"Ouch. Fair. But hurtful."

Suddenly, the ground shook.

*THRUM.*

A beam of purple light shot up from the center of the outpost. It pierced the clouds. Alioth roared—a sound like a thousand tornadoes screaming in unison.

"They're turning it on," Logan said.

" Paradox is accelerating the timeline," I realized. "He's not waiting for the Anchor to decay. He's pulling the plug."

I drew my katanas.

"Okay, peanut. No more jokes. No more bits."

I looked at him.

"We stop this. Or we die."

Logan popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

"Let's go," he said.

# CHAPTER 19: THE POLITICS OF ETERNITY

**LOCATION: TVA HEADQUARTERS (MAIN TIMELINE)**

*Side Story G: The Dissent*

Hunter B-15 walked down the corridor.

The TVA was designed to be timeless. No clocks. No windows. Just the endless, low hum of bureaucracy. But today, the hum was different. It was frantic.

"Sector 4 is reporting fluctuations," Analyst Kevin said, running to keep up with her. "The Time Ripper is drawing power from the main loom. It's unstable."

"Where is Paradox?" B-15 asked.

"He's at the Outpost. In the Void."

B-15 stopped. She turned to Kevin.

"The Void is a dumping ground," she said. "It's not a power station. What is he doing?"

Kevin looked nervous. He adjusted his glasses.

"He... he says he's conducting a 'radical pruning'. He says the timeline is sick. He says mercy is inefficient."

B-15 felt a cold knot in her stomach.

She remembered the old TVA. The one ruled by the Time Keepers. The one that pruned without question. They had fought to change that. They had fought to let the branches grow.

And now Paradox was trying to bring back the shears.

"He's not pruning," B-15 said. "He's murdering. He's killing a timeline because he doesn't like the ending."

She tapped her TemPad.

"Open a channel to the Outpost," she ordered.

"It's blocked, Ma'am. Paradox has locked the frequency."

B-15 looked at the wall monitor. It showed the Sacred Timeline. A beautiful, glowing rope of light.

But there was a dark spot. A rot. Spreading from Earth-10005.

"He's going to kill them all," Kevin whispered.

"Not if we stop him," B-15 said. "Assemble the Minutemen. The *loyal* ones."

"Ma'am? That's treason."

"No," B-15 said. "That's leadership."

She drew her baton. It glowed purple.

"Let's go save a universe."
