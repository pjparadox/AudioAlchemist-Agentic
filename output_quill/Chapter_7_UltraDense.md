# CHAPTER 7: THE TRASH COMPACTOR (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE DROP ZONE**

*Side Story D: The Pruned Nobody*

**POV: STANLEY (VARIANT 442-B)**

Stanley lived in a refrigerator.

It was a 1957 Smeg. Lead-lined. Mint green. It was the only thing in his universe that had survived the pruning.

Stanley was not a hero. He was an actuary. He calculated risk. And the risk calculation for leaving the refrigerator was 100% chance of death.

He peeked through the magnetic seal.

The Void stretched out before him like a wound in the universe. It was a desert of rust and bone. Mountains of scrap metal—twisted skyscrapers, broken Helicarriers, the shattered remains of a Stark Tower—pierced the grey sand.

The sky was the color of a hematoma. Purple. Yellow. Angry.

And *It* was there.

Alioth.

The cloud beast. The Eraser.

It didn't just roar; it vibrated. Stanley could feel it in his teeth. A low, thrumming bass note that signaled the end of existence.

*Don't move,* Stanley told himself. *Don't breathe. You are a vegetable crisper. You are inert matter.*

Then, the sky tore open.

A portal. Orange and jagged.

Debris rained down. A tank. A billboard for a movie called *Green Lantern 2* (Stanley shuddered; some timelines deserved to die). And... a man.

A man in a red suit.

He hit the sand with a wet *thud*.

Stanley watched.

The man stood up. He brushed dirt off his spandex. He looked around at the apocalypse and put his hands on his hips.

"Okay!" the man yelled. His voice carried over the wind. "Who ordered the existential dread with a side of sand? I asked for dressing on the side!"

Stanley pulled the door shut.

*He's loud,* Stanley thought. *Loud things get eaten.*

Outside, the man started singing.

"I'm a Barbie girl! In a Barbie wooooorld!"

Stanley closed his eyes. He clutched his stapler.

*Goodbye, Red Man. It was nice knowing you for twelve seconds.*

**POV: WADE WILSON**

The fall didn't feel like falling. Falling implies gravity. Falling implies physics.

This felt like being deleted.

One moment, I was in Paradox's office, smelling his expensive cologne and the copper tang of his broken nose. The next, I was tumbling through orange static.

It wasn't cold. It wasn't hot. It was the temperature of static on a TV screen. It was the sensation of your foot falling asleep, but applied to your soul.

*Flash.*

I saw a timeline where I was Batman. (The nipples were a choice).
I saw a timeline where I was a barista. (I made excellent foam art).
I saw a timeline where I was happy. (That one hurt the most).

Then, reality reasserted itself.

*CRASH.*

I hit the ground.

Sand forced its way into my nose, my mouth, my ears. It wasn't soft sand. It was sharp. Metallic. It tasted of ash and rust.

I rolled. Tumbling down a dune made of pulverized concrete.

My shoulder hit something hard.

*Snk.*

A piece of rebar went through my trap.

"Gah!"

I stopped rolling. I lay there, face down in the dirt.

Pain.

Glorious, grounding pain. It told me I wasn't dead. Dead people don't feel rebar in their trapezius.

I pushed myself up. The rebar slid out with a wet *shhhhk*. Blood—hot and sticky—trickled down my back.

"Okay," I wheezed. "Okay. That happened."

I looked up.

The scale of the place hit me like a physical blow.

It was endless. A junkyard of the gods. And the sky...

The sky was alive.

A purple storm cloud. Massive. Roiling. It had eyes. It had a hunger that radiated like heat.

*Alioth.*

"Logan!" I shouted.

My voice sounded small. Pathetic.

"Wolvie! Peanut! Hairball!"

Nothing.

Just the wind howling through the hollowed-out skull of a Giant-Man.

I started walking.

My boots crunched on debris. I stepped over a license plate that said "GOTHAM". I stepped over a vibranium shield (dented).

Then I saw it.

A boot. A yellow boot.

Sticking out of a pile of tires.

I ran. My lungs burned. The air here was thin, metallic. It tasted like sucking on a penny.

I clawed at the tires. "Logan!"

I pulled him out.

He was heavy. Dense. Like moving a statue made of meat.

He was unconscious. His face was a mask of blood and mud. His healing factor was working—I could see the skin knitting together over a gash on his forehead—but it was slow. The Void dampened everything. Even miracles.

"Wake up," I slapped his cheek. *Thwack.*

He didn't move.

"Wake up!" I slapped him harder.

His eyes snapped open.

Yellow. Bloodshot. Feral.

He popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

He tried to stab me.

It was a lazy stab. Drunk. Slow. I leaned back, and the claws swiped the air an inch from my nose.

"Easy, tiger," I said. "You're hungover. You're pruned. You're in a bad mood. I get it."

He tried to stand. He stumbled. He fell to his knees.

He vomited.

It was mostly whiskey. Clear. Pungent. It splashed onto the sand, sizzling slightly.

"Classy," I said.

Logan wiped his mouth. He looked up at me.

"You," he growled.

"Me," I agreed.

"You shot me."

"I did. In the head. Point blank. It was very *Of Mice and Men*. 'Tell me about the rabbits, Logan.' *Bang.*"

He looked around. He saw the purple sky. He saw Alioth.

"Where is this?"

"The Void," I said. "The end of time. The place where bad ideas go to die."

I gestured to the landscape.

"And guess what? We're the newest exhibit."

Logan stood up. He swayed.

For the first time, I realized that the suit—the yellow and blue suit—wasn't just a costume. It was a target. It was bright. It was loud. In this world of grey and rust, he was a neon sign saying "EAT ME."

"We need cover," I said. "Before the cloud dog sees us."

He didn't argue. He just stared at the horizon with eyes that had seen too much.

"I need a drink," he said.

"Of course you do," I sighed. "Come on. I smell ozone and... is that chimichangas? No. Probably rotting flesh. But let's follow it anyway."
