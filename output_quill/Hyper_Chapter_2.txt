# CHAPTER 2: THE VICTORY LAP

The bar was called "The Gavel," because lawyers were nothing if not uncreative with their watering holes. It was dark, smoky, and loud. The air was thick with the smell of expensive scotch, cheap beer, and ambition. It was the kind of place where deals were made in the shadows and reputations were dissected over ice.

The firm had rented out the back room. It was a sea of suits. Grey, black, navy. The uniform of the soldier ant.
"To Erin!"
The toast came from everywhere. Glasses clinked.
Erin raised her vodka tonic. She took a sip. It was cold, sharp, and clean. It washed away the taste of Van Hopper's breath.

"You really pulled that one out of the fire," Ray said, sliding into the booth next to her. Ray was a junior associate who looked at Erin with a mix of terrified awe and lust. He was twenty-five, fresh out of Harvard, and still believed that the law was about Justice.
"I thought the neighbor's testimony was going to sink us," Ray continued, loosening his tie. "When she said she saw the car... I thought we were done."

"The neighbor was senile, Ray," Erin said, leaning against the padded leather of the booth. "You just had to remind the jury that she watches *Soap Operas* all afternoon. Reality blurs for people like that. They see drama where there is only rain."

Ray laughed, but it was a nervous sound. He took a long pull of his beer.
"Still. Van Hopper... he did it, right?"
Erin froze. She looked at him. The question hung in the air, heavy and wet, cutting through the noise of the party.
She turned slowly to face him. Her eyes were cold.
"Ray," she said, her voice dropping to that dangerous register she used in court when she was about to gut a witness. "Did the jury say he did it?"
"No."
"Did the judge say he did it?"
"No."
"Then he didn't do it."
"But... moral truth?" Ray stammered. "I mean, the guy had a history of domestic violence. The wife had bruises..."
"Moral truth is for priests and poets, Ray. We deal in legal truth. And legal truth is a construct. It's a building we build, brick by brick, out of evidence and procedure. If the building stands, the client walks. If it falls, they go to jail. Today, the building stood."

She turned away, dismissing him. She couldn't have this conversation. Not tonight. Not when the adrenaline was still humming in her veins.
She scanned the room.
Success.
It looked like this. Tailored suits, laughter, the promise of bonuses.
So why did she feel like she was standing at the bottom of a deep well?

She looked at her reflection in the mirror behind the bar.
*Who are you?*
The woman in the mirror didn't answer. She just looked tired. Her blonde hair was perfect, her makeup was flawless, but her eyes... there was a hollowness there.
The adrenaline was fading, leaving behind the crash. The emptiness.
She had saved a monster today. And she would get a raise for it.

She pushed past Ray and walked to the payphone in the hallway. It smelled of stale tobacco and old gum.
She dialed a number she knew by heart.
*Ring. Ring. Ring.*
"Hello?"
Her mother's voice. Tired. Small.
"Hey, Mom."
"Erin? Is everything okay? It's late."
"I know. I just... I won the case, Mom. Van Hopper. He walked."
Silence on the other end.
"That's... good, honey. I know you worked hard."
"It's huge, Mom. Karl is talking about the partnership. The Senior Partnership."
"That's wonderful, Erin. Really." A pause. "Did you go to Mass on Sunday?"
Erin closed her eyes, leaning her forehead against the cold metal of the phone box.
"No, Mom. I was working."
"You work too much. You need... you need something else in your life."
"I have a life, Mom. I have a career."
"You have a job, Erin. A life is... family. Faith."
"I have to go, Mom. The party is loud."
"Okay. I'll pray for you."
"Thanks."

Erin hung up. The dial tone hummed for a second before she slammed the receiver down.
*Pray for me.*
As if prayer changed anything. Prayer hadn't saved Mrs. Van Hopper. Prayer hadn't stopped James from wrapping that cord around her neck.
Only a good lawyer had saved him.

"Trouble at home?"
Erin spun around.
It was Mark Campbell. A prosecutor from the DA's office. He was leaning against the wall, holding a beer. He looked disheveled, like he had had a long day.
"Campbell," Erin said, composing herself. "Spying on the enemy?"
"Just getting a drink. Congratulations on Van Hopper. That was... masterful. Evil, but masterful."
"I'll take that as a compliment."
"You should. You made a jury believe that rain makes cars invisible. That's a special kind of magic."
"It's not magic, Mark. It's doubt."
Mark took a sip of his beer. "You know he's going to do it again, right? Guys like that... they don't stop. Next time, he won't use a cord. He'll use a knife. Or a gun."
"And next time, maybe you'll have better evidence," Erin said, brushing past him.
"How do you live with it, Erin?" Mark asked quietly.
Erin stopped. She didn't turn around.
"I win, Mark. That's how I live with it."

She walked back into the bar, but the noise was too much now. It sounded like static. Like screaming.
She found Karl Gunderson in the corner, holding court with the junior partners.
"You're not celebrating," he observed, seeing her face.
"I am. Internally."
"Save it," Karl said, his voice cutting through the noise. "I need you in my office tomorrow morning. Early."
"Good news?"
Karl smiled. It was the smile of a shark who just smelled blood in the water.
"Big news. The Archdiocese."
"Church business? I thought they had their own lawyers."
"Not for this. This is... special." Karl swirled his scotch. The amber liquid caught the light. "Go home, Erin. Get some sleep. You're going to need it."

Erin looked at him.
"Is it bad?"
"It's a homicide, Erin. A priest killed a nun. Or a student. It's messy."
"And you want me?"
"I want the woman who let James Van Hopper walk. I need a miracle worker."

Erin nodded. She finished her drink in one swallow.
"I'll see you at eight."
She walked out of the bar, into the rain.
The city was dark. The streetlights reflected in the puddles like eyes.
She hailed a cab.
She felt cold.
Not just the weather.
A deep, internal cold.
As if she had just agreed to something she didn't understand.
As if she had just signed a contract in invisible ink.
