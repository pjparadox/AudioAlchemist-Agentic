# CHAPTER 5: THE DESCENT

The drive to the County Jail was a descent in every sense of the word. Erin drove her Mercedes from the gleaming canyons of the financial district down to the rusted, graffiti-stained arteries of the south side. The architecture changed from glass and steel to brick and barbed wire. The people changed from suits to jumpsuits.
The rain had picked up. It wasn't cleansing; it was oppressive. It smeared the windshield with grey sludge. The wipers fought a losing battle. *Thwump-hiss. Thwump-hiss.*

She parked in the visitors' lot, a cracked asphalt slab surrounded by a chain-link fence topped with razor wire.
She checked her reflection in the rearview mirror. Perfect. Impenetrable.
But her hands were shaking.
Just a little.
*It's the coffee,* she told herself. *Too much caffeine.*
But she knew it wasn't the coffee.
It was the file. The photo of Emily Rose. The look in those black eyes.

She got out of the car, locking it with the remote. *Chirp.*
She walked to the intake center.
The smell hit her first.
It wasn't just a smell; it was a physical presence. A wall of odor.
Industrial pine cleaner trying to mask the scent of unwashed bodies. Stale cigarette smoke. Urine. And underneath it all, the metallic tang of fear. It was the smell of caged animals.
Erin breathed through her mouth.

She checked her bag with the guard, a woman named Officer Higgins (no relation to the witness, Erin hoped). Higgins had tired eyes and a belt weighed down by keys, a radio, and pepper spray.
"Attorney visit for Moore," Erin said, sliding her bar card across the scratched plexiglass.
Higgins looked at the card, then at Erin.
"You're the one," she said. It wasn't a question.
"I'm his attorney."
"Good luck," Higgins said. She slid a visitor badge back. "Block D. Solitary."

Solitary?
Erin frowned. "Why is he in solitary?"
"Warden's orders. He was... upsetting the other inmates."
"Upsetting them how?"
Higgins leaned forward. "Preaching. Screaming in Latin at night. The guys in D Block are tough, lady. Killers. Rapists. But Moore? He scares them."
Erin clipped the badge to her lapel. "I'll keep that in mind."

She walked through the metal detector. *Beep.*
"Underwire," she muttered, raising her arms.
Higgins waved the wand. "Go ahead."

The corridors were long, echoey tunnels of concrete painted a color that could only be described as "institutional despair green."
*Clack. Clack. Clack.*
Erin's heels sounded like gunshots in the silence.
Inmates watched her from the communal cells in B Block. Hands gripping the bars. Eyes tracking her.
"Hey, sweetness! You looking for a client?"
"I didn't do it, baby! Come talk to me!"
"Look at the legs on that one. Walking tall."
Erin didn't flinch. She stared straight ahead, her face a mask of indifference. She had walked these halls a hundred times. They were just noise. Background radiation.

But as she approached D Block, the noise stopped.
The air grew colder here.
The lights buzzed with a frantic energy.
She passed a cell. A man was standing at the bars. He was naked from the waist up, his body covered in tattoos of spiders.
He looked at Erin.
He didn't catcall. He didn't smile.
He hissed.
*"We see you,"* he whispered.
Erin stopped. She looked at him.
"Excuse me?"
The man smiled. His teeth were filed into points.
*"The one who walks with the Law. The one who sells doubt."*
Erin felt a chill run down her spine.
"Do I know you?"
*"We know you. We know the penny taste in your mouth. We know the cat who stares at nothing."*

Erin stepped back. Her heart slammed against her ribs.
How could he know?
"Guard!" she yelled.
The guard at the end of the hall looked up from his magazine.
"Move along, counselor. Don't talk to the animals."
Erin looked back at the man.
He was gone. Retreated into the shadows of his cell.
But she could hear him laughing. A dry, scratching sound.

She walked faster.
She reached the heavy steel door of the interview room.
"He's in Interview Room 2," the guard said, unlocking it. "Watch yourself. He's been weird all night."
"Define weird."
"Talking to himself. Argumentative. Like there's two people in there."
"Is there?"
"No. Just him. And his Bible."

Erin took a deep breath. She smoothed her skirt.
She was Erin Bruner. She was a partner. She was a shark.
She wasn't afraid of a priest.
She opened the door and stepped inside.

The room was small. Claustrophobic.
Father Richard Moore sat at the metal table.
He was wearing his clerical collar, but it was dingy, frayed at the edges. His black shirt was wrinkled.
He looked up.
Erin stopped.
She expected a fanatic. A crazy person.
She saw a man who looked like he had been hollowed out by grief.
His eyes were dark, intelligent, and haunted.
"Father Moore," she said.
He didn't blink.
"You're the lawyer," he said. His voice was rough. Gravel.
"I'm Erin Bruner."
"You smell like rain," he said. "And fear."

Erin placed her briefcase on the table. *Click.*
"I'm not afraid, Father. I'm here to help you."
Moore smiled. It was a sad, terrible smile.
"No one can help me," he whispered. "The war has already begun."
Erin sat down.
"Then let's prepare for battle," she said.
But looking at him, she couldn't shake the image of the man in the cell.
*We know the cat who stares at nothing.*
She opened her file.
Her hand was steady.
But inside, she was screaming.
