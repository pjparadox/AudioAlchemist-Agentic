# CRITIQUE REPORT: BOOK 1 START

## General Assessment
The draft successfully captures the "Chimichanga Blend" of styles. The transitions between the visceral violence of Blackmore (Prologue), the cynical observation of Prince (Chapter 1), and the atmospheric dread of King (Chapter 2) are effective. However, the dialogue tags need refinement to meet the "No Said" mandate.

## Section-Specific Feedback

### Prologue: Bye Bye Bye
*   **Strengths:** Excellent visceral descriptions ("sound like a gunshot wrapped in a wet towel"). Good integration of music cues.
*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   "The lead agent said" - Generic tag.
    *   "I said" - Can be replaced with action.
*   **Action:** Replace 2-3 dialogue tags with action beats or remove them entirely.

### Chapter 1: The Commute
*   **Strengths:** Strong sensory details ("stale coffee," "polyester monstrosity"). The internal monologue is sharp.
*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   "So," the customer said. -> Can be implied.
    *   "Wade said" (multiple instances).
*   **Action:** Sharpen the dialogue exchange. Remove "said" in the rapid-fire sales pitch section.

### Chapter 2: A Very Wilsons Birthday
*   **Strengths:** Good emotional weight. The Stephen King pastiche is strong in the narration.
*   **Weaknesses:**
    *   High frequency of "said" tags in the conversation with Vanessa.
    *   "He said," "She said" pattern is repetitive.
*   **Action:** Rewrite the Vanessa/Wade dialogue to use physical intimacy (touch, look) as beats instead of tags.

## Formatting Check
*   **Voiceovers:** Correctly formatted (Italics/No Quotes).
*   **Sensory Details:** Good presence of smell and touch.

## Verdict
**Status:** REVISION REQUIRED (Minor).
**Focus:** Dialogue Tag Excision.
