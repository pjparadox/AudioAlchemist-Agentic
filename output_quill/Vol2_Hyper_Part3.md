# CHAPTER 14: THE AFTERMATH

**LOCATION: THE CANYON CAMP**

We didn't go far. We found a cave. It smelled of bat guano and damp stone, but it was defensible.

We built a fire.

Logan sat by the entrance, staring out at the dark. He was cleaning his claws again. The rag was turning black with Sabretooth's blood.

I watched him.

His healing factor was working. The bite on his shoulder was already a pink scar. But the look in his eyes... that wasn't healing. That was festering.

"You okay?" I asked.

"I killed my brother," he said.

"He was trying to kill you," I pointed out. "And me. And probably a lot of other people."

"He's still my brother."

He scrubbed a spot on his claw that wouldn't come clean.

"In my timeline," Logan said, "Victor and I... we fought for a century. Back to back. We were monsters together."

He looked at the fire.

"I thought... I thought maybe here, in the Void... maybe he'd be different. Maybe we could have..."

He trailed off.

"Redemption?" I offered.

"Peace," he corrected. "Just peace."

I moved closer to the fire. The warmth felt good on my skin.

"There is no peace for guys like us, Wolvie. We're weapons. We're built for war."

"I'm tired of war," he whispered.

"Me too," I said. "But the war isn't over. Not yet."

I pulled a chimichanga out of my pouch. It was squashed. Cold.

"Want half?" I offered.

He looked at the sad, flat burrito.

"No."

"More for me."

We ate in silence. The fire crackled. Outside, Alioth roared in the distance.

It was a grim night. But we were alive.

# CHAPTER 15: THE SILENCE

**LOCATION: THE OPEN PLAIN**

The next day was a slog.

The buggy had a flat tire. We fixed it with duct tape and hope.

We drove across an endless grey plain. The silence in the car was heavy. It wasn't the angry silence of before. It was a thoughtful silence. A mourning silence.

"You know," I said, "that decapitation last night? Solid 9 out of 10. You lost a point for the landing."

Logan didn't growl. He just adjusted his grip on the wheel.

"Don't push it," he said.

"I'm just saying. You have style. You have flair. You could be a dancer. *Magic Mike XXL: Mutant Mayhem*."

"Stop."

"Okay. Stopping."

I looked out the window.

"We make a good team," I said softly.

Logan didn't answer immediately. He looked at the road.

"We're not a team," he said. "We're two idiots in a buggy."

"Two idiots who killed Sabretooth," I corrected. "Two idiots who are going to save the world."

He glanced at me.

"Maybe," he said.

It wasn't a yes. But it wasn't a no.

# CHAPTER 16: THE CAMPFIRE (SETUP)

We camped near a cluster of rocks that looked like teeth jutting out of the ground.

The wind had picked up. It howled through the gaps in the stone, sounding like a chorus of ghosts.

We huddled near the fire.

"Why do you wear the suit?" Logan asked suddenly.

I looked up. He was staring at my red spandex.

"Because it hides the blood," I said. "Classic line. True story."

"No," he said. "Why do *you* wear it? You could wear anything. You could be anyone."

"It's who I am," I said. "It's the only thing that fits. Wade Wilson is... messy. Deadpool is clean. Deadpool has a script."

I looked at his suit. The yellow and blue.

"Why do *you* wear it?" I asked. "You never wore it in the movies. You hated it."

Logan looked down. He touched the yellow fabric.

"Scott," he said. "Scott wanted me to wear it."

"Cyclops?"

"Yeah. He said... he said the X-Men needed to be symbols. Bright. Hopeful. He said people needed to see us coming and know that help had arrived."

He laughed. A bitter, dry sound.

"I told him he was an idiot. I told him I wasn't a billboard. I told him I wasn't a hero. I told him to take his yellow spandex and shove it."

He traced the X on his belt.

"I never wore it. Not once. Until..."

He stopped.

"Until the funeral?" I asked.

He nodded.

"I put it on," he whispered. "After they died. I put it on to bury them. Because... because I wanted them to know I was one of them. Finally. I wanted them to know I was on the team."

He looked at me. The firelight danced in his wet eyes.

"But it was too late. They were dead. And I was just a clown in a yellow suit standing over a grave."

# CHAPTER 17: THE CONFESSION

**THE BESTSELLER MOMENT**

The fire popped. A spark flew up into the dark sky, joining the fake stars.

I looked at him. The Wolverine. The legend. The man on the lunchbox.

He looked small. He looked broken.

*He bleeds like it matters. I bleed like a punchline.*

"I didn't just ignore the call," Logan said. His voice was barely a whisper. "I was... I was afraid."

I froze.

"Afraid?"

"I was afraid that if I went... I'd kill them."

He looked at his hands. His knuckles were white.

"I lose control, Wade. You know that. The Berserker Rage. It's not a superpower. It's a disease. It's a fire in my brain. I was afraid I'd hurt Jeannie. Or the kids. Or Scott."

Tears leaked out of his eyes. He didn't wipe them away. They tracked through the dirt on his cheeks.

"So I stayed away. I drank. I told myself I was protecting them by not being there. I told myself I was the danger."

He sobbed. A racking, ugly sound.

"But they needed me. The humans... they came with Sentinels. They slaughtered them. And I was safe in a bar, staring at a bottle of whiskey."

He looked at me. His face was a mask of agony.

"I didn't kill them, Wade. But I let them die. Is there a difference?"

I stared at him.

I wanted to make a joke. I wanted to say something about how that's a classic trope. I wanted to diffuse the tension because the tension was crushing me.

But I couldn't.

Because he was right.

And because I knew exactly how he felt.

I stood up. I walked around the fire.

I sat down next to him.

I didn't hug him. He would have stabbed me.

I just sat there. Shoulder to shoulder. Two broken men in the dark.

"No," I said. "There isn't a difference. But you're here now."

"Does it matter?" he asked.

"It matters to me," I said. "And it matters to the kid we saved. And it matters to the universe we're going to save."

I reached into my pouch. I pulled out the picture of Vanessa.

"We messed up, Logan. We both did. We failed the people we loved. We let them down."

I held the picture over the fire.

"But we can't change the past. We can only change the ending."

I dropped the picture.

It curled. It blackened. It turned to ash.

"So let's not fail the next ones," I said. "Let's save the world. Not because we're heroes. But because we're the only ones left."

Logan watched the picture burn.

He took a deep breath. He wiped his face.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay?"

"Okay. Let's save your damn world."

He stood up. He offered me a hand.

I took it.

His grip was strong. Anchor strong.

"Maximum Effort?" he asked.

I smiled beneath my mask.

"Maximum Effort."
