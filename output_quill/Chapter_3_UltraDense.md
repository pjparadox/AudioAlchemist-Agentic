# CHAPTER 3: THE QUIET KNIFE (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**POV: VANESSA CARLYSLE**
**TIMELINE: EARTH-10005 (PRESENT DAY)**
**LOCATION: THE APARTMENT**

The apartment didn't smell like home anymore. It smelled like a performance.

It smelled of cheap tequila (the kind that burns the nose before it hits the tongue), taco seasoning that was mostly salt and red dye #40, and the humid, over-sweet scent of fifty bodies crammed into a space designed for two.

I stood in the doorway of the kitchen, holding a bowl of guacamole that Blind Al had already insulted three times.

"It's too chunky!" she had screamed, waving a chip like a dagger. "I want paste! I want green sludge!"

I looked past Al. I looked past Peter, who was showing Colossus a picture of his cat with a level of earnestness that was almost painful.

I looked at Wade.

He was standing near the hallway mirror. He thought he was alone.

He wasn't looking at the party. He was looking at his reflection. He was adjusting that terrible, lumpy toupee—a piece of synthetic hair that sat on his scarred scalp like a nervous badger.

I watched him practice.

He stretched his lips wide. *Smile.*
He held it for a count of three.
Then he let it drop. His face went slack. The light went out of his eyes.
Then he did it again.
*Smile. Hold. Drop.*

It was mechanical. It was the same way he used to practice reloading his Desert Eagles. Muscle memory. Repetition until the action became instinct.

But this wasn't a reload. This was a lie.

A sharp pain twisted in my chest. Not a heartburn pain. A grief pain. A quiet knife sliding between my ribs.

*He’s dying,* I thought. *Not the cancer. The cancer is gone. But the spark? The chaos? The thing that made him Wade? It’s starving.*

I remembered the first time I met him. The bar. The swagger. The way he took up space not because he was big, but because he was *loud*. He was a disaster, sure. A walking collection of red flags sewn into a quilt. But he was a *beautiful* disaster. He was alive. He burned bright enough to light up the dark corners of my own life.

Now?

Now he was a car salesman named Wade who wore beige.

He was doing it for me. I knew that. He was trying to be the man he thought I wanted. The safe man. The man who came home at 5:30 PM with nothing on his hands but ink stains. The man who could give me a family without the risk of mercenaries kicking down the door.

But I missed him.

God help me, I missed the mercenary. I missed the edge. I missed the dangerous, unpredictable idiot who made me laugh until I couldn't breathe.

*Be careful what you wish for,* my mother used to say. *You might get a husband who sells Kias.*

Wade sighed. I saw his shoulders slump. It was a heavy motion, like he was carrying the weight of the entire world's mediocrity on his back.

"Happy Birthday to me," he whispered to the mirror.

His voice broke. Just a little. A tiny crack in the porcelain.

I couldn't watch anymore. It felt like an intrusion. Like watching someone pray to a god they didn't believe in.

I took a deep breath. I pasted a smile on my face. A real one. Because I did love him. I loved him enough to play along. I loved him enough to pretend I didn't see the cracks.

"Hey, handsome," I called out.

Wade spun around.

The transformation was instant. Terrifyingly fast.

The mask slammed down. The "Salesman Wade" persona clicked into place like a visor. His eyes widened. The smile became fixed. The posture straightened.

"Ness!" He beamed. "You caught me! Just... admiring the merchandise. High resale value on this guy. Low mileage. Only driven on Sundays by a little old lady from Pasadena."

He walked over to me.

He kissed me.

His lips were warm. They tasted of the cheap beer he'd been nursing all night.

But his eyes?

His eyes were screaming. They were panic-stricken, blue chips of ice searching mine for approval, for validation. *Do you believe me? Am I doing it right? Is this what normal looks like?*

"Everyone is here," I said, keeping my voice light. "Blind Al is arguing with the guacamole."

"Classic Al," Wade said. "She hates texture. If it's not a slurry, it's a threat."

He took my hand.

His grip was loose. Careful. Like he was afraid he might crush me if he held on too tight. Like he was afraid his own strength—his own nature—would ruin the illusion.

"Let's go party," he said.

"Wade," I said.

He stopped. "Yeah?"

"You okay?"

I asked it, but I already knew the answer. I wanted him to tell me the truth. I wanted him to drop the mask. I wanted him to say, *No, Ness. I'm miserable. I hate the wig. I hate the cars. I want to stab something.*

If he said that, maybe we could fix it. Maybe we could find a middle ground.

But he didn't.

"Me?" He laughed. It was a brittle sound. "Never better. I'm living the dream, baby. The American Dream. Credit debt, lower back pain, and a lingering fear of the IRS."

He winked.

It was a lie. A perfect, polished, heartbreaking lie.

*I have to let him go,* I thought. The realization hit me with the force of a physical blow. *If I love him, I have to let him go. I can't keep him in this cage. I can't watch him fade away until there's nothing left but the beige.*

I squeezed his hand.

"Okay," I said. "Let's go."

We walked into the living room.

The party was loud. The music—some pop hit from five years ago—was thumping.

Dopinder was trying to flirt with a girl who looked terrified. Negasonic was texting with a ferocity that suggested she was coordinating a drone strike. Yukio waved, radiating pink energy and genuine joy.

It was a family. A weird, broken, beautiful family.

But Wade... Wade looked like a ghost haunting his own life.

He moved through the room like a guest. He laughed at the right times. He made the right jokes. But he wasn't *present*.

He grabbed a chimichanga. He took a bite.

"Mmm," he said. "Moist."

He looked at the window.

Outside, the rain was falling. Grey, miserable rain.

His eyes lingered on the dark glass. He was looking for something out there. A threat. A villain. A plot twist. Anything to break the monotony.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

The sound cut through the laughter.

Three sharp raps. Precise. Authoritative. Not the knock of a neighbor complaining about the noise. Not the knock of a pizza delivery guy.

This was a knock that meant business.

Wade froze.

I saw it. The shift. The tension in his shoulders coiled tight. The way his weight shifted to the balls of his feet. The way his hand drifted toward his hip, reaching for a holster that wasn't there.

For a second, the Salesman was gone. The Mercenary was back.

And he looked... relieved.

"I'll get it," Wade said.

His voice was different. Deeper. calmer.

He walked to the door.

I watched him go. And I knew.

*This is it. This is the end of the quiet.*

I felt a tear slide down my cheek. I didn't wipe it away.

*Go,* I whispered to his back. *Go be who you are.*
