# CHAPTER 18: WITNESS: THE PRIEST

The courtroom was packed. Standing room only.
The air was electric. You could feel the tension vibrating in the wood of the benches.
"The defense calls Father Richard Moore."
A murmur went through the crowd.
Moore stood up.
He smoothed his cassock.
He walked to the witness stand.
He didn't look at the jury. He didn't look at the judge.
He looked at Erin.
His eyes were clear. Calm.
He placed his hand on the Bible.
"I do," he said.
He sat down.

Erin walked to the podium.
She felt the bruise on her wrist throbbing under her suit jacket. It was a reminder. A brand.
"Father Moore," she began. "Why did you perform an exorcism on Emily Rose?"
"Because she was possessed," Moore said. His voice was quiet, raspy. "Because the medical treatment was killing her."
"Objection," Owen Thomas said, not looking up from his notes. "Medical conclusion from a non-expert."
"Sustained," Judge Brewster said. "The jury will disregard the second part of the answer."
Erin nodded. "Father, did you tell Emily to stop taking her medication?"
"I told her that the medication was silencing her soul. I told her that she had a choice."
"And what did she choose?"
"She chose to fight."

Erin walked closer.
"Father, the prosecution claims you were negligent. That you ignored the medical facts. That you let a girl die because of your... superstition."
Moore looked at Owen.
"Superstition is a word we use for things we are afraid to understand."
"Were you afraid, Father?"
"Yes."
"Of what?"
"Of failing her."
"And did you fail her?"
Moore paused. He looked at his hands.
"I failed to save her body," he whispered. "But I did not fail her soul."

Erin turned to the jury.
"Tell us about the night of October 31st. Halloween."
A hush fell over the room.
"It was storming," Moore said. "The barn was cold. We had Emily on the cot. We had restraints."
"Why restraints?"
"Because when the entities took control... she had the strength of ten men. She would have hurt herself. She would have hurt us."
"Entities? Plural?"
"Yes."
"How many?"
"Six."
Erin let the number hang there.
"And did you speak to them?"
"I commanded them to reveal themselves."
"And did they?"
"Yes."

Owen stood up. "Your Honor, I must object. We are now entering the realm of theology, not fact. We cannot cross-examine a demon."
"We are establishing the defendant's state of mind," Erin argued. "If he believed he was talking to demons, it explains his actions."
"I'll allow it," Brewster said. "But tread carefully, Ms. Bruner."

Erin looked at Moore.
"Tell us the names, Father."
Moore closed his eyes. He seemed to be in pain.
"They spoke in different voices," he said. "Different languages."
"What did they say?"
"One said... 'I am the one who dwelt within Cain'."
The jury shifted. Juror 11 (The Nurse) crossed herself.
"Another said... 'I am the one who dwelt within Nero'."
Moore's voice grew louder. Stronger.
"Another said... 'I once dwelt within Judas'."
The temperature in the courtroom seemed to drop. Or maybe it was just the collective shiver of the audience.
"And the others?"
"One was Legion. One was Belial."
"And the last one?"
Moore opened his eyes. He looked directly at Owen Thomas.
"The last one," Moore whispered, "did not speak a name. It spoke a title."
"What title?"
*"I am Lucifer, the Devil in the Flesh."*

The silence that followed was absolute.
It wasn't just quiet. It was a vacuum.
Then, a sound.
*Crack.*
A lightbulb in the ceiling fixture above the witness stand exploded.
Glass rained down.
People screamed.
Judge Brewster banged his gavel. "Order! Order!"
Erin didn't move. She looked at Moore.
He hadn't flinched.
He was looking at the empty air above the jury box.
He was smiling.
A sad, knowing smile.
"They are here," he whispered. "They are listening."

Erin looked at the jury.
They were terrified.
Not of Moore.
Of the room.
Of the shadows in the corners.
She had done it. She had broken the wall of rationality.
She had let the contagion in.
"No further questions," she whispered.
She sat down.
Her hand was shaking.
She put it under the table.
She touched the bruise on her wrist.
It was hot. Burning hot.
As if the hand that made it was still holding on.
