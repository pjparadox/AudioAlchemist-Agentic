# CHAPTER 6: THE SACRED TIMELINE (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE TVA)**
**LOCATION: MR. PARADOX'S OFFICE**

Mr. Paradox's office smelled of expensive cologne and genocide.

It was a subtle scent. Sandalwood. Leather. And underneath it, the faint, metallic tang of a billion lives being snuffed out for the greater good.

I sat in the chair. It was designed to be uncomfortable. It angled forward slightly, forcing you to engage your core or slide off. It was a power chair. A "I am the boss and you are a disposable asset" chair.

"Paradox," I said, dusting off my orange jumpsuit. "Like two doctors? Or like when you park a pair of boats?"

Mr. Paradox didn't smile. He walked behind his desk.

The desk was a slab of obsidian. Polished to a mirror sheen. It reflected my face back at me—a distorted, scarred map of bad choices.

"You're probably wondering why you're here," Paradox said. He steepled his fingers. His hands were manicured. Soft. Hands that had never held a shovel or a gun, only a pen that signed death warrants.

"I assume it's about the Blockbuster late fees," I said. "I swear, I returned *Jerry Maguire*. I rewound it and everything. I was kind. I was the kindness."

"This isn't about *Jerry Maguire*," Paradox said.

He tapped a key on his keyboard. A mechanical *clack*.

A monitor on the wall flickered to life.

It wasn't a normal screen. It was a window into time.

I saw myself.

Not me-here. Me-then.
I saw Deadpool slicing through Francis's goons.
I saw me hugging Vanessa.
I saw me shooting Ryan Reynolds in the head while he held the *Green Lantern* script. (You're welcome, Canada).

"We have been watching you, Mr. Wilson," Paradox said. "You are... unique."

"I prefer 'special'," I said. "Like the Olympics. Or the sauce."

"You have soiled the timeline," Paradox continued. "You have jumped between realities like a flea on a dog. You used Cable's device to fix your mistakes. To save your girlfriend."

"And Peter," I added. "Don't forget Peter. He has a mustache. It anchors the whole team."

"Technically," Paradox said, "you should be pruned. Erased. Sent to the Void."

"But?"

"But," Paradox smiled. It was a smile that didn't reach his eyes. It stopped at his mouth, like a 'Do Not Enter' sign. "I see potential. I see a man who is wasted on a dying timeline."

"Dying?" I felt a cold knot in my stomach. A physical heaviness. "What do you mean, dying?"

"Your universe," Paradox said. "Earth-10005. It is decaying. It is withering on the vine. It lost its Anchor Being."

"Anchor Being?"

"A specific individual," Paradox explained, "whose existence is so pivotal that when they die, the entire timeline begins to unravel. Slowly at first. Then... all at once."

On the screen, the image changed.

A grave. A wooden cross.

Logan.

"He died," Paradox said. "And because he died, your world is dying. It might take a thousand years. It might take a week. But it is inevitable."

I stared at the screen.

*Logan. That grumpy, hairy bastard.*

I remembered the box. The bones. The silence of the forest.

*He really was the center of the universe. He was the tentpole holding up the circus.*

"So," I said. "We're screwed."

"Your world is," Paradox corrected. "But *you* don't have to be."

He stood up. He walked around the desk.

He put a hand on my shoulder. His suit fabric was silk. Smooth. Expensive. It contrasted with the rough, itchy canvas of my prisoner jumpsuit.

"I am offering you a ticket out, Wade. A chance to join the Sacred Timeline. The MCU. A world of heroes. Avengers. Gods. You can matter. You can be important."

He gestured to the screen.

I saw it.

I saw Thor. The God of Thunder. He was crying. He was holding a masked figure in his arms.

Me.

"Why is Thor crying?" I asked.

"That happens in the distant future," Paradox said, quickly turning off the screen. "Don't worry about it."

"He was crying," I whispered. "Into my mask. He smelled like lavender and lightning."

"The point is," Paradox said, leaning in. "You can leave the trash behind. You can save yourself."

"Save myself," I repeated.

I looked at the blank monitor.

"And my friends? Vanessa? Blind Al? Dopinder?"

Paradox sighed. A long, weary exhalation.

*Side Story C: The Efficiency of Mercy*

**POV: PARADOX**

Paradox looked at the mercenary.

He saw a clown. A mess. A tumor with legs.

*He doesn't understand,* Paradox thought. *He thinks this is cruelty. It is not. It is gardening.*

The timeline was a garden. And Earth-10005 was a weed. It was choking. It was brown and brittle. To let it live was to prolong its suffering.

He remembered the first time he used the Time Ripper. The silence that followed. The peace.

*Mercy is inefficient,* Paradox told himself. *Compassion is slow. The Ripper is fast. It is clean. I am not a monster. I am a janitor.*

"Regrettable casualties," Paradox said aloud. "But necessary pruning. To grow the tree, one must cut the dead branches."

**POV: WADE**

I looked at him.

I looked at the suit. The slick hair. The smug, bureaucratic soul of a man who viewed genocide as a spreadsheet error.

*He wants me to let them die,* the voice in my head said. *He wants me to be a hero by letting everyone I love turn to dust.*

I stood up.

"You know," I said. "I've been selling cars for six years. I've heard a lot of pitches. 'No money down.' 'Zero APR.' 'We'll throw in the floor mats.'"

I walked over to the camera. I looked directly into the lens.

"But this?" I said. "This is the worst deal in the history of trade deals."

I turned back to Paradox.

"I don't want to be an Avenger," I said. "I want to be an Avenger... *adjacent*."

I grabbed him by the lapels.

"And nobody prunes my friends."

*The 3-Beat Brutality.*

*Beat 1: Mechanics.*
I pulled his head down. I drove my forehead into the bridge of his nose.
*HEADBUTT.*

*Beat 2: Consequence.*
*CRACK.*
The sound was wet and crunchy. Like stepping on a beetle.
Blood—bright red, arterial—sprayed across his pristine white shirt. It splattered his face. It ruined the silk tie.
He stumbled back, clutching his face. "My nose! You broke my nose!"

*Beat 3: Aftertaste.*
My forehead throbbed. A dull ache.
But the satisfaction? It tasted like sugar. It tasted like justice.

"In a TVA facility!" Paradox shrieked. "That's... that's administratively impossible!"

"I'm unpredictable!" I yelled. "I'm the X-factor! I'm the glitch in the system!"

I grabbed a TemPad from the desk.

"Where is the suit?" I demanded.

"What suit?" Paradox gurgled through a mouthful of blood.

"My suit! The red one! The tight one! The one that smells like crotch and justice!"

He pointed to a locker in the corner.

I ran to it. I ripped the door open.

There it was.

Red. Black. Beautiful.

"Hello, lover," I whispered.

I grabbed it.

"Paradox!" I shouted. "I'm gonna go find myself a Wolverine! And when I come back, we're gonna have a serious talk about your HR policies!"

I typed in a command. *Open Portal.*

An orange rectangle fizzled into existence.

I jumped.

The office vanished. The smell of expensive cologne vanished.

Replaced by the roar of the multiverse.
