# CHAPTER 5: SNATCH-AND-BAG (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: EARTH-10005 (PRESENT DAY)**
**LOCATION: THE APARTMENT**

The knock wasn't just a sound. It was a puncture wound in the atmosphere of the room.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Three sharp, precise impacts that cut through the low-fi pop music and the chatter about guacamole texture. It was the sound of plot arriving. It was the sound of consequences.

I stood by the door.

My hand hovered over the knob.

*Don't open it,* the voice whispered. *If you open it, the genre changes. If you open it, you stop being Wade Wilson, Happy Boyfriend, and you start being something else. Something louder.*

I looked back at Vanessa. She was watching me. Her eyes were wide, knowing. She looked like a woman watching her husband leave for war, except the war was just on the other side of a piece of painted particleboard.

"I'll get it," I said.

I turned the lock. *Click.*

I opened the door.

The hallway should have smelled like old carpet and Mrs. Higgins' cat. Instead, it smelled of ozone. It smelled of static electricity and starched uniforms.

Standing there were four men.

They looked like what would happen if a riot cop had a baby with a 1970s sci-fi movie prop. Black armor. Orange visors. Glowing batons that hummed with a menacing, violet light.

"Wade Wilson," the lead agent said.

His voice was filtered, tinny. It didn't sound human. It sounded like authority given form.

"Not interested," I said, trying to close the door. "We have a strict 'No Fascist Solicitors' policy. It's in the lease."

He blocked the door with his baton.

*Thud.*

The wood groaned.

"This isn't a solicitation," the agent said. "You are coming with us."

"Look, fellas," I said, leaning against the doorframe. "I'm having a moment here. A birthday moment. There is cake. There is tequila. There is a very tall Russian man who is currently weeping over a cat picture. Read the room."

The agent didn't read the room. He reached for his belt.

*Reflexes.*

My body knew what to do before my brain did. My weight shifted. My hand twitched toward a gun that wasn't there.

*Don't fight,* I told myself. *Not here. Not in front of Ness.*

"You are coming with us," the agent repeated.

They grabbed me.

It wasn't a gentle escort. It was a snatch. Hands clamped onto my arms like vices. They yanked me into the hallway.

"Hey!" I yelled. "Watch the suit! It's a rental!" (It wasn't. It was a Men's Wearhouse special, buy one get one free).

The door slammed shut behind me.

I was alone with them.

"Okay," I said. "You want to dance? Let's dance. But I lead."

I tried to headbutt the lead agent.

*BAD IDEA.*

His helmet was hard. My forehead was soft.

*CRACK.*

Stars exploded in my vision. Not metaphorically. Literal, cartoon stars.

"Target secures," the agent said.

They ripped my clothes off.

"Whoa!" I screamed. "Buy a guy a drink first! At least take me to dinner! I like Italian! I like carbs!"

My beige suit—the armor of Wade Wilson—was torn away. Buttons popped and pinged off the walls. My shirt was shredded.

I was naked.

Well, mostly naked. I was wearing my boxers. They had little unicorns on them.

"Is this standard protocol?" I asked, shivering. The hallway was freezing. "Because if this is a strip search, you're going to be disappointed. I'm a grower, not a shower."

They didn't laugh. They shoved me into a jumpsuit.

It was orange. It smelled of industrial detergent and despair. It was the color of a traffic cone that had given up on life.

"Collar," the agent said.

They clamped something around my neck.

*Click.*

Suddenly, the world felt... heavy.

My healing factor? Gone. The constant, buzzing itch of cellular repair vanished, leaving behind a cold, empty silence in my veins. My cancer? Back. I could feel it waking up, stretching its tumors.

"That's not cool," I wheezed. "That's my emotional support mutation."

The lead agent pressed a button on a device.

A door opened.

Not a door in the wall. A door in the air. A rectangle of swirling orange light.

"Move," he said.

He shoved me.

I stumbled forward. Into the light. Into the impossible.

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE TVA)**
**LOCATION: HOLDING CELL 4**

*Side Story A: The Junior Agent*

**POV: AGENT MILLER**

Miller stood by the door of the holding cell. He tried not to fidget.

He was new. This was his first week out of the Academy. He had spent three years learning the Sacred Timeline, memorizing the temporal loom schematics, and practicing his baton swings.

He was ready. He was a Minuteman.

But the prisoner...

Subject 89-7. Wade Wilson.

The file said "Unpredictable." The file said "Meta-Narrative Hazard."

Miller looked at the prisoner.

Wilson was sitting on the bench, arguing with the wall.

"The lighting in here is terrible," Wilson said to the empty air. "It's very 'Soviet chic'. Can we get a ring light? I look like a thumb that's been in a bath too long."

Miller gripped his baton tighter. *Do not engage. Do not make eye contact.*

"Hey," Wilson said.

He turned.

Miller stared straight ahead. He focused on a rivet in the opposite wall.

"You," Wilson said. "The one with the 'I just graduated' posture. You're new, aren't you?"

Miller didn't blink.

"I can tell," Wilson continued. His voice dropped. It wasn't the manic clown voice anymore. It was lower. Sharper. "Your uniform is too clean. No scuffs. No blood. And you're holding that baton like it's a microphone. You nervous, kid?"

Miller felt a bead of sweat trickle down his back. *How does he know?*

"It's okay to be nervous," Wilson whispered. "You're in a room with a monster. A killer. A guy who killed the entire Marvel Universe in a comic book once. Although that was non-canon."

Miller swallowed.

"But here's the secret," Wilson said. He stood up. He walked to the glass. "You're not the main character, Miller."

Miller froze. *He knows my name. I'm not wearing a nametag.*

"You're an extra," Wilson said. His eyes—blue, piercing, terrifying—bored into Miller's soul. "You're a background asset. You have no backstory. You have no lines. You're just here to get hit. You're here to demonstrate that I'm dangerous."

Miller took a step back.

"Don't worry," Wilson smiled. It was a gruesome expression on his scarred face. "I'll make it quick. I'll make it memorable. You'll be the guy who got knocked out by Deadpool in the first act. That's a legacy, Miller. That's fame."

Miller raised his baton. "Step back!"

"Action!" Wilson yelled.

He moved.

He didn't just move. He *glitched*. One second he was by the bench, the next he was at the door.

He slammed his head into the glass.

*THUD.*

"Let me out!" Wilson screamed. "I have a date! I have a life! I have a plot to advance!"

Miller triggered the shock collar.

*ZZZZZT.*

Wilson convulsed. He dropped to his knees.

But he was laughing.

"More!" Wilson yelled. "Harder, daddy! Give me the juice!"

Miller looked at the other agents. They looked bored. They had seen this before.

But Miller... Miller was terrified.

Because he realized the prisoner was right.

He wasn't a guard. He was a prop. And the scene was just beginning.

**POV: WADE WILSON**

The shock collar tasted like licking a battery while chewing tin foil.

*Spicy.*

I lay on the floor of the cell. The ceiling was spinning.

"Okay," I muttered. "So. We're doing the 'Captured by the Shadowy Organization' trope. Classic. Very *Winter Soldier*. Very *Bourne Identity*."

I stood up. My knees shook.

"I demand a lawyer!" I shouted. "I demand a phone call! I demand to speak to the showrunner!"

The door hissed open.

A man walked in.

He wasn't in armor. He was in a suit. A nice suit.

He looked like a British villain from a movie where the dog dies.

"Mr. Wilson," he said.

"Mr. Fancy Pants," I retorted.

"I'm Mr. Paradox," he said. "Please. Come with me. We have much to discuss. And try not to bleed on the carpet. It's imported."

"From where?" I asked. "The 1970s?"

He smiled. It was a shark's smile.

"From a timeline where carpet is the dominant life form," he said.

I paused.

"Okay," I said. "That's actually funny. I hate you already."
