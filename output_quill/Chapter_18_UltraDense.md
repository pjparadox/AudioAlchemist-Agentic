# CHAPTER 18: THE MACHINE (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID SECTOR)**
**LOCATION: TVA OUTPOST**

The outpost didn't belong here.

In the Void, everything was organic in its decay. Rust was a fungus; rot was a smell; steel was just bones that hadn't turned to dust yet.

But the TVA outpost was a blister of sterility.

It sat on a plateau of obsidian—sand that had been fused into glass by immense heat. It was white plastic and orange glass, slammed into the grey bedrock of the wasteland like a syringe into a bruise.

We crouched on a ridge, looking down.

"It's quiet," Logan said.

"It's too quiet," I added. "It's 'we blew the budget on the final fight so we can't afford extras' quiet. It's 'level design limitation' quiet."

The wind shifted.

It hit me then. The smell.

It wasn't just ozone. It was the smell of *un-happening*.

Have you ever smelled a lightning strike? It tastes like copper and burnt hair. It makes the fillings in your teeth ache. Now imagine that lightning strike is hitting history itself.

It smelled like the library of Alexandria burning.
It smelled like your first pet dying.
It smelled like every mistake you ever made, every "what if," every "I should have," cooked down into a gas and pumped into the air.

"The Time Ripper," I whispered.

"What?" Logan asked. He sniffed the air, his nose wrinkling.

"That hum. Can you feel it?"

Logan nodded. He touched his chest, right over his heart.

"It feels like..." he struggled for the word. "Like I'm being erased. Like I'm fading."

"It's vibrating the atoms," I said. "It's unzipping the DNA of the universe. It's trying to turn 'is' into 'was'."

We moved closer.

The ground around the outpost was glassed. Smooth. Black. It was slippery. We walked on our own reflections—two distorted monsters approaching the castle.

"How do we get in?" Logan asked.

"We knock," I said. "And by knock, I mean we drive a Honda Odyssey through the front door."

"We don't have the Odyssey anymore."

"Right. We wrecked it. RIP, Goldie. Okay. Plan B. We use the element of surprise."

"Which is?"

"We walk in the front door and look like we belong. Act like you own the place. Walk with the confidence of a mediocre white man."

"You're wearing a red spandex suit covered in blood and dog hair," Logan noted. "You don't belong anywhere."

"Ouch. Fair. But hurtful."

Suddenly, the ground shook.

*THRUM.*

A beam of purple light shot up from the center of the outpost. It pierced the clouds.

Alioth roared.

It wasn't the roar of a predator. It was the roar of pain. The beam was hurting it. Feeding it.

"They're turning it on," Logan said.

"Paradox is accelerating the timeline," I realized. "He's not waiting for the Anchor to decay. He's pulling the plug. He's flushing the toilet while we're still in the bowl."

I drew my katanas.

The metal felt cold in my hands. Reassuring.

"Okay, peanut," I said. "No more jokes. No more bits. No more fourth wall breaks."

I looked at the camera. (Okay, one more).

"We stop this. Or we die."

Logan popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

The sound was sharp. Clear. It cut through the hum of the machine.

"Let's go," he said.

We ran.

Our boots hammered on the glass ground.

*Thud. Thud. Thud.*

We were two glitches in the system, running toward the delete key.
