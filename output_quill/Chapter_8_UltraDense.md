# CHAPTER 8: A MAN-SHAPED SCAR (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE HELICARRIER BAR**

We walked for an hour. Or a day. Time didn't work here. The sun (if it was a sun) didn't move. It just hung in the sky, a pale, sickly eye watching us decay.

We found shelter in the fuselage of a crashed Helicarrier.

It was massive. A metal whale beached on the sands of eternity. The S.H.I.E.L.D. logo was faded, scratched out by claws bigger than anything I wanted to think about.

Inside, someone had built a bar.

Because of course they had. Even at the end of the world, people need to forget.

It was makeshift. Tables made of cable spools. Stools made of buckets. The bar itself was a piece of wing flap.

The air was heavy. It smelled of:
*   **Kerosene:** The fuel that still leaked from the engines.
*   **Unwashed Bodies:** Decades of sweat and fear.
*   **Cheap Alcohol:** The kind that cleans wounds and erases memories.

The bartender was a variant. A guy with green skin and three eyes. He didn't ask for ID. He just pointed to a bottle.

Logan sat down. He slumped.

The table was sticky. It was coated in a resin of spilled drinks and grime.

"Two," Logan rasped. "Leave the bottle."

The bartender slid a bottle of clear liquid toward us. It had no label. Just a skull drawn in marker.

Logan uncorked it with his teeth. He spat the cork onto the floor.

He drank.

He didn't sip. He swallowed half the bottle in one go. His throat worked. His eyes watered.

"Jesus," I said. "Pace yourself, peanut. That stuff looks like it could strip paint off a battleship."

Logan slammed the bottle down.

*Bang.*

The sound echoed in the hollow metal chamber.

"Shut up," he said.

"We need a plan," I said. "We need to find a way back. Paradox said—"

"I don't care what he said," Logan interrupted. "I'm not going back."

"What?"

"I'm done." He looked at his hands.

His hands were scarred. Not the healing factor scars. These were deeper. Lines of guilt etched into the skin. His knuckles were raw. His fingernails were dirty with the soil of a thousand graves.

"I failed," Logan whispered.

The bar went quiet. Even the three-eyed bartender stopped polishing a glass.

"My world," Logan said. "The X-Men. The humans. They're dead."

"I know," I said. "I saw the Wikipedia page. 'The Westchester Incident'. Sad stuff. Very *Schindler's List* meets *X-Men*."

"You don't know," Logan snapped. "You don't know anything."

He leaned forward.

The smell coming off him was intense. It wasn't just the whiskey. It was the smell of old blood. Iron. Copper. It was the smell of a slaughterhouse that had been closed but never cleaned.

"I wasn't there," Logan whispered. "They called me. Scott. Jean. They called for help. And I was in a bar. I was drunk. I ignored the call."

He gripped the bottle. The glass creaked under the pressure of his Adamantium skeleton.

"When I got back... it was over. The mansion was burning. The bodies..."

He shuddered. A tremor ran through his frame.

"I buried them," he said. "All of them. And then I walked away. And I kept walking. And I kept drinking. Until I ended up here."

He looked at me.

His eyes were yellow pools of self-loathing.

"So don't tell me I'm a hero," he spat. "I'm not a hero. I'm the reason they're dead. I'm a man-shaped scar."

I stared at him.

*Okay. Heavy. This is... real. This isn't a bit. This isn't a setup for a punchline.*

*What do I do? Make a joke? Deflect?*

*No. Truth.*

"I get it," I said softly.

"You don't—"

"I do," I interrupted. "I really do. I wasn't there for Vanessa. I wasn't there when the bullet hit her. I could have stopped it. I didn't."

I touched my mask. The fabric felt rough under my fingers.

"We carry it," I said. "The guilt. It's part of the suit. It's the lining. It keeps us warm."

Logan looked at me. For a second, just a second, the hate in his eyes softened. He saw something recognizable. A fellow monster.

Then he blinked. The wall came back up.

"Go to hell," he said.

He stood up. The stool scraped against the metal floor. *SCREEE.*

"I'm leaving."

"Leaving where?" I asked. "To the bathroom? Because it's a bucket in the corner. I checked. It's full."

"Anywhere," Logan said. "Anywhere you aren't."

He turned to leave.

"No," I said.

I stood up. I blocked his path.

"Get out of my way, Wade."

"No. I need you. My world needs you. And frankly? You need this. You need redemption."

"I don't want redemption," Logan roared. Spittle flew from his mouth. "I want a drink!"

He shoved me.

I flew back, crashing into the bar. Bottles shattered. Glass rained down.

"Okay," I said, picking myself up. "Physical comedy. I can do that."

I drew my katanas. *SHING.*

"If you want to leave," I said, "you have to go through me. And I am very annoying to go through."

Logan popped his claws. *SNIKT.*

The sound was sharp. Final.

"Fine," he said.
