# CHAPTER 4: THE AVENGER PITCH

**TIMELINE: EARTH-616 (THE SACRED TIMELINE)**
**DATE: MARCH 14, 2018**

The elevator ride was quiet.

Not the good kind of quiet. Not the "we just saved the world and are enjoying the silence" quiet. It was the "I am about to ask the coolest kid in school to prom and I am wearing a diaper" kind of quiet.

I checked my watch. It was a Hello Kitty watch. It ticked loudly. *Tick. Tock. Tick. Tock.*

*Okay, Wade. Focus. You are Wade Wilson. You are a special forces operative. You have killed dictators with a Zamboni. You can do this.*

I adjusted my tie. I wasn't wearing the suit. I was wearing *the* suit. No, not the red one. A business suit. Charcoal. Sharp. I looked like I was about to defend a mob boss in court.

The doors opened.

Stark Tower. Or Avengers Tower. Or whatever they call it this week.

The lobby was sleek. Glass. Chrome. The smell of money and vibranium. It smelled like Elon Musk's cologne, if Elon Musk showered.

I walked to the reception desk.

"Wade Wilson," I said to the receptionist. "Here to see Happy Hogan."

The receptionist looked at me. She didn't blink. "Do you have an appointment?"

"I have a destiny," I said. "And a referral. From Dopinder. He drives a taxi. He knows people."

She sighed. "Floor 42."

I got back in the elevator.

*Floor 42. The answer to life, the universe, and everything. That's a good sign. Unless it's a Douglas Adams reference, in which case the planet is about to be demolished to make way for a hyperspace bypass.*

The doors opened again.

And there he was.

Happy Hogan. Iron Man's best friend. The guy who drove the car in the movies.

He was sitting behind a desk, looking busy. He was moving papers around. I suspect they were blank.

"Mr. Hogan!" I marched in. "Happy! Can I call you Happy? I feel like we're vibing."

Happy looked up. He looked... annoyed. He looked like a man who had dealt with too many Skrulls before lunch.

"Mr. Wilson," Happy said. "You're late."

"I was saving a cat," I lied. "From a tree. It was a very tall tree. And a very fat cat. It had diabetes. I had to administer insulin."

"Sit down."

I sat. The chair was leather. Expensive leather. It groaned under my weight.

"So," Happy said, looking at my resume. It was written in crayon. "You want to join the Avengers."

"I don't just *want* to join," I said, leaning forward. "I *need* to join. I need to matter, Happy. I've done the solo thing. I've done the X-Men trainee thing—which, by the way, pays in exposure, which doesn't cover rent. I'm ready for the big leagues."

Happy stared at me.

"You're a mercenary," Happy said. "You kill people for money."

"I *un-alive* bad guys for *justice*," I corrected. "And money. But mostly justice. Look, I have skills. I heal. I'm great with swords. I have a very high pain tolerance. And I'm funny. Every team need a funny guy. You have Tony, but he's... sarcastic. I'm *wacky*."

"We don't need wacky," Happy said.

"What do you need?" I asked. Desperation crept into my voice. "I can be serious. Look at this face."

I took off my sunglasses.

Happy winced.

"Okay," I said. "Bad example. But inside? Inside, I am Captain America. I am moral. I am good. I salute the flag. I recycle."

*Please,* I thought. *Please say yes. I need this. If I'm an Avenger, I matter. If I matter, I'm worthy of Vanessa.*

Happy sighed. He closed the folder.

"No," Happy said.

The word hung in the air.

"No?" I repeated.

"No," Happy said. "You're not Avengers material, Wade. You're... volatile. You're a liability. We save lives. You take them."

"I can change!" I stood up. "I can be non-lethal! I'll use beanbag rounds! I'll use stern language! I'll use interpretive dance!"

"Go home, Wade," Happy said. "Go back to your life. Be a hero to the people who love you. That's enough."

*That's enough.*

The words hit me like a physical blow.

*It's not enough. Being Wade isn't enough.*

I walked out.

I took the elevator down.

I didn't cry. I didn't rage. I just stood there, watching the numbers go down. 42. 41. 40.

*Going down,* the voice in my head whispered. *All the way to the bottom.*

# CHAPTER 5: SNATCH-AND-BAG

**TIMELINE: EARTH-10005 (PRESENT DAY)**

I opened the door to the apartment.

The party was still going. But the mood had shifted. The cake was gone. The tequila was low.

I had just broken up with Vanessa. Well, technically, we just had a "moment." But it felt like a breakup. It felt like the end of the world.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

"I'll get it," I said.

I opened the door.

TVA agents. Armored. Glowing sticks. Orange vibe.

"Wade Wilson," the lead agent said.

"Not interested," I said, trying to close the door.

He blocked it with a baton.

"This isn't a solicitation," he said. "You are coming with us."

They grabbed me.

"Hey!" I yelled. "Watch the suit! It's a rental!" (It wasn't).

They dragged me into the hallway.

They ripped my clothes off.

"Whoa! Buy a guy a drink first! At least take me to dinner! I like Italian!"

They put me in a TVA prisoner jumper. It smelled of starch and oppression.

Then they shoved me through a portal.

*Side Story A: The Junior Agent*

**POV: AGENT MILLER**

Miller stood by the door, watching the prisoner.

The prisoner—Subject 89-7—was currently arguing with the wall.

"The lighting in here is terrible," the prisoner said to the empty air. "It's very 'Soviet chic'. Can we get a ring light?"

Miller didn't answer. He had been warned.

*Briefing Officer:* "Do not engage. He's a Class-4 Meta-Narrator. He creates false realities through dialogue. He will make you doubt your own existence."

Miller adjusted his grip on his baton. He was new. This was his first week. He just wanted to do a good job. He wanted to protect the Timeline. He had a family back in Sector 4. A wife. Two kids.

"Hey," the prisoner said. He turned to look at Miller.

The prisoner's face was... difficult to look at. Scarred. Melted. But his eyes were blue and piercing. They seemed to see right through Miller's helmet.

"You're new, aren't you?" the prisoner asked.

Miller stared straight ahead.

"I can tell," the prisoner said. "Your uniform is too clean. And you're holding that baton like it's a microphone. You nervous, kid?"

Miller didn't blink.

"It's okay to be nervous," the prisoner whispered. "You're just an extra. A background character. You have no lines. You're just here to get hit."

Miller felt a chill.

"Don't worry," the prisoner smiled. "I'll make it quick. I'll make it memorable. You'll be the guy who got knocked out by Deadpool. That's a legacy, Miller. That's fame."

Miller swallowed. *How does he know my name? I'm not wearing a nametag.*

"I read the script," the prisoner whispered.

Miller took a step back.

Suddenly, the prisoner moved.

He headbutted the lead agent. He grabbed a TemPad.

Miller raised his baton.

"Action!" the prisoner yelled.

# CHAPTER 6: THE SACRED TIMELINE

**POV: MR. PARADOX**

Paradox adjusted his cufflink.

He watched the monitor. Wilson was in the hallway, dismantling his security team with a level of chaotic efficiency that was frankly insulting.

*Disgusting,* Paradox thought. *He fights like a child throwing a tantrum. No elegance. No precision. Just noise.*

"Sir," his assistant, B-15 (no, not that one, a different one, let's call him Kevin), spoke up. "Should we deploy the heavy containment?"

"No," Paradox said. "Let him come."

"But sir... he's violent."

"He's desperate," Paradox corrected. "Violence is just his language. I speak fluent desperation."

*Side Story C: The Efficiency of Mercy*

Paradox turned away from the screen. He looked at the Time Ripper.

It hummed in the center of the room. A beautiful machine. Elegant. Final.

People called him a monster. They called him a bureaucrat. But they didn't understand.

The Timeline was sick. It was overgrown. Billions of branches, all withering, all suffering. He had seen timelines where humanity was enslaved by sentient fungi. Timelines where the sun exploded in 1999. Timelines where *Cats* won Best Picture.

He remembered the first time he pruned a timeline. It was a mercy killing. A universe where the Avengers lost in 2012. Earth was a slave colony. Suffering was absolute.

He erased it.

And suddenly... silence. Peace. No more pain.

*Mercy is inefficient,* he told himself. *Compassion is slow. The Time Ripper is fast. It is clean. It is the ultimate kindness.*

He was saving them. He was saving them from the slow, agonizing death of an Anchor-less existence.

"Mr. Wilson is here," Kevin announced.

The door flew open.

Wade Wilson stood there. He was wearing the suit. The red one. It was filthy.

"Knock knock!" Wilson yelled.

Paradox smiled. He put on his 'Helpful Administrator' face.

"Mr. Wilson," Paradox said. "So glad you could join us. Please. Ignore the blood on the carpet. It's vintage."

"You want to save my world?" Wilson asked. He was panting. "Prove it."

"I don't want to save your world, Wade," Paradox said softly. "I want to save *you*."

He walked around the desk.

"Your world is a cancer," Paradox said. "It is stage four. I am the surgeon. And you... you are the healthy cell I am transplanting."

Wilson stared at him.

"You're not a surgeon," Wilson said. "You're a butcher."

"Semantics," Paradox waved a hand. "Now. Let's talk about the Avengers."
