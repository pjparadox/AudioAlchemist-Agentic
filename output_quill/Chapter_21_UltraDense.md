# CHAPTER 21: THE CIRCUIT (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (TIME RIPPER CHAMBER)**
**LOCATION: THE CORE**

I was in the chamber. The energy was tearing me apart.

It wasn't like fire. Fire burns from the outside in. Fire is honest.
This was deletion.
It started in my marrow. It felt like my bones were vibrating at a frequency that carbon wasn't meant to handle.

My skin was peeling off in strips.
*Slough.*
It came away like wet paper. I saw my own musculature exposed to the blinding purple light. The fibers were cooking, turning grey, then black, then dust.

I looked at Logan.

He was next to me.

He was burning too. His yellow suit—the suit he hated, the suit he wore for Scott—was vaporizing.
His skin was gone.
He was a skeleton dipped in lava. The Adamantium glowed cherry-red. It hissed as his healing factor tried, desperately, to knit flesh over the superheated metal.

"WADE!" he screamed.

His voice was a raw, wet tear in the soundscape. His vocal cords were regenerating and burning simultaneously.

We were holding the cables.
They were thick, pulsing veins of power. We were the circuit. We were the bridge between the machine and the ground.

The pain was beyond pain. It was absolute. It was a white noise that drowned out every thought, every joke, every pop culture reference.

"Let go!" Cassandra shrieked. She was trapped in the beam, disintegrating. Her face was a mask of pure terror. "You'll die! You'll both die!"

"That's the plan!" I yelled. My jaw was melting. I felt my teeth loosen in their sockets. "The plan is... *stupid*!"

I looked at Logan.

He looked at me.

His eyes—the only part of him that wasn't burning, preserved by some miracle of biology or plot armor—were clear. They weren't yellow. They were blue. A deep, soulful blue that had seen too much.

He reached out.

His hand was a skeletal claw of red-hot metal and raw tendon.

He grabbed my hand.

*SNAP.*

The connection locked into place.

Two anchors. One circuit.

The energy surged. It doubled. It tripled.

It wasn't just pain anymore. It was connection. I felt his life.
I felt the weight of his skeleton.
I felt the cold of the Canadian wilderness.
I felt the heat of Jean Grey's fire.
I felt the guilt. The shame. The love.

And he felt me.
He felt the cancer. The fear. The desperate need to be loved. The clown makeup I wore over my soul.

"Maximum Effort?" he asked.

It was my line. He stole my line. The magnificent, hairy bastard.

I laughed. My lips cracked and fell off.

"Maximum Effort."

We squeezed the cables. We squeezed each other's hands.

*BOOM.*

The world went white.

It wasn't a color. It was the absence of everything.

And then... silence.

***

**THE AFTERMATH**

I woke up.

I was lying on something hard. Cold.

I opened my eyes.

The ceiling was white. Clean.

"Am I dead?" I croaked.

"No," a voice said.

I sat up.

Logan was sitting in a chair next to the bed. He was wearing civilian clothes. Jeans. A flannel shirt. He looked... normal.

"You're alive," he said.

"We're alive," I corrected. "We survived the murder-suicide pact."

He smirked.

"Yeah. We did."

I looked at my hands. They were pink. New skin. Smooth.

"Cassandra?" I asked.

"Gone," Logan said. "Dust."

"Paradox?"

"In custody. B-15 took him. He's going to be auditing timelines for the rest of eternity."

I leaned back.

"We did it," I whispered. "We saved the world."

"Yeah," Logan said. "We did."

He stood up.

"Hungry?" he asked.

"Starving," I said. "I could eat a horse. Or a unicorn. Or a chimichanga."

"I know a place," he said.

"Shawarma?"

"Shawarma."
