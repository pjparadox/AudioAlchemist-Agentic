# CHAPTER 10: 3:00 AM

The city at 3:00 AM was not asleep; it was simply holding its breath.
Erin Bruner lay in her bed, the sheets tangled around her legs. She was awake. She had been awake since 2:59.
She stared at the digital clock. The red numbers burned in the darkness.
*3:00.*
It stayed 3:00 for a long time. Too long.
Erin blinked. The colon between the numbers wasn't blinking. Time had stopped.

She sat up. The air in her bedroom was cold. Not the cool of air conditioning, but the deep, bone-aching chill of a meat locker.
She reached for her water glass. Her hand shook.
*Stop it,* she told herself. *You're a partner at a major law firm. You don't get scared of the dark.*
But she was scared.
Because the smell was back.
Sulfur.
And something else. Something sweet. Rotting flowers.

*Scritch.*
It came from the living room.
Erin grabbed the heavy brass lamp from her nightstand. She didn't have a gun. She didn't believe in them. Right now, she regretted that political stance.
She walked into the hallway. The floorboards, usually creaky, were silent. As if the wood itself was afraid to make a sound.

The living room was bathed in the orange glow of the streetlights.
Jasper, her cat, was standing on the back of the sofa.
He was hissing.
His back was arched so high it looked like his spine might snap. His tail was a bottle brush of fear.
He wasn't looking at the door. He wasn't looking at the window.
He was looking at the empty armchair in the corner.
The leather chair Erin's father had given her.

Erin looked at the chair.
It was empty.
But the leather... the seat cushion was depressed.
Deeply depressed.
As if a heavy weight was sitting there.
Erin froze. Her breath caught in her throat.
"Who's there?" she whispered.
The depression in the leather shifted.
The leather creaked. *Eeeeee.*
The sound of someone shifting their weight. Leaning forward.

Erin backed away. "I'm calling the police."
The depression began to rise.
Slowly.
As if the invisible occupant was standing up.
Erin didn't wait. She turned and ran back to her bedroom, slamming the door and locking it. She dragged the dresser in front of the door.
She sat on the bed, clutching the lamp, staring at the handle.
*3:01.*
The clock blinked. Time started again.
But the smell of rotting flowers lingered until dawn.

# CHAPTER 11: THE PROSECUTION

Ethan Thomas did not sleep in on Sundays. He was up at 6:00, kneeling by his bed.
"Lord," he prayed, his large hands clasped together. "Grant me the sword of truth. Let me cut through the lies of the Enemy."
Ethan believed in the Enemy. He didn't see the Devil as a metaphor, or a psychological archetype. He saw him as a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour.
And right now, the lion was hiding behind a collar.

He stood up, dressing in his Sunday best. A dark blue suit. Conservative. Solid. Like his faith.
He drove to the First Methodist Church on 4th Street. He sat in the front pew.
During the sermon, the pastor spoke about forgiveness.
Ethan frowned.
Forgiveness was for the repentant.
Richard Moore was not repentant. He was proud. He was dangerous.

After the service, Ethan walked to the DA's office. It was empty on a Sunday.
He went to his desk and opened the file.
*The People vs. Richard Moore.*
He looked at the autopsy photos of Emily Rose.
He felt a surge of righteous anger.
This wasn't just negligence. This was ritual sacrifice.
He picked up his pen.
He began to write his opening statement.
"We are not here to judge a man's faith," he wrote. "We are here to judge his actions."
He crossed it out.
"We are here," he wrote, pressing the pen so hard it tore the paper, "to name the darkness."

# CHAPTER 12: THE STRATEGY

"We need a narrative," Karl said.
The war room at the firm was littered with coffee cups and takeout boxes. It was Monday morning. The trial started in two days.
Erin stood at the whiteboard. She had written two words:
*Possession* vs. *Psychosis*.

"Thomas is going to go hard on the science," Erin said. "He has Dr. Vogel. He has the autopsy. He has the weight—68 pounds. He's going to say Moore starved her."
"So we attack the science," Karl said.
"We can't," Erin said. "The science is solid. She *did* starve. She *did* have epilepsy."

"Then what's the play?"
Erin uncapped a red marker. She drew a circle around *Possession*.
"We don't attack the science," she said. "We expand the context."
"Meaning?"
"We argue that science is incomplete. We put the supernatural on trial. But not as 'magic'. As an alternative diagnosis."

Karl frowned. "You want to argue that demons are a medical condition?"
"I want to argue that *Emily believed* they were. And because she believed it, the medical treatment wasn't working. In fact, it was making her worse. Moore didn't stop the treatment because he was a fanatic. He stopped it because it was killing her."

"It's risky," Karl said. "You're asking a jury to validate a hallucination."
"I'm asking them to validate *her* reality," Erin said. "If the jury believes that *Moore believed* he was saving her soul... that's not homicide. That's a mistake. A tragedy. But not a crime."
Karl nodded slowly. "Reasonable doubt."
"Exactly. We don't have to prove God exists. We just have to prove that the State can't prove He doesn't."

# CHAPTER 13: THE RAIN

The storm arrived on Tuesday.
It didn't start as a drizzle. It started as a deluge. A wall of water that slammed into the city, turning the streets into rivers of oil and trash.
Erin watched it from her office window.
The sky was a bruised purple. Lightning forked down, hitting the radio tower on top of the courthouse.
*Crack-BOOM.*
The building shook.

She felt it then. The same feeling she had in the car. The pressure.
It was as if the city itself was under siege.
The phone rang.
It was Moore. calling from the jail.
"Ms. Bruner," he said. His voice was calm. Too calm.
"Father? Everything okay?"
"The rain," he said. "Do you see it?"
"I see it. It's quite a storm."
"It's not a storm," Moore said. "It's an arrival."

Erin felt a chill on her neck.
"What do you mean?"
"They know the trial is starting. They're gathering, Erin. The Prince of the Air. He's coming to watch."
The line crackled with static. A harsh, hissing sound.
*"...watch... you..."*
"Father?" Erin asked. "Was that you?"
Silence.
Then the line went dead.

Erin hung up the phone.
She looked back at the window.
The rain lashed against the glass, sounding like a thousand fingernails tapping for entry.
*Scritch. Scritch. Scritch.*
She grabbed her briefcase.
She walked out of the office, turning off the lights.
But even in the dark, she could feel eyes on her back.
The trial had begun.
And the defendant wasn't the only one on trial.
