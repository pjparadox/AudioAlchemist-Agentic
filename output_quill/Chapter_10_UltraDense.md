# CHAPTER 10: THE MIND TOUCH (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE BORDERLANDS)**
**LOCATION: THE GIANT-MAN RIB CAGE**
**POV: CASSANDRA NOVA**

*Side Story F: The Tasting*

The mind is not a book.

People say "read my mind," but they are wrong. They think thoughts are sentences written on a page. They think memories are movies playing on a screen.

They are quaint.

A mind is a meal.

Cassandra Nova sat on her throne. It was built from the ossified remains of a Celestial's thumb. Smooth, white bone, cool to the touch. It hummed with a low, necrotic frequency that tickled the base of her spine.

She ran a finger along the armrest. It felt like polished ivory.

"Bring them," she whispered.

Her voice didn't need to be loud. It carried on the psychic wind. It slid into the ears of her followers like warm oil.

She closed her eyes and extended her senses.

The Void was a buffet.

Most of it was fast food.

There were the scavengers. Their minds were simple, greasy things.
*Hunger. Fear. Greed. Lust.*
They tasted like stale popcorn. Like cheap burgers left under a heat lamp for too long. Salty. Unsatisfying.

Then there was Alioth. The cloud beast.
His mind was a storm. A hurricane of hunger. He tasted of ozone and static. Too spicy. Too much voltage. To touch his mind was to lick a power line.

But the new arrivals...

She licked her lips. Her tongue was dry, rasping over her teeth.

One flavor was chaotic.

*Wade Wilson.*

She tasted him on the wind. It was a sensory assault.
His mind was a funhouse mirror maze. Thoughts bounced and refracted. He knew he was in a story. He knew *she* was in a story.
It tasted of artificial cherry flavoring. It tasted of Pop Rocks exploding on the tongue. It tasted of battery acid and old comic book paper.
It was sour. Fizzy. Annoying.

*Deliciously annoying,* she thought. *A palate cleanser. A sorbet made of madness.*

But the other one.

*Logan.*

She inhaled deeply through her nose.

The scent of his mind hit her like a wave of dark water.

It was deep. Earthy. Bitter.

She pushed past the surface thoughts—the anger, the pain, the desire for whiskey. Those were just the garnish.

She went deeper.

She tasted the Adamantium.
It was a cold, metallic tang that coated the roof of her mouth. It tasted of violation. Of being cut open and filled with something that didn't belong. It tasted of surgical steel and green antiseptic.

She went deeper still.

She tasted the memories.

*1845.*
The taste of a sickbed. Fever sweat. The smell of old wool.
A scream.
Bone claws popping for the first time. The wet *shhhhk* of calcium tearing through skin.
The taste of a father's blood. Salty. Iron-rich. The shock of patricide.

*The Wars.*
She tasted the mud of the Somme. Mustard gas. Rotting feet.
She tasted the jungle rot of Vietnam. Napalm. The humidity of the Mekong Delta.
She tasted the ash of Nagasaki. The radiation that tingled on the tongue like spicy pepper.

*The Weapon X Program.*
The tank. The wires. The feeling of drowning in green fluid. The sensation of having your bones drilled into. The vibration of the drill bit against the skull.

And then... the fresh wound.

*Westchester.*

It tasted of ash. Of burning wood. Of betrayal.
It tasted of a phone ringing in an empty hallway.
It tasted of the silence after the screaming stopped.

Cassandra shuddered. A wave of pleasure rolled through her, starting at her toes and curling up her spine.

"Oh," she whispered. "He is exquisitely broken."

She opened her eyes. They were blue. Pale. Empty.

Her lieutenants—Pyro, Sabretooth, Toad—were watching her. They looked nervous. They knew that when she was hungry, anyone could be on the menu.

Pyro shifted his weight. "Mistress?"

"The Wolverine," Cassandra said. Her voice was thick with desire. "I want him alive. I want to peel him apart, layer by layer. I want to see what's at the center of all that pain."

She looked at Pyro.

"Burn the red one," she said. "He tastes like cheap plastic. But bring me the animal."

She leaned back in her bone chair.

She could still taste the tragedy on her tongue. It was like a fine vintage. A Cabernet of sorrow.

"It's dinner time," she said.
