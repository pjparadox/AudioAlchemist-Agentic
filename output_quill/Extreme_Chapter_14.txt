# CHAPTER 14: EMILY: THE SPIRAL

*Flashback. December 1976.*

The pills were orange.
Small, oval, innocent-looking things.
*Gambrolic. 200mg.*
Emily held the bottle in her hand. It rattled. *Ch-ch-ch.*
It sounded like a rattlesnake.

She was back in her dorm room. The university had let her return, "pending stabilization."
Stabilization.
That's what they called it.
Emily called it "The Fog."
She took the pill. She swallowed it dry. It tasted of chalk and chemical bitterness.
Within twenty minutes, the fog rolled in.
It wasn't a visual fog. It was a sensory one.
The world lost its sharp edges. Colors became muted, pastel washes of grey and beige. Sounds were muffled, as if she were underwater.
Alice's voice, usually sharp and funny, became a drone.
"You okay, Em? You're staring at the wall again."
"I'm fine," Emily said.
But her voice sounded wrong. Slow. Thick.
Her tongue felt huge in her mouth. A slab of meat she couldn't control.
*Slurring,* Dr. Vogel had said. *A temporary side effect.*

She tried to study. *Kant. The Critique of Pure Reason.*
The words swam on the page. They wouldn't hold still.
She looked at the clock.
*2:00 PM.*
Safe.
But was she?
The medicine stopped the convulsions. She didn't fall down. She didn't seize.
But it didn't stop *Them*.
In fact, it made it worse.
Before, when the seizures hit, she could fight. She could pray. She could scream.
Now, she was locked in.
The Gambrolic built a wall around her body, paralyzing her muscles, slowing her reflexes. But it left her mind wide open.
It was like being tied to a chair and gagged while a fire burned in the room.

She walked to the window.
The campus was covered in snow. White, clean, silent.
She saw a dog trotting across the quad. A golden retriever. Happy. Tail wagging.
It stopped.
It looked up at her window. Three stories up.
It froze.
Its tail went between its legs.
It bared its teeth.
It barked.
A vicious, snarling bark that echoed off the brick buildings.
Students stopped and looked. The dog was snapping at the air, backing away, terrified.
Emily stepped back from the window.
*They see me,* she thought. *Even the animals see it.*

She went to the mirror.
She looked at her face.
Her skin was grey. Her eyes were dull, glazed over by the drugs.
But behind the glaze...
She saw a flicker.
A shadow moving in the depth of her pupil.
*We like the pills,* the voice whispered.
It wasn't in her ear. It was in the marrow of her bones.
*They make you quiet. They make you still.*
*They make you a better vessel.*

Emily grabbed her rosary from the desk.
"Hail Mary..." she tried to say.
But her mouth wouldn't form the words. The muscles were too relaxed. Too sedated.
"Mmmm... mmmm..."
She tried to lift her arm to make the sign of the cross.
It felt like lead.
She dropped the rosary. It clattered to the floor.
*Look at you,* the voice sneered. *You can't even pray.*
*You are broken. You are meat.*
*And we are hungry.*

The room began to darken. Not the lights. The *air*.
The shadows in the corners lengthened, stretching out like fingers.
The smell returned.
Burning rubber. Old blood.
Emily tried to run.
But the Gambrolic held her fast. A chemical straitjacket.
She slumped onto the bed.
She couldn't move. She couldn't scream.
She could only watch as the closet door slowly, deliberately, began to open.
*Creak.*
And from the darkness, a hand reached out.
A long, grey hand.
Holding a cup.
*Drink,* the voice whispered. *Drink the darkness.*
Emily closed her eyes.
And the screaming began.
But this time, it was silent.
A scream trapped inside a drugged mind, echoing forever in the hollow halls of her skull.
