# CHAPTER 12: THE RUINS (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE 20th CENTURY FOX LOT**

We dropped the kid off at a resistance outpost a few miles down the road. (Yes, there's a resistance. It's mostly just people who haven't been eaten yet, hiding in a bunker made of vibranium scraps and hope).

We kept driving.

The landscape shifted. The organic rot of the settlement gave way to concrete skeletons. Rebar twisted like dead vines. The ground became harder, the sand giving way to cracked asphalt.

We stopped at a massive ruin half-buried in the sand.

I got out. The wind was howling here. It sounded hollow, like blowing across the top of a giant, empty bottle.

I wiped the dust off a stone block.

*20th CENTURY FOX.*

The monolith. The fanfare structure. The symbol of my corporate origin.

I sat on the giant 'X'.

It was cold. The stone was rough, pitted by the Void winds. It felt like a tombstone. A monument to a civilization that had been bought, sold, and erased.

"This is it," I said. My voice was quiet. "This is where I came from."

Logan stood next to me. He looked at the ruin. He chewed on a cigar stub he'd found in the glove box.

"It's just rocks," he said.

"It's not just rocks," I said. "It's history. It's *X-Men*. It's *Daredevil*. It's *Elektra*! Okay, maybe we don't talk about *Elektra*. But it was home."

I ran my hand over the stone. I could feel the fissures. The cracks where the timeline had split.

"It was messy," I said. "The timelines didn't make sense. The casting changed every three movies. We rebooted more often than a Windows 95 PC. But it was *ours*."

I looked at the 'F'. It was cracked down the middle.

"They bought it," I said. "Disney. They bought the whole thing. And then... they pruned it. They sent it all here. To the landfill."

I felt a genuine pang of grief. Not for a studio. But for the era. For the messy, weird, inconsistent movies that made me. For the time when superhero movies felt like they were held together by duct tape and passion, not algorithms.

"Everything ends," Logan said.

He sat down next to me. The stone groaned under his weight.

"Does it have to?" I asked. "Why does it have to end? Why can't we just... keep going?"

"Because nothing lasts," Logan said. "Not studios. Not heroes. Not us. We're just IP, Wade. We're content. And content has a shelf life."

He looked at the horizon. The purple sky was swirling. Alioth roared in the distance, a reminder of the ultimate copyright strike.

"I miss them," I whispered. "Vanessa. Blind Al. Even Weasel. I miss my world. I don't want it to be just... rocks in a desert. I don't want to be a piece of trivia on a wiki page."

"Then fight for it," Logan said.

"I am," I said. "But I'm tired, Wolvie. I'm so tired. Being the funny guy... it's exhausting. It's heavy. It's like wearing a suit made of lead."

He looked at me.

He put a hand on my shoulder. It was heavy. Warm. It was the first time he had touched me without trying to kill me.

"Me too, kid," he said. "Me too."

We sat there for a long time. Two relics of a dead universe, sitting on the tombstone of the company that created us.

The wind howled.

*Dunn dunn dunn dunn...*

It sounded like the opening fanfare, played on a broken trumpet by a ghost.

I pulled out a lighter. I offered it to Logan.

He leaned in. He lit the cigar. The smoke drifted up, curling around the broken letters.

It smelled of cheap tobacco and nostalgia.

"Let's go," Logan said. "We have a villain to kill."

"Yeah," I said. "Let's go make a sequel."
