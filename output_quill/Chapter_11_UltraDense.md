# CHAPTER 11: THE SETTLEMENT (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE RIB CAGE**

We parked the Honda Odyssey behind a dune made of compacted trash and despair.

"Stealth mode," I whispered.

Logan looked at the vehicle. It was gold. It was 2011. It was shaped like a loaf of bread that had given up on its dreams.

"We're in a gold minivan, Wade," he said. "In a grey desert. There is no stealth mode. There is only 'Target Rich Environment'."

"It's camouflage," I argued. "We blend in with the... majestic gold sands of the Void. Or maybe we look like a mirage. A mirage of affordable family transportation."

"The sand is grey," Logan noted. He popped the door open. It creaked—a rusty, mournful sound.

We stepped out.

The heat hit us first. It wasn't the clean heat of a desert. It was the stifling, humid heat of a landfill in July. It smelled of roasting plastic and ozone.

We walked toward the settlement.

It was built inside the ribcage of a Giant-Man variant.

The bones were massive. White arches that curved into the purple sky, forming a cathedral of calcium. They were pitted by the wind, scarred by battles fought by things bigger than us.

"That's big," I said. "That's a lot of milk."

Logan didn't smile. He was scanning the perimeter. His eyes were constantly moving, analyzing threats, calculating kill zones.

We moved into the camp.

The smell changed. It became organic.

*Sweat.* Thousands of gallons of it, soaked into rags and dirt.
*Urine.* The ammonia tang of fear.
*Meat.* Cooking meat. I didn't ask what kind. In the Void, you don't ask. You just eat or you don't.

The camp was a shantytown of scraps. Huts made of old billboards. Tents made of parachutes.

The people were worse.

Variants. Hundreds of them.

I saw a Gambit. He was sitting on a crate, dealing cards to a Blob who looked like he was melting.
I saw a Daredevil (the Ben Affleck one). He looked sad. He was trying to read a braille book that had been water-damaged.
I saw a Howard the Duck. He was roasting a rat on a stick. He looked me in the eye and took a bite. It was a power move.

"Keep your head down," Logan muttered.

He pulled his collar up. He hunched his shoulders. He was trying to make himself small. But you can't hide a Wolverine. He walked with a density that displaced the air around him. People moved out of his way instinctively. They smelled the predator on him.

We found the fuel depot.

It was a tanker truck—an old Roxxon model—guarded by a Toad variant.

Toad was sitting on a oil drum. He was eating sunflower seeds. He spat the shells onto the ground with a rhythmic *pffft*.

"We need gas," Logan said. His voice was a low rumble.

Toad looked up. He had yellow eyes and a smile that was too wide for his face.

"Trade?" Toad asked. His tongue flicked out, catching a fly. *Snap.*

"I have a kidney," I offered. "It grows back. It's a renewable resource. Farm to table."

Toad ignored me. He looked at Logan. He looked at the jacket.

"The jacket," Toad said. "Leather. Real leather."

Logan touched his jacket. It was battered. Stained. It was the only thing he owned that felt like him.

"No," Logan said.

"Then no gas."

I stepped forward. "Look, Kermit. We're on a mission from God. Or at least, a god-complex bureaucrat with a nosebleed. We need the go-juice. We have places to be. Sequels to film."

Toad smiled. It was wet.

"There is another way," Toad said.

He pointed.

At the edge of the camp, under the shadow of a massive rib, there was a cage.

It was made of rebar and laser grids.

Inside, a boy.

He looked about twelve. He was thin. Shivering. His eyes glowed with a faint, blue bio-luminescence.

"The Boss wants the kid," Toad said. "Cassandra. She collects... power sources. You take the kid to the Borderlands, I give you a full tank. Premium."

I looked at the kid.

He looked terrified. He looked like he knew exactly what "The Boss" did to power sources.

I looked at Logan.

His jaw tightened. A muscle jumped in his cheek. A vein throbbed in his temple.

*He's remembering,* I thought. *He's remembering the kids he didn't save. The ones at the school. The ones in the fire.*

"We're not slavers," Logan growled.

"Then walk," Toad said. He spat another shell. *Pffft.*

We walked away.

We went behind a stack of shipping containers.

"We take the fuel," Logan whispered.

"And the kid?" I asked.

Logan stopped. He looked at the massive white ribs curving above us. He looked at his hands.

"We can't save everyone, Wade."

"We can save *him*."

"If we start a fight, the whole camp comes down on us. We die. Your world dies. The math doesn't work."

"Math?" I hissed. "Since when do we care about math? We're X-Men! Well, you are. I'm an X-Man trainee. But the point stands! We save the cat! We save the kid!"

"I'm not an X-Man," Logan said.

"You're wearing the suit," I pointed out. I poked the yellow spandex on his chest. "Yellow and blue. The colors of 'I make bad decisions for the right reasons'. The colors of hope, you grumpy bastard."

He looked at the suit. He looked at the cage.

He sighed. A long, weary exhale that sounded like a tire deflating.

"I take the guards," he said. "You get the kid."

"Deal."

That night, we didn't just steal gas. We started a riot.

*The Raid.*

*Beat 1: Stealth.*
Logan moved like smoke. For a guy made of metal, he was quiet. He slipped behind the perimeter guard.
*Snap.*
One hand over the mouth. One twist. The guard dropped.
I crept to the cage. The lock was electronic. I jammed my knife into the panel. Sparks flew.
"Hey, kid," I whispered. "You like road trips? I hope you like Avril Lavigne."
The kid looked up. "Who are you?"
"I'm the fairy godmother," I said. "But with more cancer and guns. And a better ass."

*Beat 2: Chaos.*
The lock clicked.
*CLANG.*
I dropped the lock. It hit the metal floor. It echoed like a gunshot.
"Oops."
Toad screamed from the fuel depot. "Get them!"
And then... the camp exploded.
Mutants everywhere. Pyro stood on a hut, blasting fire. A guy with quills shot spikes from his face.
Logan dropped the stealth. *SNIKT.*
He became a whirlwind. He cut through the mob. He wasn't killing them—mostly—he was just disabling them. A slash to the leg. A punch to the gut with Adamantium-weighted knuckles.

*Beat 3: Escape.*
I grabbed the kid. "Run to the van! The gold one! It's the only gold thing in the universe!"
We sprinted.
Fireballs exploded around us. The heat was intense. It singed my suit. It smelled of burning hair.
We made it to the van. Logan jumped in the driver's seat. He was bleeding from a dozen cuts.
"Hold on!"
He gunned it. The Odyssey roared.
We smashed through the gate. Wood splintered. Metal screamed.
We hit the open sand.

I looked back. The camp was a hornet's nest.

"We did it!" I yelled. "We saved the—"

*THUNK.*

An arrow hit the back window. It shattered.

"Why is it always the windows?" I complained. "Do you know how hard it is to get safety glass in the Void? It's a supply chain nightmare!"

The kid was huddled in the back seat. He was glowing.

"Thank you," he whispered.

Logan looked in the rearview mirror. He met the kid's eyes.

For a second, the hardness in Logan's face melted. He wasn't the weapon. He wasn't the failure. He was the protector.

"Don't thank us yet," Logan said. "We're not safe."

"We're never safe," I said. "But we have gas. And snacks."

I held up a bag of pretzels I stole from Toad.

"Road trip!"
