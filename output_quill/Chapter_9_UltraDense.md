# CHAPTER 9: THE HOSTAGE DEAL (ULTRA-DENSITY)

**TIMELINE: NULL (THE VOID)**
**LOCATION: THE HONDA ODYSSEY**

The fight spilled out of the Helicarrier like a drunk tumbling down stairs.

We crashed into the sand. I rolled, coming up spitting grit. Logan was already on his feet, a yellow blur of rage and bad posture.

"You're not leaving!" I yelled.

"Watch me," he snarled.

He turned toward a vehicle parked in the shadow of the wreckage.

It was a Honda Odyssey. Gold. 2011. The pinnacle of suburban engineering. The Chariot of the Gods, if the Gods needed to pick up six kids from soccer practice at 4 PM.

"Shotgun!" I shouted, sprinting.

I threw myself through the open passenger window. I landed in the seat with a *thump* that rattled my already-bruised spine.

"Start the car!" I yelled. "We're taking this discussion on the road!"

Logan didn't start the car. He lunged through the driver's window.

"I'm going to gut you!" he roared.

He didn't sit. He threw himself across the center console.

The space inside the Odyssey was small. Intimate. It was a confessional booth made of plastic and beige upholstery. It smelled of:
*   **Old Goldfish Crackers:** The dusty, cheddar scent of a thousand snacks lost between the seats.
*   **Spilled Milk:** Sour. Fermented. The ghost of a lactose intolerance incident from 2012.
*   **Resignation:** The specific, heavy musk of a parent who has realized their life is over.

And now, it smelled of blood.

*Beat 1: Mechanics.*
Logan's left claw punched through the dashboard plastic.
*CRUNCH.*
It sounded like a bone breaking. Sparks flew from the radio, showering us in hot, orange light.
His right claw went for my neck.
I caught his wrist.
My grip strength (enhanced) against his Adamantium skeleton (indestructible). It was like trying to hold back a hydraulic press with a pool noodle.
I jammed my thumb into the wound on his shoulder—the bullet hole I had put there earlier.
"Ow!" he yelled.

*Beat 2: Consequence.*
He headbutted me.
*CRACK.*
My nose exploded. Cartilage turned to mush. Bone splintered into my sinuses.
Blood—warm and metallic—blinded me instantly. It filled my mouth. I tasted copper and failure.
I kicked him. My boot connected with his midsection. He slammed back against the driver's side door.
*SMASH.*
The window shattered. Safety glass rained down on us like diamonds.
"You broke the window!" I screamed, spitting blood. "There goes the resale value! Do you know what the Kelly Blue Book is on a vehicle with structural damage?"

*Beat 3: Aftertaste.*
We were panting. The air in the van was hot, humid with our breath and the smell of ozone from the sparking dashboard.
My nose knitted itself back together with a series of wet, sickening *clicks*. It felt like worms moving under my skin.
Logan pulled his claw out of the dashboard. It came free with a screech of tearing plastic that set my teeth on edge.

"Are you done?" I asked.

"No," he said.

"Good. Me neither."

I reached for the ignition. The key was there. Because in the Void, people leave their keys. What are you gonna do? Steal it? Drive to where?

I turned the key.

*Chug. Chug. Chug.*

The engine sputtered. It sounded like a smoker coughing up a lung.

Then... it caught.

*Vroom.*

The radio came to life.

*Avril Lavigne. 'I'm with You'.*

The acoustic guitar filled the small, violent space.

*It's a damn cold night...*

I looked at Logan.

He looked at me. He had a piece of dashboard plastic stuck in his hair. He looked like a feral cat that had gotten stuck in a dryer.

"It's a sign," I said.

"It's hell," he corrected.

I slammed the car into drive.

We peeled out. The tires spun in the loose sand, throwing up a rooster tail of dust.

"Where are we going?" Logan demanded. He had retracted his claws, but his hands were still fists.

"To find the bad guy," I said. "Every story needs a villain. And I heard she lives in a skull. Which is very metal. Very *Masters of the Universe*."

Logan sank back into the seat. He looked out the window at the endless grey wasteland.

"I hate you," he said.

"I know," I said. "But you're stuck with me. Just you, me, and Avril."

I cranked the volume.

"IT'S A DAMN COLD NIGHT!" I sang. My voice was off-key. Intentionally. "TRYING TO FIGURE OUT THIS LIFE!"

Logan closed his eyes. He slammed his head back against the headrest.

*Thump.*

"Kill me," he whispered. "Again."

"Can't," I said. "You're the Anchor. You're essential personnel. You're the load-bearing wall of the universe."

I looked at him.

He looked broken. Not just physically. The bullet hole was healing, but the slump in his shoulders? That was permanent.

*He's mourning,* I realized. *He's mourning a world I never saw. A family I never met.*

I reached into the glove box.

"Want a wet nap?" I offered. "It's lemon scented. It might help with the..." I gestured to his blood-soaked shirt. "...everything."

He took the wet nap. He stared at it.

"I'm going to kill you," he said again. But there was no heat in it. It was just a habit. A reflex.

"Get in line, peanut," I said. "Get in line."

We drove on. Two monsters in a minivan, heading into the dark heart of a dead world.
