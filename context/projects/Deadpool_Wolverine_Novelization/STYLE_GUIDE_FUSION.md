# STYLE_GUIDE_FUSION.md

## The "Chimichanga Blend"

### 1. The Narrator: Wade Wilson (Prince/Dinniman Hybrid)
*   **Jun Prince Influence:**
    *   Analytical, cynical, modern.
    *   Frames the universe as a set of rules/tropes to be exploited (Game/LitRPG adjacency).
    *   Use of capitalization for emphasized concepts (e.g., *The Mouse*, *The Timeline*).
*   **Matt Dinniman Influence:**
    *   High-energy chaos.
    *   Absurdist humor delivered with deadpan seriousness.
    *   "System Apocalypse" vibes: The TVA is an insane bureaucracy that Wade navigates like a Dungeon Crawler.
    *   Direct address to the audience: "Look, you think this is easy?"

### 2. The Violence: Blackmore Mode
*   **Triggers:** Combat, Injury, Regeneration.
*   **Technique:**
    *   Focus on the *sound* of wet impact.
    *   Focus on the *pressure* and *temperature* of pain.
    *   Anatomical specificity: "The radius snapped with the sound of a dry branch, the ulna following in a wet slide."
    *   No "cool" movie violence; make it *heavy* and *exhausting*.

### 3. The World: King Mode
*   **Triggers:** Setting the scene, Emotional beats, Backstory, Transitions.
*   **Technique:**
    *   Conversational pacing. "It was the kind of cold that didn't just bite; it gnawed."
    *   Deep dives into minor details to build realism (brand names, smells, textures).
    *   Folksy metaphors for horrific things.
    *   Building dread before the punchline.

## Example Synthesis
*   *Script:* "Deadpool kills the TVA agents with Wolverine's bones."
*   *Novelization:*
    *   (King): Describe the North Dakota wind, the silence of the grave, the smell of frozen earth.
    *   (Dinniman): Wade's internal monologue about the stupidity of the TVA armor design and the rights issues.
    *   (Blackmore): The sensation of the adamantium ribcage snapping, the wet warmth of the blood on his hands, the specific crunch of a helmet caving in.

## Glossary of Style
*   **Maximum Effort:** Not just a catchphrase, but a Dinniman-esque "Skill Activation".
*   **The Fourth Wall:** Treated as a thin veil between the "Story" (King) and the "Game" (Prince).
