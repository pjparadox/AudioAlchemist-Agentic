# LEGAL COMPARISON: The Exorcism of Emily Rose vs. Reality

## 1. The Core Legal Argument

| Feature | The Movie (*Emily Rose*) | The Reality (*Anneliese Michel*) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal System** | **Adversarial (USA).** Prosecution vs Defense in a battle of narratives. Jury decides facts. | **Inquisitorial (Germany).** The Court (Judges) actively investigates the truth. No Jury (in the American sense). |
| **The "Smoking Gun"** | **"Gambutrol".** A fictional drug. The defense argues the drug *locked* Emily in the possessed state, preventing the exorcism from working. This makes the doctor liable. | **"Duty of Care" (Garantenstellung).** The prosecution argued the parents/priests had a duty to force-feed Anneliese or call a doctor when she hit 30kg. No specific "drug interaction" was the hinge. |
| **The Defense** | **Scientific Doubt.** "The drug caused the failure." + **Possession as Fact.** "She was truly possessed." | **Religious Freedom (Art 4 GG).** "We are protected by the Constitution to believe in demons." + **Trust.** "We trusted the Bishop/Parents." |

## 2. The Verdict

| Feature | The Movie | The Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Ruling** | **Guilty** of Negligent Homicide. | **Guilty** of Negligent Homicide (*Fahrlässige Tötung*). |
| **The Sentence** | **Time Served.** The jury recommends no prison time. Father Moore walks free immediately. | **6 Months Suspended.** The defendants are sentenced to prison, but it is suspended for 3 years probation. They walk free. |
| **The Implication** | A "Moral Victory." The jury believed the possession was real enough to mitigate the sentence entirely. | A "Legal Compromise." The court affirmed the State's power over Religion (you must feed dying people) but showed mercy to the grieving parents. |

## 3. Fictionalization Analysis
*   **Is it the same?** **Functionally, yes.** Both result in a "Guilty but Free" outcome that validates the tragedy without destroying the defendants.
*   **Is it fictionalized?** **Legally, yes.** The movie invents the "Gambutrol" subplot to create a "Medical Whodunit" (the drug did it!). The real case was a much dryer, sadder debate about when a parent loses the right to treat their child with prayer. The movie "Americanized" the trial by making it about a specific *medical error* rather than a broad *Constitutional conflict*.
