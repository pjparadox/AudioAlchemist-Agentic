# CONVERSION STRATEGY: From Novelization to Standalone

## The Challenge
To convert a story initially conceived as a "Novelization of *The Exorcism of Emily Rose*" into a standalone work that avoids copyright infringement of the film's specific inventions (Characters: Erin Bruner, Father Moore; Plot Devices: Gambutrol, 3 AM scare).

## Path A: The "Capote" Approach (Creative Nonfiction)
**Goal:** A high-fidelity retelling of the *Anneliese Michel* case.
**Legal Safety:** **High.** Facts are public domain. You cannot copyright history.

### The Strategy
1.  **Return to the Source:** Strip away all "Emily Rose" elements (snowy city, female lawyer). Restore the "Klingenberg" elements (70s Bavaria, The Sawmill, The Bishop).
2.  **Narrative Structure (*In Cold Blood* Style):**
    *   **Omniscient Narrator:** Do not use a single POV character (like the movie's lawyer). Use a floating camera that moves from the Parents to the Priests to the Dying Girl.
    *   **Non-Chronological:** Intercut the *Trial (1978)* with the *Exorcism (1975)*. This creates tension without needing a fictional "mystery".
3.  **The Horror:** Derives from the *banality* of the negligence. The horror is not "jump scares" but the slow starvation documented in the autopsy.

## Path B: The "Blatty" Approach (Inspired Fiction)
**Goal:** A new horror novel "based on true events" but distinct from both the real case and the movie.
**Legal Safety:** **Medium-High.** Requires careful "filing off of serial numbers."

### The Strategy
1.  **Change the Venue:** Move the story from Germany/USA to **Ireland (1980s)** or **Rural France (1920s)**. This changes the legal stakes and the cultural flavor.
2.  **Change the "Lock":** In *Emily Rose*, the lock was "Gambutrol". In your novel, invent a new mechanism.
    *   *Idea:* The "Demon" is actually a *Tulpa* created by the community's collective guilt.
    *   *Idea:* The "Possession" is a viral meme spread through a specific prayer book.
3.  **The "Scare" Mechanics:**
    *   *Avoid:* The "3:00 AM" waking (Emily Rose specific).
    *   *Replace with:* A specific *sound* (e.g., The buzzing of flies that isn't there) or a *visual distortion* (e.g., Everyone looks like they are wearing wax masks).

## Best Practices (Mercury's Research)
*   **Fact vs. Fiction:** In Creative Nonfiction, you can invent *dialogue* if it captures the "spiritual truth" of the scene (and you admit it in the Author's Note), but you cannot invent *events*.
*   **The "Small Truth":** Bestsellers in this genre (e.g., *The Devil in the White City*) succeed because they focus on the *sensory details* (the smell of the coal dust, the texture of the rosary beads) rather than just the plot.
*   **The "Why":** The movie asks "Is God real?" Your book should ask "Why did they let her die?" The shift in *theme* distinguishes it from the film.
