# TRIAL ANATOMY: The "Klingenberg Case" (1978)

## 1. The Charge: §222 StGB (Fahrlässige Tötung)
*   **Statute:** "Whosoever through negligence causes the death of a person shall be liable to imprisonment not exceeding five years or a fine."
*   **The Core Legal Question:** Was the death *preventable*? If so, at what point did the defendants have a "Duty of Care" (*Garantenstellung*) to intervene medically, regardless of their religious beliefs?

## 2. Prosecution Theory (Karl Stenger)
*   **The Narrative:** This was not a spiritual battle; it was a medical tragedy fueled by superstition.
*   **Key Argument:** "Preventability."
    *   Expert Witness (Medical): Dr. Schleip testified that forced feeding (via nasogastric tube) would have saved her life even one week before death.
    *   Tranquilizers (Valium/Distraneurin) could have calmed the "demonic" rage.
*   **The Trap:** The priests claimed the "demons" forbade eating. The prosecution argued that obeying a "demon's" command over a doctor's advice is inherently negligent.

## 3. Defense Theory (Erich Schmidt-Leichner)
*   **The Narrative:** The defendants acted in good faith, exercising their Constitutional right to religious practice (Article 4 of the German Basic Law).
*   **Key Argument:** "Trust."
    *   The parents trusted the priests (experts in spirituality).
    *   The priests trusted the Bishop (authority).
    *   Anneliese *refused* medical care. To force her would have been an assault on her will.
*   **The "Exorcism Tapes" Strategy:** Schmidt-Leichner played the tapes in court. His goal was to show the *reality* of the possession (or at least the subjective reality for the defendants).
    *   *Result:* The courtroom was chilled. The "Demon Voice" was undeniable evidence of *something* abnormal, even if not supernatural.

## 4. The Verdict & Sentencing
*   **Guilty.**
*   **Sentence:** 6 months imprisonment.
*   **Suspension:** Suspended for 3 years probation.
*   **Reasoning:**
    *   The court ruled that while the defendants believed in the possession, their belief did not exempt them from the law.
    *   "Spiritual help does not replace medical help."
    *   The light sentence acknowledged the defendants had no malicious intent and, in the case of the parents, had "suffered enough" (*Poena naturalis*).
