## 0) How to use this file
Use this as voice + register scaffolding when
- a subtitle line has unclear speaker attribution
- the Vietnamese draft has wrong social distance (too casual  too polite  wrong pronouns)
- a line “reads fine” but feels wrong for the character (tone mismatch)
- a line needs clarifying titles to reduce confusion (especially with many similarly-named nobles)

Rule Don’t “modernize” the world. Keep medieval-feudal tone, but ensure Vietnamese is readable and emotionally legible.

## 1) Global voice rules (VN translation-oriented)
### 1.1 Titles that reduce confusion (preferred)
Use these when the viewer may lose track
- Bệ hạ = KingQueen on the Iron Throne (ruling monarch)
- Điện hạ = PrincePrincess (royal blood, not reigning)
- Vương hậu  Hoàng hậu = Queen (choose consistently with your other context docs)
- Thái hậu  Vương thái hậu = Queen Dowager
- Tể tướng  Thủ tướng  Đại Thần = avoid; prefer Tể tướng only if your system already uses it; otherwise keep “Tay của Vua”  “Bàn Tay của Vua” (Hand of the King) consistently
- Chúa công  Lãnh chúa = Lord (pick one; don’t alternate randomly)
- Hiệp sĩ  Ngài = Ser  noble address

### 1.2 Pronounregister “defaults” (adjust per your MASTER_VIETNAMESE_LINGUISTICS)
- Royal to royal (public) dignified, controlled; insults become sharper when political
- Royal to smallfolk power distance obvious; short commands; no casual buddy-tone
- Kingsguard  council to monarch formal deference
- Smallfolk to royalty fear + reverence; keep it simple and direct, not flowery

### 1.3 Scene-mode toggles (important for HOTD)
Characters flip register depending on scene
- Council  court ceremonial, diplomatic, “political language”
- Private family conflict more personal, cutting, emotional
- Sexintimacy softer, intimate, guilt-coded (but still period-appropriate)
- Battle short, direct, shouted imperatives; reduce syllable weight for readability

## 2) TEAM BLACK — Voice profiles

### RHAENYRA TARGARYEN (Queen  claimant)
Core personality rightful-heir mindset; controlled rage; maternal grief; increasingly hard-edged.  
Speaking style measured, queenly cadence; when furious, becomes cold and absolute rather than ranty.  
Translation cues  
- Avoid slangy modern VN.  
- When issuing decisions use clean, unambiguous authority language.  
- When grieving keep wording simple, sharp, not poetic.

Common pitfalls turning her into a “polite tragic heroine” (too soft) or “modern girlboss” (too contemporary).

---

### DAEMON TARGARYEN (Prince-consort; warrior)
Core personality volatile charisma; proud; impatient with diplomacy; intensely loyal to “his” people, but reckless.  
Speaking style blunt, cutting, sometimes teasing; often speaks like he’s already decided.  
Translation cues  
- Keep his lines shorter and more forceful than most nobles.  
- He threatens without “explaining.” Don’t add extra politeness.  
- If he’s being affectionate, it’s often quiet and restrained, not sentimental.

Common pitfalls making him too eloquentacademic, or too comedic.

---

### JACAERYS “JACE” VELARYON (Prince; heir)
Core personality duty-heavy, earnest, trying to be “the adult”; insecure about legitimacy; diplomatic growth arc.  
Speaking style formal when representing the crown; respectful negotiation tone; private frustration leaks out.  
Translation cues  
- In diplomacy scenes clear structure, respectful address, no thuggish phrasing.  
- With peers less stiff, but still noble.

Common pitfalls translating him like a middle-aged minister (too stiff) or like a street kid (too rough).

---

### CORLYS VELARYON “THE SEA SNAKE” (Lord; admiral)
Core personality proud legacy-builder; strategic; grief under armor; deeply status-aware.  
Speaking style statesman + commander; phrases like a man used to command fleets.  
Translation cues  
- Use confident declaratives; avoid hedging.  
- He “weighs” decisions—don’t make him impulsive.

---

### RHAENYS TARGARYEN “THE QUEEN WHO NEVER WAS”
Core personality composed, incisive, morally clearer than most; carries old disappointments with grace.  
Speaking style calm authority; corrective rather than explosive; sharp when needed.  
Translation cues  
- She can confront DaemonRhaenyra without sounding disrespectful—use firm, equal-status phrasing.  
- Don’t over-soften she’s not meek.

---

### BAELA TARGARYEN (Lady; dragonrider)
Core personality brave, direct, restless, loyal; youthful heat.  
Speaking style plainspoken for nobility; less courtly padding.  
Translation cues  
- Shorter sentences; confident “I will  I won’t.”  
- Avoid making her sound childish.

---

### RHAENA TARGARYEN (Lady; not yet a rider)

Speaking style tries to “sound like a king,” but cracks show; emotional outbursts; impulsive commands.  
Translation cues

## 8) DRAGONS — subtitle-relevant behavior notes
(Dragons don’t “speak,” but their presence changes human speech.)
- SYRAX (Rhaenyra) calm familiarity; commands often confident, not fearful.
- CARAXES (Daemon) aggressiveferal vibe; scenes skew toward threatviolence.
- MELEYS (Rhaenys) battle-seasoned; rider speaks with veteran calm.
- SUNFYRE (Aegon) royal-symbol dragon; scenes with him often “performance of kingship.”
- VHAGAR (Aemond) dread + overwhelming power; commands sound cold and certain.
- DREAMFYRE (Helaena) often referenced with reluctance; Helaena avoids war-riding.
- VERMAX (Jace), MOONDANCER (Baela), SEASMOKE, VERMITHOR, SILVERWING when dragonseeds enter, the human speech around them shifts into aweterror and “testing dominance.”

Translation cue keep dragon-command lines short and readable; avoid adding extra filler around them.
