# Chapter 7: The Diagnosis

Dr. Marek sat on the witness stand, a monument to medical certainty. He was a man who believed the brain was a machine, and broken machines could be fixed with the right chemicals.

"I administered an electroencephalograph," Marek said, his voice flat. "An EEG."

"And what did it reveal?" Owen Thomas asked, pacing in front of the jury box.

"No epileptic patterns," Marek admitted. "No evidence of seizures."

Owen stopped pacing. He turned to the jury, his expression one of mock confusion. "Really? Did that surprise you?"

"Not at all," Marek said smoothly. "It is common to diagnose epilepsy without EEG confirmation. However, I ordered further tests."

"And what did those indicate?"

"A possible epileptic focus in the left temporal lobe."

"And the treatment?"

"Tegretol. An anti-convulsant."

"Did she take it?"

"For a while," Marek said, his lips thinning. "But she stopped coming to her appointments."

"Did she give a reason?"

"Yes. She told me her priest had convinced her it was a spiritual condition. Not medical."

"Was it your impression that she had placed her care entirely in Father Moore's hands?"

"Objection," Erin said, standing up. "Leading."

"Sustained," the Judge said.

Owen rephrased. "What was the result of Father Moore's suggestion?"

Marek looked directly at the priest. His eyes were cold. "I believe Father Moore's suggestion killed her. Stopping the treatment led directly to her death."

Erin watched the jury. They were absorbing it. *The priest killed the girl.* It was a simple story. A clean story.

She stood up for cross-examination. She needed to dirty the water.

"Doctor," she said, approaching the stand. "You said you observed a 'possible' epileptic focus. Doesn't that imply it might *not* have been epilepsy?"

"It's possible," Marek conceded.

"And if she wasn't epileptic, then stopping the drug was the right thing to do, wasn't it?"

"But she was epileptic."

"Really?" Erin smiled, but it didn't reach her eyes. "Tell me, Doctor, when Emily saw objects move on their own... is that a symptom of epilepsy?"

"No."

"Is it a symptom of psychosis?"

"It... could be."

"So, according to you, she was epileptic *and* psychotic? Two separate, severe mental illnesses striking a healthy girl at the exact same moment?"

Marek stiffened. "It's rare. But possible."

"More possible than something else?"

"Objection," Owen said. "Argumentative."

"Withdrawn," Erin said. She sat down. She had planted the seed. *Doubt.*

# Chapter 8: The Witching Hour

The County Jail was a place of endless noise. Shouting, clanging, the rhythmic thudding of bodies against steel. But in the hallway leading to Father Moore's cell, Erin heard something else.

A whisper.

*"One... two... three... four... five... six..."*

It was faint, threading through the cacophony like a silver wire. Erin stopped. She looked into the cell next to her.

It was empty.

She frowned. She walked on.

Inside Moore's cell, the air was heavy.

"We have to work fast," Erin said, sitting on the metal chair. "They're killing us with the medical testimony."

Moore looked at her. He seemed tired, worn down by a battle she couldn't see.

"Before we start," he said, "there is something I must tell you. Something I should have said before."

"Okay."

"There are forces surrounding this trial," Moore said. His voice was low, intense. "Dark forces. Be careful, Erin. The grass you tread on is home to many serpents."

Erin sighed. "I'm not afraid of demons, Father. I'm afraid of the jury."

"Demons exist whether you believe in them or not," Moore said. "And your involvement may open you up to their attacks."

"I'm fine," Erin said, dismissing him. "Now, tell me about the dorm room. What happened next?"

"She went to the hospital," Moore said. "That's when the visions changed."

*Flashback.*

The hospital room was sterile, white, and smelled of antiseptic. Emily sat on the bed, swinging her legs. She looked small. Vulnerable.

She was singing. A hymn.

*"Tender shepherd, hear me, bless thy little lamb tonight..."*

She looked out the window. The sun was setting, bleeding purple and red across the sky.

On the horizon, storm clouds gathered. They were dark, roiling, moving against the wind.

As Emily watched, the clouds began to knit together. They formed a shape. A nose. A mouth. Eyes.

A face.

A giant, ghastly face in the sky, leering down at the city. It opened its mouth, a cavern of darkness that swallowed the light.

Emily stopped singing. She stood up, backing away from the window.

The door opened. Jason walked in, holding a bouquet of flowers.

"Emily?"

She turned.

Her face wasn't her face.

It was a skull. The skin was pulled tight, translucent, revealing the bone beneath. Her eyes were black pits. Her teeth were jagged shards.

Jason dropped the flowers.

"Emily!"

She screamed. It wasn't a scream of fear. It was a scream of recognition.

*Present Day.*

Erin walked across the campus with Jason. The leaves were falling, crunching under their feet.

"She believed it started at the hospital," Jason said quietly. "She said the faces... they changed. Everywhere."

"Changed how?" Erin asked.

"She saw demons," Jason whispered. "In the rain. In the windows. In the faces of strangers. She said everyone was wearing a mask, and sometimes the masks slipped."

# Chapter 9: The Face in the Rain

Emily walked across the quad. The sky was low and heavy, a bruised ceiling pressing down on the world. The wind bit through her coat.

Students hurried past her, heads down, fighting the weather. To Emily, they looked like threats. Obstacles.

Thunder rumbled. She ducked into a classroom building.

Inside, it was quiet. Students were taking an exam. Rain spatters against the tall, narrow windows.

Emily sat in the back. She looked at the window.

The raindrops weren't sliding down the glass. They were forming a pattern. An image.

A face.

The same ghastly face from the clouds. It stared at her through the glass, weeping rain.

Emily gasped, covering her mouth.

A student in front of her turned around. "Are you okay?"

Emily looked at him.

His face melted. His eyes ran like ink. His mouth stretched, opening wider and wider until his head split. A demon leered at her from beneath the skin.

Emily scrambled back, knocking over her chair. *Clatter.*

The professor looked up. The other students turned.

The demon was gone. It was just a boy, looking concerned.

"Emily?" the professor asked.

She grabbed her bag and ran.

Outside, the rain was a deluge. She splashed through puddles, the water soaking her shoes.

She stopped at a crosswalk. A car passed. In the fogged window, a face pressed against the glass. A screaming, skeletal face.

She ran.

She crossed the quad. A couple walked toward her, sharing an umbrella. As they passed, the umbrella tilted up.

Two faces. Both monstrous. Both grinning.

Emily screamed. She dropped her bag. She ran toward the chapel, the only sanctuary she knew.

Inside, it was dim. Candles flickered on the altar.

Emily walked down the aisle, her breath coming in jagged gasps.

Two old women knelt in the front pew, praying.

Emily walked past them.

They looked up.

Their lips were peeled back, revealing bloody gums and sharpened teeth. They weren't praying. They were gnashing their teeth.

They screamed at her.

Jason burst into the chapel. "Emily?"

She was standing at the altar. She was twisted, her body arching backward away from the crucifix as if physically repelled by a magnetic force.

She was growling.

"Don't touch me!" she roared. The voice was deep, masculine.

She turned to him. Her face flickered—human, demon, human.

She collapsed.

"Jason," she wept, her voice small again. "Please... don't leave me."

# Chapter 10: The Locket

Erin sat at the bar in Sidebar. She was drinking too fast.

On the TV above the bar, the news played. James Van Hopper—the murderer she had set free—had killed again. A young couple.

Erin felt sick. She went to the bathroom. She splashed water on her face.

She looked in the mirror.

For a second, she saw something behind her. A tall, dark-haired woman in black.

She spun around.

Empty.

She went back to the bar. There was a fresh martini waiting for her.

"I didn't order this," she said.

The bartender pointed to a handsome guy down the bar. He smiled.

"You look like you could use a friend," he said.

Erin drank the martini. She took him home.

It was a mistake. She knew it as soon as they stumbled through her door.

"Wait," she said, pushing him away. "Stop."

He didn't stop. He was aggressive, entitled.

"I said stop!" Erin shoved him. "Get out."

He stared at her, angry. Then he left, slamming the door.

Erin locked it. She looked through the peephole. He kicked the door—*BAM*—then walked away.

She was alone.

She went to bed.

*Tick. Tick. Tick.*

Her alarm clock read **2:59 AM**.

She slept.

**3:00 AM**.

Erin woke up.

The smell. Burning.

She got up. She checked the apartment. The lights went out. *Zap.*

A creak.

The front door—the door she had deadbolted—swung open.

Light from the hallway spilled in.

Erin ran. She slammed the door. She locked it.

She retreated to her bedroom.

The next morning, on the sidewalk outside the courthouse, she saw something glinting on the concrete.

She bent down.

It was a silver locket. Engraved with initials. **E.C.B.**

*Erin Christine Bruner.*

Her initials.

It wasn't hers. But it was there. A gift? A warning?

She picked it up. It was cold.

# Chapter 11: The Anthropologist

The trial resumed. Erin needed to shift the narrative. She needed to validate the supernatural.

"The Defense calls Dr. Sadira Adani."

Dr. Adani was an anthropologist, a woman who treated possession as a cultural fact, not a fairy tale.

"Possession is a basic human experience," she testified. "It happens in every culture. It is not psychosis. Psychotics are confused. Emily was lucid."

"Why did the exorcism fail?" Erin asked.

"The drugs," Adani said. "Gambutrol. It intoxicates the brain. An exorcism requires the subject to be mentally present. To reject the entity. The drugs locked her in the possessed state. They prevented the cure."

Owen Thomas destroyed her on cross-examination.

"You study voodoo?" he asked, sneering. "Snake handling? Peyote rituals?"

"I study human experience," Adani said, bristling.

"And this theory about the drugs? Did you test it?"

"No, that would be unethical."

"So it's just a theory. A guess. Thank you, Doctor."

Erin watched the jury. They were laughing.

She was losing.

She needed a witness. An eyewitness.

Ray ran in. "I found one," he whispered. "Dr. Cartwright. He was there. At the exorcism."

# Chapter 12: The Tape

Erin met Dr. Cartwright in a park at dusk.

He was a wreck. He was feeding pigeons, his hands shaking so hard the breadcrumbs scattered like confetti. He kept looking over his shoulder at the tree line.

"I'm a man of science," Cartwright whispered. "I was. Until that night."

"Will you testify?" Erin asked.

"They'll kill me," Cartwright said.

"Who?"

"Them. The ones in the dark."

He handed her a tape recorder. "Moore recorded it. Take it. It's your burden now."

That night, Erin listened to the tape.

*Hiss. Crackle.*

Then, the voice.

*"One... two... three... four... five... six..."*

It was a nightmare captured on magnetic tape. A voice that sounded like tearing metal.

Erin stared at the recorder.

*Tick.*

Her clock stopped. **3:00 AM**.

The tape recorder clicked off.

But the voice didn't stop.

*"Erin..."*

It whispered from the hallway.

Erin didn't move. She knew, with a terrifying certainty, that if she opened the door, she would see something that would shatter her mind.

She sat in the dark, clutching the locket, and waited for the dawn.
