## Chapter 16: The Haunting of Erin Bruner

Erin Bruner lived in a fortress.

Her apartment was on the thirty-fourth floor of a steel-and-glass tower that pierced the city skyline like a needle. It was a space designed to repel the messiness of life. White walls. Minimalist furniture. Art that cost more than a house in the suburbs.

She stood at the window, looking down at the grid of lights below. The city was a circuit board, humming with electricity and ambition.

She was alone. She was always alone.

She poured a glass of Pinot Noir. She sat on the white leather sofa. She turned on the TV. The news was playing, a muted flicker of other people's tragedies.

She fell asleep.

*Cut to Black.*

*Fade In.*

Erin's bedroom. **2:59 AM**.

The digital clock on the nightstand was the only light in the room. The red numbers burned in the darkness.

*Tick. Tick. Tick.*

Erin slept. Her breathing was shallow, rhythmic.

The numbers flipped. **3:00 AM**.

Erin's eyes snapped open.

She sat up. She wasn't groggy. She was instantly, terrifyingly awake.

The room was silent. But it wasn't empty.

She smelled it.

Burning.

It was faint, a wisp of scent curling through the air. Like a match struck in another room. But it grew stronger. Acrid. Oily. The smell of burning insulation. The smell of singed hair.

*Fire.*

Panic, cold and sharp, spiked in her chest. She threw off the covers. She got out of bed.

"Hello?" she called out. Her voice sounded small in the high-ceilinged room.

She walked into the hallway. The shadows seemed to pool in the corners, darker than they should be. The air was heavy, charged with static. The hair on her arms stood up.

She checked the living room. Empty.

She checked the kitchen. The stove was cold. The electronics were off. The smoke alarm—a high-tech, sensitive device—was silent.

She poured a glass of water at the sink. Her hands were shaking.

*Zap.*

The lights died.

The apartment plunged into absolute darkness.

The glass slipped from her fingers. *Smash.*

Erin froze. She stood in the dark, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird.

*Creak.*

A sound from the living room.

The sound of a door opening.

She turned.

A sliver of light from the hallway cut across the living room floor. A blade of illumination.

Her front door—the heavy, solid wood door she had deadbolted before bed—was standing wide open.

Erin stared at it. Her mind tried to process the impossible. *I locked it. I know I locked it.*

She didn't move. She couldn't.

She waited for someone to walk through the door. An intruder. A thief.

But no one came. The hallway was empty.

It was just the door, standing open, inviting the darkness in.

She forced her legs to move. She ran to the door. She slammed it shut. She threw the deadbolt. She engaged the chain.

She backed away, her breath coming in gasps.

She retreated to her bedroom. She locked that door too. She curled up on the bed, pulling the duvet over her head like a child hiding from monsters.

On the nightstand, the clock blinked. **3:00 AM**.

It wasn't a glitch. It was a message.

## Chapter 17: Visions

The world was breaking.

For Emily Rose, reality had become a thin sheet of ice over a deep, dark ocean. And the ice was cracking.

She sat in the library. It should have been safe. Rows of books. Silence. Students studying.

She looked at the boy sitting across from her. He was reading a history textbook.

He looked up. He smiled.

His smile stretched. It kept stretching. His skin tore. His jaw unhinged.

His eyes rolled back, revealing whites that were veined with black fire.

A demon leered at her.

Emily looked down at her book. *Don't look. Don't look.*

She looked up.

The boy was just a boy. He was frowning at her. "Are you okay?"

"I have to go," Emily whispered.

She ran.

She ran out into the rain. The campus was a blur of grey stone and wet pavement.

She stopped at a crosswalk. A car passed.

In the fogged window, a face pressed against the glass. A screaming, skeletal face. Its mouth open in a silent howl.

She ran.

She crossed the quad. A couple walked toward her, sharing an umbrella. They were laughing.

As they passed, the umbrella tilted up.

Two faces. Both monstrous. Both grinning. Their skin was melting, dripping off their skulls like wax.

"We see you, Emily," they seemed to say. "We see you."

Emily screamed. She dropped her bag. She ran toward the chapel. The spire rose into the grey sky like a finger pointing to God.

*Sanctuary.*

## Chapter 18: The Chapel

The chapel was dim. The air smelled of incense and old wood. Candles flickered on the altar, casting long, dancing shadows.

Emily walked down the aisle. Her breath came in jagged gasps. She was soaked to the bone.

Two old women knelt in the front pew. They were dressed in black. They were praying.

Emily walked past them. She needed to get to the altar. She needed to touch the cross.

The women looked up.

Their faces were ruins. Their lips were peeled back, revealing bloody gums and sharpened teeth. Their eyes were black pits.

They weren't praying. They were gnashing their teeth.

They screamed at her. A sound of pure, concentrated hate.

Emily stumbled back. She fell.

She scrambled to her feet and ran to the altar. She grabbed the railing. She looked up at the crucifix.

Jesus looked down.

But he wasn't looking at her with love. He was looking at her with pity. Or maybe fear.

Suddenly, her body seized.

Her back arched. It bent backward, defying anatomy. Her spine popped. Her head touched her heels.

She was a bow drawn to the breaking point.

She tried to scream, but her throat was locked. She could only growl. A low, guttural sound that vibrated in her chest.

Jason burst into the chapel. "Emily!"

He saw her. He saw the impossible angle of her body. He saw the way she was twisted away from the altar, as if a magnetic force was repelling her.

"Oh my God," he whispered.

He ran to her. He reached out to touch her.

"DON'T TOUCH ME!"

The voice didn't come from her throat. It came from her chest. It was deep, masculine, ancient.

She turned her head. Her neck cracked.

Her face flickered. Human. Demon. Human. Demon.

Jason recoiled. He fell back.

Emily collapsed. Her body went limp, falling to the stone floor like a marionette with cut strings.

She looked up at him. Her eyes were her own again. Filled with tears. Filled with terror.

"Jason," she wept. "Please... don't leave me."

## Chapter 19: The Return Home

The drive back to the farm was a funeral procession of one car.

Jason drove. Emily sat in the passenger seat, staring out the window. She looked hollowed out.

They left the city behind. The buildings gave way to suburbs, then to strip malls, then to fields.

The corn was dead. The stalks stood in the frozen mud, brown and withered.

Emily didn't speak. She just watched the world go by. A world she was leaving.

They pulled into the driveway. The farmhouse looked smaller than she remembered. Greyer.

Maria and Samuel came out to meet them.

Maria looked at her daughter. She saw the bruises under her eyes. She saw the way she held herself, as if she were made of glass.

"Oh, Emily," Maria whispered.

She hugged her. Emily stood stiffly in her mother's arms.

"I'm home, Mama," she said. Her voice was flat. "I'm home."

They took her upstairs. To her old room.

It looked the same. The quilt on the bed. The crucifix on the wall. The dolls on the shelf.

But it felt different. The air was colder here.

Emily sat on the bed. She looked at the dolls. Their glass eyes seemed to watch her.

"Rest," Maria said. "Just rest."

They left her alone.

But she wasn't alone. She knew that. She was never alone anymore.

## Chapter 20: The First Night

Night fell. The farmhouse was an island in a sea of darkness.

Alice, the youngest sister, couldn't sleep. She heard sounds coming from Emily's room.

Murmuring. Thumping.

She got up. She walked down the hall.

"Emily?" she whispered.

She opened the door.

The room was dark.

Emily was in the corner. She was crouching on the floor.

"Emily?"

Emily turned.

Her mouth was full. She was chewing.

Blood smeared her chin.

Alice stepped closer. "What are you eating?"

Emily opened her hand.

Insects. Spiders. Beetles. A dead fly.

She shoved them into her mouth. *Crunch.*

Alice screamed.

Samuel and Maria ran into the room.

Emily looked at them. She screamed back.

It wasn't a scream of fear. It was a scream of challenge.

She threw herself backward. She crab-walked across the floor, her limbs moving with impossible speed.

"Call him!" Samuel shouted. "Call the priest!"

Father Moore arrived an hour later. He carried a Bible and a stole.

He walked into the room.

Emily was on the bed. She was rigid. Catatonic.

Moore walked to the bed. He looked down at her.

"Emily?"

She opened her eyes.

"I am the one who dwells within," she whispered.

Moore felt a chill go through him. He recognized the presence. He had felt it in the yard.

"I am Father Moore," he said. "I am here to help you."

"Help?" the voice mocked. "There is no help here, Priest. Only hunger."

She smiled. Her teeth were stained with insect blood.

"We are hungry," she said. "And we are many."
