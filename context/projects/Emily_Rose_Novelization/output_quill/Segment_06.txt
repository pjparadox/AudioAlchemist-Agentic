Chapter 9: The Six Names

The courtroom was packed. Word had gotten out. The "Exorcist Priest" was testifying again.

Father Moore sat in the box. He looked tired, but his eyes were clear.

"The night of October 31st," Erin said. "Tell us about the barn."

"The house wasn't big enough," Moore said. "The energy... it was too much. The windows were breaking. We moved her to the barn."

*Flashback. Halloween Night.*

The barn door slammed open. Wind and rain swirled in, mixing with the smell of old hay and manure.

Emily was on the ground. She was covered in mud and straw. She was growling.

Samuel held her legs. Jason held her arms. They were struggling. She was impossibly strong, her limbs thrashing with the power of a piston.

"Doctor!" Moore shouted. "Monitor her!"

Cartwright, shivering in the corner, stepped forward with his stethoscope. He placed it on her chest.

"Her heart," he stammered. "It's too fast. 180. 200."

"We must finish it," Moore said.

He knelt beside her. He held the crucifix over her face.

"I command you!" he roared, his voice cracking. "By the power of Christ! Tell me your names!"

Emily stopped moving. She went rigid. Her back arched, lifting her torso off the ground, balanced only on her heels and head.

She opened her mouth.

"One," she whispered. The voice was dry, dusty, ancient. "I am the one who dwelt within Cain!"

Thunder boomed outside, shaking the rafters.

"Two!" A hiss, sharp and sibilant. "I am the one who dwelt within Nero!"

"Three!" A guttural snarl. "I once dwelt within Judas!"

"Four!" A high, maniacal giggle. "I was with Legion!"

"Five!" A voice of cold, heavy iron. "I am Belial!"

Moore was sweating, his face pale. The air in the barn was heavy, pressurized. The horses were screaming in their stalls, kicking the wood to splinters.

"And the sixth?" Moore screamed. "Who are you?"

Emily dropped to the ground. She looked up at him. Her eyes were black voids.

"And I..."

The shadows in the barn seemed to lengthen, to stand up. A darkness coalesced behind her, forming a shape. A cloak. A hat.

The Black Figure.

"I am Lucifer," the voice boomed. It wasn't just coming from her mouth. It was coming from the walls, the floor, the air itself. "The Devil in the Flesh!"

The horses broke free. They stampeded, hooves thundering on the dirt floor, rushing past the men, out into the storm.

Moore fell back, overwhelmed.

Emily collapsed.

***

Present Day.

The courtroom was silent.

"Six names," Moore said softly. "Cain. Nero. Judas. Legion. Belial. Lucifer."

Owen Thomas stood up. He walked to the jury box.

"Tibetan monks," he said casually.

Moore blinked. "Excuse me?"

"Tibetan monks," Owen repeated. "They can create multiphonics. Two tones at once. Did you know that?"

"No."

"So the 'dual voices' on the tape... the 'demonic chorus'... could just be a vocal trick? A symptom of her psychosis?"

"It wasn't a trick," Moore said.

"And the names?" Owen asked. "Cain? Nero? Judas? All figures from history. Figures a girl interested in religion would know."

"She didn't know Aramaic," Moore said. "Or Latin."

"She studied catechism, didn't she? She was a good Catholic girl."

Owen leaned on the railing. "And this 'Black Figure' you saw? Did anyone else see it?"

"No," Moore admitted.

"Just you. The man who stopped her medication."

Owen smiled. It was a predator's smile.

"No further questions."

***

Erin sat at the defense table. She felt the weight of the room pressing down on her.

She looked at the yellow envelope.

"Redirect," she said, standing up.

She picked up the envelope.

"Father Moore," she said. "You said Emily accepted her fate. Why?"

"She had a vision," Moore said.

Erin took out the letter. "Defense Exhibit C."

She read it. She read the words of a dying girl who chose pain over peace. Who chose to be a witness.

"People say God is dead," Erin read, her voice trembling slightly. "But how can they think that if I show them the Devil?"

She put the letter down.

"She died," Erin said to the jury, "so you would know. So you would wonder."

She looked at Owen. He looked annoyed, but not triumphant. Not anymore.

"The Defense rests."

***

The Closing Arguments.

Owen Thomas went first. He was good. He was logical.

"Facts," he said, taping the autopsy photo to the podium. "We deal in facts. The fact is, a girl is dead. The fact is, she was sick. The fact is, she was denied treatment."

He pointed at Moore. "That man killed her. Not with a knife. With negligence. With superstition."

He sat down.

Erin stood up. She walked to the podium. She looked at the photo of Emily's ruined face.

She took it down. She turned it over.

"I am a woman of doubt," she said. "Owen Thomas is a man of faith. But in this room, we deal with possibilities."

She walked the line of the jury box, looking each juror in the eye.

"Is it a fact that she was epileptic? Maybe. Is it a fact she was possessed? I don't know."

She paused.

"But Emily believed it. Father Moore believed it. And they acted on that love. Not negligence. Love."

She touched the locket.

"Don't send a good man to prison for loving a girl enough to fight the Devil for her soul."

***

Chapter 10: The Verdict

The jury was out for four days.

Four days of waiting. Four days of silence.

Erin sat in the empty courtroom with Moore.

"Whatever happens," Moore said, "you told the story."

"It's not enough," Erin said. "I want you free."

"I am free," Moore said. "My conscience is clear."

The bailiff opened the door. "They're back."

The courtroom filled up. The air was electric.

The Foreman stood up. He was a farmer. He had calloused hands. He wouldn't look at Owen Thomas.

"In the case of The People vs. Richard Moore..."

He took a breath.

"...we find the defendant Guilty of Negligent Homicide."

Erin closed her eyes. It felt like a physical blow.

"However," the Foreman said.

Erin opened her eyes.

"We recommend a sentence of... time served."

The room erupted.

Judge Brewster banged the gavel. "Order! Order!"

She looked at the jury. She looked at Moore.

"The Court accepts the recommendation. Father Moore, you are sentenced to time served. You are free to go."

It was a paradox. A legal miracle. Guilty, but innocent. Condemned, but free.

Owen Thomas slammed his notebook shut. He walked out without a word.

Erin hugged Moore. She was crying.

"Thank you," he whispered.

"Thank you," she said.

Epilogue: The Epitaph

Spring.

The cemetery was green. Life pushing up from the dead earth.

Erin and Moore stood by the grave.

"Do you believe her?" Erin asked. "About the vision?"

Moore looked at the headstone. "I believe that faith and doubt can coexist," he said. "Faith without doubt can be a dangerous thing. But doubt... doubt opens the door."

"I quit the firm," Erin said. "I was offered a job teaching."

"Will you take it?"

"Maybe. I think I'm done with sharks."

She looked at the stone.

*1986 - 2005*

And below, the words Emily had chosen:

*"Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling."*

Erin touched the locket. She felt the cold metal warm against her skin.

She turned and walked away, down the path, back toward the city. The wind blew through the trees, a soft, whispering sound.

She didn't look back. She knew the shadows were there. She knew the darkness was real.

But she also knew, for the first time in a long time, that she was walking in the light.
