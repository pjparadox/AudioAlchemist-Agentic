Erin sat at the bar in Sidebar, nursing a second martini. The buzz of conversation around her was white noise, a barrier against the world. She was reading the newspaper. The headline screamed: **PRIEST ARRESTED IN EXORCISM DEATH.**

Beside it were two photos. One of Father Moore, looking haggard in handcuffs. The other of Emily Rose, her high school yearbook photo. Smiling. Innocent.

"I just spoke with the Archdiocese," Karl said, appearing behind her like a bad conscience.

Erin didn't turn. "And?"

"They said the priest is expecting to testify. What did you tell him?"

"I told him what he needed to hear," Erin said. "Don't worry, Karl. When the time is right, I'll talk him out of it."

She looked past Karl. A man had entered the bar.

He was in his thirties, with the kind of clean-cut, aggressive good looks that belonged on a campaign poster. He scanned the room with predatory efficiency.

Owen Thomas. The Prosecutor. God's Lawyer.

"I hope you know what you're doing," Karl muttered, following her gaze. He walked away, leaving Erin exposed.

Owen approached the bar. He didn't order a drink. He ordered a soda water with lime. He was on duty, even here. Especially here.

"May I join you?" he asked.

"Sure," Erin said. "Can I buy you a drink? They make an excellent martini."

"I don't drink," Owen said, sitting down. "The judge thinks we should offer your client a new plea. She thinks the community would be better served without a trial."

"How do you feel about it?" Erin asked. "Personally."

"Personally?" Owen smiled, but it didn't reach his eyes. "My job is to represent the People. I try to be objective."

"I ask because I know you're a churchgoer," Erin said, probing for a soft spot. "And now you're prosecuting a man of God."

"The priest broke the law," Owen said. His voice hardened. "A young girl is dead. If he's a 'man of God,' then personally, I think he's even more subject to the laws of moral behavior. If it were up to me, he'd get no deal at all."

"What about compassion?" Erin asked. "Forgiveness? Isn't that part of your creed?"

Owen leaned in. "If you have compassion for your client, Counselor, you'll get him to accept the plea. Reckless endangerment. Twelve months. Probable probation. But if he refuses... I go all the way. Maximum sentence. Ten years."

"He won't take it," Erin said. "He says he wasn't negligent. He says he wants the truth."

"Truth?" Owen scoffed. "Have you seen the autopsy photos?"

Erin faltered. She had. They were nightmares captured on glossy paper.

"We'll go to trial," Erin said, recovering.

"Good," Owen said. "So do I."

***

Erin's apartment was high above the city, a sanctuary of silence and expensive art.

It was late. She sat on her sofa, a glass of wine in her hand, staring at the TV. The news was playing, a muted flicker of tragedies.

She fell asleep.

*Cut to Black.*

*Fade in.*

Erin's bedroom. 2:59 AM.

The digital clock on the nightstand glowing red in the darkness.

*Tick. Tick. Tick.*

Erin slept, her breathing shallow.

The numbers flipped. **3:00 AM**.

Erin's eyes snapped open.

She sat up. The room was silent. But there was a smell.

Burning.

It was faint, like smoke from a distant fire, but distinct. Acrid. Chemical.

She got out of bed. "Hello?"

She checked the living room. The kitchen. The smoke alarm was silent. The stove was cold.

She poured a glass of water at the sink.

*Zap.*

The lights died. The apartment plunged into darkness.

Erin froze. The glass slipped from her fingers. *Smash.*

In the silence, she heard it.

*Creak.*

The sound of a door opening.

She turned.

A sliver of light from the hallway cut across the living room floor. Her front door—which she had locked—was standing wide open.

Panic, cold and sharp, spiked in her chest.

She ran to the door. She slammed it shut. She threw the deadbolt. She looked through the peephole.

The hallway was empty.

She backed away, her breath coming in gasps. She retreated to her bedroom, locking that door too. She curled up on the bed, pulling the duvet over her head like a child hiding from monsters.

On the nightstand, the clock blinked. **3:00 AM**.

***

The County Jail. Night.

The hallway was a tunnel of shadows. Prisoners slept, their snores echoing like growls.

In his cell, Father Moore woke up.

He sat up on his cot. He wasn't alone.

He looked at the door.

Standing in the hallway, on the other side of the bars, was a figure.

Tall. Black robes. A wide-brimmed hat.

It stood motionless, facing him.

Moore couldn't see the face. He could only see the darkness where a face should be.

"Most glorious Prince of the Heavenly Army," Moore whispered, his voice trembling. "Holy Michael the Archangel, defend us in battle..."

The figure watched him. It didn't move. It simply radiated a cold, malignant hate.

Then, slowly, it stepped back into the shadows and vanished.

***

The Courtroom. Morning.

The gallery was packed. The air buzzed with anticipation.

On an easel, Owen Thomas had placed a photograph. It was blown up to poster size.

It was the autopsy photo.

Emily Rose. Her face was a ruin. Gaunt, bruised, teeth broken, eyes sunken into black hollows. She looked like a skull wrapped in parchment.

The jurors stared at it. Some looked away. One woman covered her mouth.

Erin rushed in, late. She looked disheveled, tired. The events of the night before hung on her like a heavy coat.

She saw the photo. She stopped. For a second, she looked terrified.

"Counselor Bruner," Judge Brewster said. "My day begins at 9:30."

"I'm sorry, Your Honor," Erin stammered. "I..."

"Are you ready?"

"Yes."

"The Prosecution calls Dr. Edith Vogel," Owen said.

Dr. Vogel was a bird-like woman, sharp and precise.

"I've known Emily her whole life," she testified. "She was a happy girl. Until she went to the University."

"Did she communicate with you after she left?" Owen asked.

"Her mother called me. At 4:00 AM. She put Emily on the phone. She was hysterical."

"Why?"

"She said she woke up in her dorm room," Vogel said. "At 3:00 AM."

*Flashback.*

Emily's dorm room. Night.

Emily woke up. 3:00 AM.

The smell. Burning.

She got up. She walked down the hallway. It was empty. Dark.

*Bang.*

A door slammed somewhere.

She ran back to her room. She locked the door. She got into bed.

She pulled the covers up.

On her desk, a cup of pens rattled. *Ch-ch-ch.*

Emily froze.

The cup slid across the desk. It tipped over. Pens scattered.

Then, the mattress depressed.

Something heavy sat on the bed.

Emily stopped breathing.

An invisible weight pressed down on her. It pushed her into the mattress. It was heavy, hot, suffocating.

She tried to scream. She couldn't.

She felt hands. Heavy, unseen hands. They moved over her body. They weren't just holding her down; they were exploring her. Violating her. They moved under her nightshirt, cold fingers tracing her skin.

She thrashed, her eyes wide, staring at the empty air that was crushing her.

Then, it lifted.

Emily gasped, sucking in air, and scrambled into the corner, weeping.

*Present Day.*

"I thought it was drugs," Dr. Marek testified. "LSD. But the tests were negative. So I diagnosed Epilepsy. Psychomotor Epilepsy."

"And the treatment?" Owen asked.

"Tegretol. It would have controlled the seizures. It would have stopped the hallucinations."

"And why did she stop taking it?"

"She told me Father Moore suggested she stop," Marek said. "He told her it was spiritual, not medical."

"And the result?"

"It killed her," Marek said flatly. "Father Moore's suggestion killed her."

Erin stood up. "Objection! Hearsay!"

"Sustained," the Judge said. "But the jury will disregard the statement about what Father Moore said."

Erin looked at the jury. They weren't disregarding it. They were looking at Moore like he was a murderer.

Erin cross-examined.

"Doctor," she said. "When Emily saw objects move on their own... is that a symptom of epilepsy?"

"No."

"Is it a symptom of psychosis?"

"Yes."

"So she was epileptic AND psychotic? Isn't that convenient?"

"Objection!" Owen shouted.

"Withdrawn," Erin said. She sat down. She had scored a point, but she was losing the war.

***

Jail. Night.

Erin sat in Moore's cell.

"They're killing us," she said. "Briggs, Marek... the medical testimony is solid."

"There are forces surrounding this trial," Moore said. "Dark forces. Be careful, Erin. The grass you tread on is full of serpents."

"I'm not afraid of demons, Father. I'm afraid of the jury."

"You're under attack," Moore said. "I can see it. 3:00 AM? The smell?"

Erin froze. She hadn't told him.

"How did you know?"

"They want to stop you. Don't let them."

He looked at her with eyes that saw too much.

"I need to know what happened next," Erin said, changing the subject. "After the dorm."

"She went to the hospital," Moore said. "That's when the visions changed."

*Flashback.*

The hospital room. Twilight.

Emily sat on the bed. She was singing quietly. "Tender shepherd, hear me..."

She looked out the window. The sun was setting.

The clouds moved. They swirled, coalescing into a shape.

A face.

A giant, ghastly face in the sky. Leering. Screaming.

Jason, her boyfriend, walked in with flowers. "Emily?"

Emily turned.

Her face was a skull. Her eyes were black pits.

She screamed.

*Present Day.*

"She saw them everywhere," Jason testified. He was on the stand now. "In the rain. In the windows. She said everyone was wearing a mask, and sometimes the masks slipped."

"And then?" Erin asked.

"Then came the spiders," Jason whispered.
