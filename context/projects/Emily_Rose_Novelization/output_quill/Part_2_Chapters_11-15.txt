# Part II: The Descent

## Chapter 11: The Ivory Tower

The University was a world away from the farm. It was a landscape of Gothic stone and ivy, of intellectual ambition and youthful energy. To Emily, it felt like a dream.

She walked through the quad, clutching her books to her chest. The air was crisp, smelling of old paper and autumn leaves. Students rushed past her, talking about philosophy, about politics, about parties. They seemed so confident. So loud.

Emily felt small. She felt like a trespasser in a temple.

She went to class. She sat in the front row. She took notes until her hand cramped. She absorbed every word, hungry for knowledge, hungry to prove that a girl from the sticks could belong here.

She met Jason in the library.

He was sitting at a table, surrounded by books on architecture. He looked up when she dropped her pencil.

"Nice catch," he said, smiling.

He had a kind face. Gentle eyes. He didn't look at her like she was a project or a charity case. He looked at her like she was a girl.

They started talking. About books. About the farm. About the city.

"You like to dance?" he asked.

"I don't know," Emily said. "I've never really tried."

"We should try," Jason said.

They went to a dance on campus. The music was loud, thumping in her chest. Emily felt awkward at first, stiff. But then Jason took her hand. He spun her around.

She laughed. It was a pure, unadulterated sound. For the first time in weeks, she didn't feel small. She felt expansive.

They walked back to her dorm under the streetlights. The leaves swirled around their feet.

"I'm glad you're here, Emily," Jason said.

"Me too," she whispered.

She went to bed that night feeling a warmth that defied the autumn chill. She fell asleep thinking of Jason's smile.

She didn't know it was the last peaceful night she would ever have.

## Chapter 12: 3:00 AM

The dream was not a dream.

Emily woke up.

She lay in her narrow dorm bed. The room was dark, illuminated only by the faint orange glow of the streetlamp outside the window. Her roommate was gone for the weekend. The other bed was empty, a flat expanse of mattress.

Silence.

It wasn't the quiet of the farm. It was a heavy, pressurized silence. The air felt thick, gelatinous.

Emily looked at the clock on her desk.

**3:00 AM**.

She frowned. Why was she awake?

Then she smelled it.

Burning.

It was faint at first, a wisp of scent. Like a match struck in another room. But it grew stronger. Acrid. Oily. The smell of burning hair. The smell of singed meat.

*Fire.*

Panic spiked in her chest. She sat up, throwing off the covers. She sniffed the air. It was strong now, choking.

She got out of bed. The floor was cold under her bare feet. She walked to the door. She opened it.

The hallway was empty.

It stretched out in both directions, a long tunnel of linoleum and closed doors. The fluorescent emergency lights buzzed.

No smoke. No fire alarm.

She walked down the hall, sniffing. The smell seemed to be everywhere and nowhere. It clung to her skin.

She reached the exit door at the end of the hall. She pushed it open.

The stairwell was cold. Empty.

*BANG.*

A sound from the other end of the hall. A door slamming.

Emily spun around. "Hello?"

Silence.

She walked back to her room. Her heart was hammering against her ribs. *Just a draft,* she told herself. *Just someone coming home late.*

She went back into her room. She closed the door. She locked it.

She got back into bed. She pulled the covers up to her chin. She stared at the ceiling, willing her heart to slow down.

*Click.*

A sound from her desk.

She froze. She turned her head slowly.

On her desk, a ceramic cup filled with pens sat near the edge.

It rattled. *Ch-ch-ch.*

Emily stopped breathing.

The cup slid.

It moved across the wood, driven by an invisible finger. It reached the edge. It tipped.

*Clatter.*

Pens scattered across the floor.

Emily sat up, a scream building in her throat.

Then, the weight fell.

It hit the mattress with a *thump*, pressing down beside her. The bed springs groaned under a heavy, sudden mass.

Something invisible was on the bed with her.

It lunged.

Emily was shoved backward. Her head hit the pillow.

A crushing weight pressed down on her chest. It was heavy, dense, suffocating. It pinned her arms to her sides.

She opened her mouth to scream, but a hand—a heavy, unseen hand—clamped over her mouth.

It smelled of rot. Of sulfur.

She thrashed, bucking her hips, kicking her legs. The weight didn't move. It was solid as stone.

She felt it moving. Writhing against her. It wasn't just holding her down; it was exploring her. Violating her.

Cold, phantom fingers traced her ribs. They slid under her nightshirt. They moved over her stomach, her breasts.

It was intimate. It was hateful.

She stared up at the empty air that was crushing the life out of her. She couldn't breathe. Black spots danced in her vision.

"Mine," a voice whispered.

It didn't come from the room. It came from inside her head. A thought that wasn't hers. A parasitic idea.

*Mine.*

The pressure increased. Her ribs groaned. She was going to die. She was going to be crushed by a ghost in a dorm room.

Then, as suddenly as it came, it lifted.

The weight vanished. The hand released her mouth.

Emily gasped, sucking in air with a desperate, ragged sound. She scrambled backward, falling off the bed, crawling into the corner of the room.

She huddled there, shaking, weeping, clutching her knees.

The room was empty. The pens lay scattered on the floor. The smell of burning hair filled the air.

And the clock read **3:01 AM**.

## Chapter 13: Science vs. Spirit

"Drugs," Dr. Marek said. He didn't look at Emily. He looked at her chart.

"I didn't take any drugs," Emily whispered. She was sitting on the exam table in the University Hospital. She was wearing a paper gown. She felt exposed.

"LSD. Mushrooms. Peyote. Students experiment," Marek said, dismissing her. "The tox screen will tell us."

The tox screen came back clean.

Marek frowned. He tapped his pen against the clipboard. "Epilepsy."

"Epilepsy?" Emily asked.

"Psychomotor epilepsy. It affects the temporal lobe. It causes seizures. Hallucinations. Muscle rigidity."

"But... the weight? The pens moving?"

"A seizure," Marek explained, his voice patient and condescending. "Your muscles contracted. Your chest tightened. Your brain interpreted this physical sensation as an external force. A demon sitting on your chest. It's a classic hallucination. The 'Old Hag' syndrome."

"It felt real."

"The brain makes it real," Marek said. "We're going to put you on Tegretol. An anti-convulsant. It will stop the seizures."

They put her in a machine. An MRI.

It was a coffin. A white, plastic coffin.

They slid her in. The machine banged and clanged, a rhythmic, industrial noise. *Thun-THUN. Thun-THUN.*

Emily closed her eyes.

*Thun-THUN.*

*One... two...*

*Thun-THUN.*

*Three... four...*

*Thun-THUN.*

*Five... six...*

She opened her eyes.

The ceiling of the MRI tube was inches from her face.

It changed.

The white plastic melted. It became skin. Grey, dead skin.

A face formed in the plastic. A screaming face. It pressed down toward her.

"Let me out!" Emily screamed. "Let me out!"

The technicians pulled her out. She was shaking.

"Just a panic attack," Marek said. "Take the pills."

Emily took the pills. She wanted to believe him. She wanted it to be a machine that was broken, not her soul.

## Chapter 14: The Face in the Clouds

She stayed in the hospital for observation.

Jason visited her. He brought flowers. Cheap daisies from the campus store.

"You look better," he lied.

Emily smiled weakly. She was sitting on the bed, looking out the window. It was twilight. The sky was a bruise of purple and red.

"I'm scared, Jason," she said.

"It's just epilepsy," Jason said. "The pills will work."

Emily looked out the window.

On the horizon, storm clouds were gathering. They were dark, massive, roiling against the wind.

"Look," she whispered.

The clouds were moving. Knitting together.

A nose. A mouth. Eyes.

A face formed in the sky. Massive. Ancient. It spanned the horizon.

It leered at her. It opened its mouth, a cavern of darkness that swallowed the light.

"Do you see it?" Emily asked, her voice trembling.

"See what?" Jason asked, looking at the clouds. "The storm?"

"The face!"

She stood up, backing away from the window.

Jason stepped toward her. "Emily, calm down."

She turned to him.

For a second—just a split second—his face wasn't his face.

It melted. The skin slid off the bone like wet wax. His eyes turned into black pits. His teeth became jagged shards of obsidian.

A demon looked out from behind Jason's face.

Emily screamed. She scrambled back, knocking over the food tray. *Clatter.*

"Emily!" Jason shouted, reaching for her.

She squeezed her eyes shut. "Get away! Get away!"

"It's me! It's Jason!"

She opened her eyes.

He was just a boy. Scared. Holding flowers.

The demon was gone.

But she knew. She knew it was still there. Waiting. Hiding behind the mask of skin.

"They found me," she whispered. "They found me."

## Chapter 15: The Pre-Trial Motions

The courtroom was empty save for the lawyers and the judge. This was the war before the war.

Erin stood at the bench. "Your Honor, the Prosecution is attempting to introduce the victim's diaries as evidence of mental illness."

"They are evidence of her state of mind," Owen Thomas argued. "She writes about demons. About faces melting. It's textbook psychosis."

"It's evidence of her *belief*," Erin countered. "Which is central to the question of Father Moore's negligence. If she believed she was possessed, and he believed it, then his actions were consistent with that belief."

Judge Brewster peered over her glasses. "I'll allow the diaries. But I'll instruct the jury that they are not proof of supernatural events, only of the decedent's state of mind."

Erin nodded. It was a partial win.

Later, they picked the jury. Voir Dire.

Erin watched them. A teacher. A mechanic. A grandmother.

"Do you believe in the Devil?" she asked a prospective juror, a middle-aged man in a plaid shirt.

"I go to church," he said.

"That's not what I asked. Do you believe the Devil is real? That he can walk the earth?"

The man hesitated. "I guess so."

"And if you were told a girl was possessed... would you believe it?"

"I'd need proof," the man said.

"Excused," Owen said immediately.

Owen wanted skeptics. He wanted people who believed in facts, in science, in things they could touch.

Erin wanted believers. She wanted people who knew that the world was bigger and darker than what they could see.

She ended up with a mix. Twelve people. Twelve subjective realities.

She looked at Father Moore. He was sitting at the defense table, reading his Bible. He looked calm.

"They're going to try to paint you as a fanatic," Erin told him.

"I am a fanatic," Moore said, not looking up. "Fanatic comes from the Latin *fanaticus*. Inspired by a deity."

"That won't play well in court."

"I'm not playing, Erin."

Erin sighed. She rubbed her temples. She needed a drink.

She didn't know it yet, but the war had already followed her home.
