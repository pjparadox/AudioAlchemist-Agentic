***

Chapter 9: The Broken Witness

"Tibetan monks," Ethan Thomas said, leaning against the jury railing. "They can produce two distinct tones simultaneously. Did you know that, Father?"

Moore sat on the stand, exhausted. "No."

"So, the 'demonic voices' on your tape... could simply be a psychotic girl straining her vocal cords?"

"No," Moore said. "I know what I heard."

Erin watched the jury. They were wavering. The tape had terrified them, but Thomas was giving them a lifeboat: Logic. Science. Monks.

She needed Cartwright.

She found him in the parking lot behind the courthouse. It was dusk. The streetlights buzzed, flickering in the cold wind.

"Dr. Cartwright!" Erin called.

He spun around. He looked worse than he had in the park. His eyes were wide, the pupils dilated. He smelled of scotch and fear.

"I can't do it," he stammered. "I can't go back in there."

"You have to," Erin said, grabbing his arm. "You're the only one who can corroborate the tape."

Cartwright pulled away. He looked past her, at the tree line. "They're watching, Erin. Don't you see them?"

"Who?"

"The dark ones."

He stepped back. "Tell Moore I'm sorry. Tell him... I know."

He stepped into the road.

The truck came out of nowhere. No headlights. Just a roar of an engine and the squeal of tires.

*CRUNCH.*

It hit him with the force of a battering ram. Cartwright flew over the hood. He hit the windshield, glass spider-webbing around his impact, then rolled off onto the asphalt.

The truck skidded, then sped away.

Erin ran to him.

Cartwright lay twisted. His neck was broken, his head turned 180 degrees, staring back at his own spine. His eyes were open, fixed on the sky. Fixed on something Erin couldn't see.

The blood didn't pool; it spread, dark and thick, steaming in the cold air. The silence that followed was absolute.

***

Chapter 10: The Letter

"We've lost," Erin said. She sat in Moore's cell. She was shaking. "Cartwright is dead."

Moore nodded slowly. He didn't look surprised. "Then it falls to you, Erin."

"To me? I'm a lawyer, not a priest."

"Read this."

He handed her a yellow envelope.

That night, alone in her apartment—with all the lights on—Erin read Emily's letter.

*Flashback. Predawn. The Wheat Field.*

Emily walked out of the farmhouse. The fog was thick, a white ocean that swallowed the world.

She was dying. Her body was a wreck of bruises and broken bones. But as she walked, the pain faded.

She saw a light. Soft. Golden.

A woman stood in the mist. She wore simple robes. Her face was kind, but sad.

"Emily," the woman said. "Heaven is not blind to your pain."

"Why?" Emily wept. "Why won't they leave?"

"I can cut the cord," the Virgin Mary said. "You can come with me now. You can have peace."

Emily looked down. She saw her own body lying on the wet grass, writhing in agony. She saw the demons waiting.

"Or," the Virgin said, "you can stay. You can suffer. And through your suffering, the world will know that the spiritual realm is real."

Emily looked at the light. Then she looked at the dark.

"People say God is dead," Emily whispered. "But how can they think that if I show them the Devil?"

She closed her eyes. "I choose to stay."

The cord snapped back. Emily woke up in her body. The pain returned, a thousand times worse. She screamed.

And then, blood began to weep from her hands. From her feet. The Stigmata. The mark of the saint.

***

Chapter 11: Fear and Trembling

Erin stood at the podium. She didn't have notes. She didn't have science. She had doubt.

"I am a woman of doubt," she told the jury. "Ethan Thomas is a man of faith. But here, in this room, we deal with possibilities."

She walked the line.

"Is it possible she was epileptic? Yes. Is it possible she was possessed? I don't know. But Emily believed it. Father Moore believed it. And they acted on that belief."

She looked at Moore.

"Don't send a good man to prison for loving a girl enough to fight the Devil for her soul."

The jury was out for four days.

When they returned, the Foreman wouldn't look at the prosecutor.

"We find the defendant... Guilty of Negligent Homicide."

Erin's heart stopped.

"However," the Foreman continued, "we recommend a sentence of... time served."

Judge Brewster banged the gavel. "Accepted. Father Moore, you are free to go."

It was a compromise. A legal paradox. Guilty, but innocent.

***

Epilogue. The Cemetery.

Spring had come. The snow was gone, replaced by green shoots pushing through the earth.

Erin and Moore stood by the grave.

"Do you believe her?" Erin asked. "About the vision?"

"I believe that faith and doubt can coexist," Moore said. "Faith without doubt can be a dangerous thing."

Erin touched the locket around her neck. "I got offered a job. Teaching."

"Will you take it?"

"Maybe. I think I'm done with corporate law."

She looked at the headstone.

**EMILY ROSE**
*1986 - 2005*

And below the date, the epitaph:

*"Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling."*

Erin walked away. The wind blew, warm and alive. She didn't look back at the shadows. She knew they were there. But she also knew, for the first time, that she wasn't walking alone.
