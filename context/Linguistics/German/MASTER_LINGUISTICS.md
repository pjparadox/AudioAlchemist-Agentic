# Master Linguistics Profile: German (Deutsch)

## 1. Contextuality & Register (Low-Mid Context)
*   **T-V Distinction (Du vs. Sie):**
    *   *Sie:* Formal. Strangers, Bosses, Elders.
    *   *Du:* Informal. Friends, Family, Children, Gods, and Pets.
    *   *The Shift:* The moment two characters switch from *Sie* to *Du* (das "Du" anbieten) is a major plot beat. It must be explicit.

## 2. Translation Pitfalls
*   **Word Order (V2 Rule):** In main clauses, the verb is 2nd. In sub-clauses, it's last. Do not mimic English syntax.
*   **Compound Nouns:** "Donaudampfschiffahrts..." - These can break subtitle CPS limits. Break them up or rephrase.
*   **Gender:** Nouns have gender (Der/Die/Das). Adjectives must agree.

## 3. Amazon/Market Specifics
*   **Genre:** High demand for Crime (Krimi) and Sci-Fi.
*   **Style:** German readers tolerate longer, more complex sentences than Americans, but for subtitles, you must simplify aggressively.

## 4. Unknown Unknowns
*   **Modal Particles (Doch, Mal, Ja, Halt):** These flavor the sentence (surprise, resignation, obviousness). Omitting them makes dialogue feel "wooden".
    *   *Example:* "Das ist ja toll!" (That is [surprisingly] great!).
