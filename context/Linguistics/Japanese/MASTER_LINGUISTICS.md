# Master Linguistics Profile: Japanese (Nihongo)

## 1. Contextuality & Register (Extremely High Context)
*   **Keigo (Honorifics):**
    *   *Sonkeigo:* Respectful language (raising the listener).
    *   *Kenjougo:* Humble language (lowering the speaker).
    *   *Teineigo:* Polite standard (Desu/Masu).
*   **Suffixes:** -san, -kun, -chan, -sama, -dono, -senpai. These are relationship markers. **Do not delete them** in Tier 2 translations unless localization explicitly demands "Mr./Ms." (which often fails).

## 2. Translation Pitfalls
*   **Subject Omission:** Subjects are rarely spoken. You must infer who is acting based on the verb form (Honorific vs Humble).
*   **Gendered Speech:** *Ore/Boku/Watashi* (Male variants) vs *Atashi/Watashi* (Female). This defines character voice.
*   **Ambiguity:** Japanese thrives on leaving things unsaid. "Chotto..." (A little...) often means "No, I definitely cannot do that."

## 3. Amazon/Market Specifics
*   **Vertical Text:** Traditional novels are Tategaki (Vertical). Kindle support is robust but formatting matters.
*   **Light Novels:** Massive market. Requires fast, dialogue-heavy translation style.

## 4. Native Script Mandate (Critical)
*   **Kana/Kanji ONLY:** Output must be in native script (Hiragana/Katakana/Kanji).
*   **NO ROMAJI:** Never output "Arigato" unless the source is specifically teaching Romaji. Use "ありがとう".
*   **Exception:** Loan words that are stylistically written in English (e.g., brand names) if consistent with source.

## 5. Unknown Unknowns
*   **Aizuchi:** The listener constantly nods/says "Hai/Ee/Un". In English subtitles, do not subtitle every "Un" or it looks messy.
*   **Role Language (Yakuwarigo):** Old men speak like old men (Jya), rich girls like rich girls (Desu wa). Preserve this flavor.
